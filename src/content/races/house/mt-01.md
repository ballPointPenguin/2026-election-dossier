Montana's 1st District covers western Montana, including Missoula, Bozeman, Butte, Kalispell and the Flathead Valley: university towns, conservative rural communities and fast-growing mountain towns in one district. Republican Ryan Zinke is retiring. Trump carried the district by roughly 12 points in 2024, while Zinke won by a much narrower 7.7, and Cook moved the race from Lean Republican to Toss Up on October 16.

## Candidates

### Aaron Flint (R)

A fifth-generation Montanan, military veteran and longtime conservative radio host with nearly two decades in Montana political media, Flint is running for office for the first time. He won the June 2 primary with about 50.1%, beating Secretary of State Christi Jacobsen and former state Sen. Al Olszewski. His platform: tax relief, deregulation, border security, more timber and mining, vocational education and public access to federal lands. Trump endorsed him and campaigned for him in Kalispell on October 14.

### Sam Forstag (D)

Forstag spent eight years as a wildland firefighter, including as a Forest Service smokejumper, and is a labor organizer. He won the Democratic primary with about 37.3% in a field that included former gubernatorial candidate Ryan Busse. He stresses his working-class background, union experience, federal workforce issues, rural healthcare, housing costs and corporate accountability. A core motivation, by his account, is federal cuts to Forest Service staffing, which he says hurt firefighting capacity and Montana's public lands.

He has also worked as a registered lobbyist, including for local governments and the ACLU. Republicans have made that a campaign issue, particularly his work on a proposed local sales-tax measure and on transgender sports legislation. Montana Free Press has noted that the local-option sales-tax proposal is narrower than some ads imply.

### Nick Sheedy (L)

The Libertarian nominee, polling a few points. The race is decided by plurality, so the winner needs no majority.

## The district

MT-1 was created when Montana regained a second seat after the 2020 census.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Missoula | University of Montana, public sector, healthcare, progressive politics |
| Bozeman / Gallatin Valley | Fast growth, tech, tourism, Montana State, housing pressure |
| Butte / Silver Bow | Mining and labor history, industrial heritage |
| Flathead Valley / Kalispell | Tourism, timber, recreation, conservative voting |
| Rural western counties | Agriculture, forestry, resource extraction, public-land management |

Missoula leans Democratic, Bozeman is changing fast, and the Flathead supplies solid Republican votes, while Butte's labor tradition doesn't fit neat urban-versus-rural categories. Housing is a big issue in Bozeman and resort-adjacent towns, and public-land access, wildfire and timber have more direct economic weight here than in most districts.

## The October 13 debate

Held in Missoula, hosted by Montana Free Press and NonStop Local.

| Issue | Flint (R) | Forstag (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordability | Tax relief, suspending federal gas taxes, deregulation | Healthcare investment, corporate accountability, household costs |
| Public lands | Access, more timber harvest, Zinke's public-lands legislation | Public ownership and access, wildfire workforce and management |
| AI and data centers | Skeptical of projects that burden communities; stresses local economic effects | Federal conditions on power, environmental impact, community consent and union labor |
| Rural healthcare | Funding to rural providers, less administrative burden | More federal investment, protecting access |
| Iran war | Wants a quick end | Wants a quick end |
| Congressional stock trading | Supports restrictions | Supports restrictions |

They overlap on public-land access, timber jobs, stock-trading limits and limiting corporate purchases of housing. Most of the disagreement is over the role of federal government, regulation and private enterprise, not whether a problem exists.

## Why it matters nationally

**Dueling working-class identities.** Flint runs on military service, Montana roots and years on talk radio; Forstag on physical labor as a smokejumper, union organizing and firsthand experience with federal land management. Montana doesn't sort neatly into urban professionals versus rural workers. The Washington Post looked at Forstag as part of a wider Democratic trend of emphasizing physically demanding jobs.

**Public lands.** Timber, wildfire, wilderness, hunting access, tourism and federal agency staffing all intersect. Both pledge to preserve access but differ on regulation, the federal workforce and balancing economic activity with the environment.

**Data centers.** A proposed data center in Montana raises questions about electricity, water, public-land access, tax incentives and local benefit. Forstag would require developers to supply their own power and win community support; Flint criticizes excessive regulation while questioning his opponent's consistency.

**House control.** MT-1 is one of the Republican-held seats whose ratings moved in October, which shapes where the parties spend.

## What to watch

Whether new polling backs up September's closeness, turnout across Missoula, Gallatin and Flathead counties, whether Libertarian support holds, and where late outside money lands.
