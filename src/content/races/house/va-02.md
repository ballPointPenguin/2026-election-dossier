Virginia's 2nd District, centered on Virginia Beach and Hampton Roads, is one of the country's most closely watched House battlegrounds. It has a large military and veteran population, has voted for both parties recently, and is hosting a rematch between two former Navy officers who have each represented it.

The principal candidates are Republican incumbent Jen Kiggans and Democratic former Rep. Elaine Luria. Independents Matt Baker and Bishop Staten are also on the ballot. Both backgrounds make defense policy, veterans' services and the use of military force unusually prominent.

## The district

VA-2 covers Virginia Beach, parts of Chesapeake and Suffolk, and Virginia's Eastern Shore.

| Area | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| Virginia Beach | Military families, tourism, suburbs, service jobs |
| Chesapeake and Suffolk | Suburban growth, logistics, manufacturing, commuting |
| Accomack and Northampton counties | Eastern Shore agriculture, fisheries, tourism, rural communities |
| Hampton Roads | Naval operations, defense contractors, shipbuilding |

Naval Station Norfolk, the world's largest naval installation, and the wider Hampton Roads military network shape the district: even where bases lie outside its boundaries, personnel, civilian employees and contractors live throughout. Defense appropriations, military housing, healthcare, veterans' benefits and shipbuilding are immediate local concerns.

| Election | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 2018 House | Luria defeated Republican incumbent Scott Taylor |
| 2020 House | Luria reelected |
| 2022 House | Kiggans defeated Luria |
| 2024 House | Kiggans over Missy Cotter Smasal, about 50.7% to 46.9% |
| 2024 president | Trump by about 4.5 points |

It voted Biden in 2020 and Trump in 2024, Republican Glenn Youngkin in 2021 and Democrat Abigail Spanberger in 2025. The 2022 race followed redistricting, so comparisons with 2018 and 2020 need to account for boundary changes. The 2026 election uses existing lines: Virginia's attempted mid-decade redistricting was blocked by the state Supreme Court (see also [VA-1](../va-01/)).

## Candidates

### Jen Kiggans (R)

She emphasizes military readiness, veterans' services, border enforcement, tax policy and healthcare, with her nursing background relevant to Medicaid, Medicare and costs. She supports significant parts of Trump's agenda, including the 2025 tax and spending law, and cites her defense and veterans' work as attention to the district's needs.

### Elaine Luria (D)

She served on ships operating nuclear propulsion systems and ran a small business before politics, was elected in 2018, reelected in 2020 and beaten by Kiggans in 2022. In Congress she sat on Armed Services and the select committee investigating the January 6 attack, a specific basis for her criticism of Trump, while Kiggans focuses more on the administration's policy agenda. Her 2026 campaign stresses healthcare affordability, cost of living, national security, oversight and accountability.

### Independents

Baker and Staten lack the fundraising or polling presence of the major candidates, but recent VA-2 elections were decided by roughly three to four points.

## The issues

**Defense and the Iran war.** Both have extensive Navy experience. Kiggans supports the administration's military operation, citing national security and Iran's nuclear program. Luria criticizes the conduct and continuation of the conflict, stressing congressional oversight, strategic objectives and the consequences for service members. In Hampton Roads, deployments affect families directly.

**Healthcare and the 2025 law.** Kiggans voted for it, stressing tax benefits and measures to strengthen Medicaid eligibility enforcement. Luria says its healthcare provisions threaten coverage and access. Large military and retiree populations make TRICARE, Medicare and veterans' healthcare important, though those are distinct programs with different eligibility and funding.

**Immigration.** Kiggans backs stronger enforcement and the administration's priorities. Luria supports border security but criticizes aspects of enforcement and stresses oversight and legal protections. In the October Christopher Newport survey, immigration was the top priority among Republican respondents (24%) versus 11% of all likely voters.

**Coastal flooding and offshore wind.** Hampton Roads has recurrent tidal flooding, land subsidence and storm risk affecting roads, neighborhoods, utilities and military infrastructure. Both candidates have supported the Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind project off Virginia Beach, meant to power about 660,000 homes when complete, per its developer, despite broader differences on climate and energy, amid debate over environmental effects, costs, permitting and the regional energy economy. Climate adaptation is already a practical infrastructure and military-readiness concern here.

**Cost of living.** Kiggans points to tax cuts, growth and Republican fiscal policy; Luria to healthcare costs, inflation, housing and who benefits from tax cuts. CNU found cost of living the top issue overall at 24%, including 32% of Democrats and 25% of independents.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Two former Navy officers.** Voters have already seen both represent the district.
- **Frequent party changes.** VA-2 has changed party control four times since 2008, from boundary shifts and changing coalitions.
- **National versus local.** Kiggans stresses her record and the district's defense needs; Luria stresses federal policy and oversight alongside her past service.
- **A military district during an active war.** The Iran conflict puts foreign policy and war powers close to home.
- **Coastal climate politics.** Offshore wind and flooding allow local agreement as well as disputes.
- **Virginia's battlegrounds.** Alongside VA-1 (Rob Wittman vs. Shannon Taylor) and VA-5 (John McGuire vs. former Rep. Tom Perriello), per Roll Call.
