New Mexico's 2nd District covers a huge swath of southern New Mexico, much of the state's border with Mexico, and parts of Albuquerque. It voted for Trump in 2024 while reelecting a Democratic congressman. Two-term Democrat Gabe Vasquez faces Republican Greg Cunningham, a retired Marine and former Albuquerque police detective endorsed by Trump.

Cook rates the district D+0, essentially even with the nation in recent presidential elections. Democratic-leaning areas around Albuquerque and Las Cruces sit alongside Republican-leaning oil and farm communities.

## The district

NM-2 holds Las Cruces, much of southern New Mexico, Permian Basin communities and parts of Albuquerque's Westside and South Valley.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Las Cruces / Doña Ana County | New Mexico State University, agriculture, healthcare, border commerce, government jobs |
| Albuquerque Westside and South Valley | Suburbs, Hispanic communities, small business |
| Carlsbad, Hobbs, southeast | Oil and gas, potash, energy services, ranching |
| Southwestern counties | Border crossings, agriculture, tourism, public lands |
| Rural central and western areas | Ranching, federal land, water scarcity, long distances to healthcare |

Border enforcement and trade, oil and gas, water, rural healthcare and federal land management matter unusually here, and communities are hundreds of miles apart with very different economies, unlike compact suburban battlegrounds such as [AZ-1](../az-01/) or [PA-7](../pa-07/).

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Vasquez D +0.7 (over Yvette Herrell) |
| 2024 House | Vasquez D +4.2 (52.1% to 47.9%) |
| 2024 president | Trump R +1.8 (49.9% to 48.1%) |

The roughly six-point gap between the presidential and Democratic House margins shows how much ticket-splitting there is. Vasquez beat Herrell, then the incumbent, in 2022 and again in 2024; Cunningham is a new opponent, so those results are informative but not directly comparable.

## Candidates

### Gabe Vasquez (D)

He stresses a district-focused identity: public lands, border communities, economic development and affordability. His priorities include lowering household costs, protecting Medicaid and food assistance, and opposing parts of the Trump administration's economic and immigration policies. In an October Source New Mexico interview he named the economy as the top issue, citing fuel and fertilizer costs, healthcare affordability and the economic effects of the Iran war. Those are his campaign's arguments, not independently established effects.

### Greg Cunningham (R)

Cunningham beat Jose Orozco in the June primary and got Trump's endorsement in April. He emphasizes public safety, immigration enforcement, border security and cost of living, and named public safety his top priority in the Source New Mexico questionnaire, arguing crime imposes costs on families and businesses. His policing and military background fits that focus, while Vasquez offers municipal government and congressional records to examine.

## The policy disputes

The Source New Mexico candidate questionnaire covers legislation, the Trump administration, data centers and the border.

| Issue | Vasquez (D) | Cunningham (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economy | Affordability, healthcare costs, effects of tariffs and spending | Economic security, tax and regulatory policy, costs of crime |
| Immigration | Border security, while criticizing some enforcement practices | Enforcement, border security, public safety |
| Healthcare and food aid | Opposes Medicaid and SNAP cuts | Fiscal policy and public safety; has discussed SNAP changes |
| Energy and public lands | Conservation, access to public lands, local community needs | Domestic energy production and economic development |
| Federal government | Criticizes major elements of Trump's agenda | Supports the administration; has Trump's endorsement |

**Border.** NM-2 is a border district, so immigration is a direct local concern touching Las Cruces, agricultural employers, cross-border commerce, federal law enforcement and immigrant families. Cunningham stresses enforcement; Vasquez backs border security while criticizing enforcement he says harms established communities. Both discussed immigration and ICE in October interviews with New Mexico PBS.

**Oil, gas and public lands.** Southeastern New Mexico is in the Permian Basin. Energy supports jobs and public revenue while raising questions about air quality, water, methane and federal leasing, and the district's public lands serve recreation, conservation, ranching and energy.

**Rural healthcare.** Long distances to hospitals and specialists make Medicaid and SNAP changes consequential. Vasquez criticizes the 2025 Republican law; Cunningham addresses those programs in the context of federal spending and eligibility.

**The Iran war.** Vasquez ties the conflict to energy and farm input prices. Diesel and fertilizer matter to farmers and ranchers, gasoline to households across a large district; how much the war explains any price rise is a separate economic question.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A Democratic-held Trump district.** Like [WA-3](../wa-03/), a case of presidential versus congressional voting.
- **Competitive inside a Democratic-leaning state.** New Mexico's other two districts have stronger Democratic histories.
- **The Hispanic electorate.** Large, diverse communities in Albuquerque, Las Cruces and the south, whose voting can't be inferred from ethnicity.
- **A new Republican nominee.** Cunningham comes from law enforcement and the military, not from Herrell's congressional background.
- **National attention.** Speaker Mike Johnson and Majority Leader Steve Scalise visited in October.
- **Compare [AZ-1](../az-01/) and [CO-8](../co-08/).** Those are Republican-held; NM-2 is Democratic-held. All have economically varied communities and close results.
