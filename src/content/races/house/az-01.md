Arizona's 1st District is an open Republican-held seat covering Scottsdale, northeastern Phoenix and several affluent, politically mixed Phoenix-area communities. It features Democrat Amish Shah, an emergency physician and former state legislator, against Republican Jay Feely, a former NFL kicker and sports broadcaster endorsed by President Trump. Libertarian Monica Alponte is also on the ballot.

The seat is open because David Schweikert ran for governor and lost the Republican primary. AZ-1 is distinctive for narrow presidential margins, a contrast between a physician-legislator and a celebrity newcomer, and a Republican registration edge alongside heavy independent and crossover voting.

## The district

AZ-1 includes Scottsdale, Paradise Valley, Fountain Hills, Cave Creek and parts of northeastern Phoenix and north Mesa.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Scottsdale | Tourism, hospitality, healthcare, technology, professional services, retirees |
| Paradise Valley | Affluent neighborhoods, resorts, high-value real estate |
| Northeastern Phoenix | Established suburbs, professionals, commercial centers |
| Fountain Hills / Cave Creek | Exurban; retirees, small business, desert recreation |
| North Mesa portions | Suburban; healthcare, service jobs |

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Schweikert R +0.8 |
| 2024 House | Schweikert R +3.8 (51.9% to 48.1% over Shah) |
| 2024 president | Trump R +3.1 (51.1% to 48.0%) |

Biden carried the district by about 1.5 points in 2020, and Cook's PVI is R+1. In 2024 voters also backed Democratic Senate candidate Ruben Gallego, evidence of ticket-splitting.

## Candidates

### Amish Shah (D)

Shah served three terms in the Arizona House and has stressed working with Republican legislators on selected bills, sometimes drawing criticism from Democrats. His priorities are healthcare access, household costs, protecting Social Security and Medicare, and opposing some of the Trump administration's tax and spending policies. He lost to Schweikert by 3.8 points in 2024, then won the July four-way primary with about 39.5% over Marlene Galán-Woods, Jonathan Treble and Rick McCartney, even though the DCCC had backed a different candidate. Supporters include former Republican Arizona House Speaker Rusty Bowers and No Labels, endorsements by specific people and groups, not by Arizona Republicans generally.

### Jay Feely (R)

Feely initially considered the more Republican AZ-5 but switched to AZ-1 with Trump's encouragement. He lives in Gilbert, outside the district, which is permitted: House members must live in the state, not the district. He won the July primary with about 49.8% over state Rep. Joseph Chaplik and John Trobough, and has Trump's endorsement and Turning Point USA's support. He stresses immigration enforcement, tax cuts, deregulation, energy production and the administration's agenda, including making some Trump executive actions permanent through legislation.

### Others

Monica Alponte is the Libertarian nominee; two write-ins, Gage Thunder and Dev Gupta, are also listed. There's not enough polling to estimate their vote.

## The issues

The candidates debated on October 7 under the Arizona Citizens Clean Elections Commission, covering affordability, immigration, healthcare, water, data centers and the Iran war.

| Issue | Shah (D) | Feely (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Universal coverage; public option; reverse Medicaid cuts | Market-oriented policies; tax and regulatory cuts |
| Taxes and affordability | Opposes broad tariffs; criticizes parts of the 2025 Republican tax law | Extend tax cuts; deregulation; more energy production |
| Immigration | Reform alongside border security | Enforcement; border barriers; deport violent offenders |
| Data centers | Opposes more in the district, citing water and resource demands | Supports AI infrastructure with siting limits and recycled water |
| Colorado River | Protect Arizona's allocation | Also protect Arizona's allocation |
| Iran war | Prevent an Iranian bomb, but criticizes the administration's handling | Defends the operation and the administration |

**Water.** The Phoenix region faces long-term supply pressure from Colorado River shortages, groundwater limits, growth and drought. Both oppose cuts they think would unfairly burden Arizona; they disagree more on data centers. Shah says their water and electricity needs impose unacceptable local costs. Feely says AI infrastructure matters economically and strategically, while backing recycled water and keeping facilities away from residential areas.

**Social Security.** Feely's past description of Social Security as resembling a Ponzi scheme is a campaign controversy. Shah criticizes it and stresses protecting benefits; Feely says the program needs reforms for long-term solvency. A past characterization isn't the same as a specific current benefit-cut proposal.

## Why it matters nationally

- **An open seat after a long incumbency.** Schweikert held it from 2011, and neither nominee is a sitting member, unlike the 2024 rematch.
- **A ticket-splitting district.** It backed Trump and Schweikert but also Gallego.
- **Local experience versus celebrity.** A legislator-physician against a national sports figure close to Trump.
- **A big independent bloc.** About 35% of registrants are outside the two major parties.
- **Money.** Over $24M in outside spending is among the highest in this dossier.
- **Compare [AZ-6](../az-06/).** Juan Ciscomani's seat is another watched Arizona race, but AZ-1 is open and Scottsdale-Phoenix suburban, not southern Arizona.
