Northern Nevada is hosting one of the more intriguing Republican-held open-seat contests of the midterms. After nearly 15 years in Congress, Republican Mark Amodei is retiring, giving Democrats an opening in a district they've never won since its creation in 1983.

It pits David Flippo, a Trump-endorsed Republican and retired Air Force lieutenant colonel, against Teresa Benitez-Thompson, a former Democratic majority leader in the Nevada Assembly. Lynn Chapman of the Independent American Party is also on the ballot.

Fundamentals favor Republicans: Trump carried the district by about 14 points in 2024 and Amodei won his last race by roughly 19 over his nearest opponent. Yet polling suggests a much closer race, with one October survey having Flippo up just two. Cook, Inside Elections and Sabato all moved NV-2 from Likely to Lean Republican in October; Inside Elections has since gone to Tilt Republican.

## The district

NV-2 covers most of northern Nevada: the Reno–Sparks area, Carson City, Lake Tahoe communities, Elko and vast stretches of the Great Basin. The relatively competitive Reno suburbs contrast with strongly Republican rural counties.

| Region | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| Washoe County | Reno and Sparks; the largest prize, with university, technology, logistics, gaming and suburban voters |
| Carson City | State government, public employees, retirees; Republican-leaning |
| Lake Tahoe / Truckee Meadows | Tourism, housing affordability, environmental management; resort communities differ from surrounding suburbs |
| Elko and northeastern Nevada | Gold mining, ranching, energy; strong Republican support |
| Rural Great Basin counties | Public lands, grazing, water rights, agriculture; large Republican margins |

Candidates must campaign across communities hundreds of miles apart while competing in the fast-changing Reno area. The arithmetic: Democrats can be competitive in Washoe County but must overcome big Republican margins elsewhere.

| Contest | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 2011 special election | Amodei (R) elected |
| 2022 House | Amodei by about 22 points |
| 2024 House | Amodei by about 19 over independent Greg Kidd |
| 2024 president | Trump by about 14.2 points |

The 2024 House result is a weaker partisan baseline because Kidd ran as a nonpartisan, not as the Democratic nominee. Cook's PVI is about R+7.

## Candidates

### David Flippo (R)

He won the nomination after a competitive June primary, and his military service is central to his identity: national security, veterans' affairs, conservative fiscal policy, border enforcement and domestic resource production. Trump's endorsement helped distinguish him from other Republicans. The establishment preferred another candidate, former state Sen. James Settelmeyer, endorsed by Amodei and Gov. Joe Lombardo, so the primary pitted Trump's pick against a more established Nevada Republican, and Flippo's win showed the strength of Trump's endorsement among primary voters.

His campaign has faced questions about his military résumé: he acknowledged that claims of a Bronze Star with valor were overstated and attributed the error to campaign staff. It became part of the general-election debate over his credibility.

### Teresa Benitez-Thompson (D)

She rose to Assembly majority leader, gaining experience with state budgeting, healthcare, education and negotiating across Nevada's divided institutions. Her campaign stresses healthcare affordability, public education, housing costs, reproductive rights and protecting federal services, positioning her as an experienced, pragmatic legislator, which matters where Democrats need independents and moderate Republicans. It contrasts with Flippo's lack of elected-office experience. The Democratic primary was crowded and included Greg Kidd, the wealthy investor who won about 36% as a nonpartisan against Amodei in 2024; she won despite his ability to self-finance.

### Lynn Chapman (Independent American Party)

Her party is associated with constitutional conservatism, limited government and opposition to expansive federal authority. She was also on the 2024 ballot, with about 5%, and could matter if the major candidates finish within a few points. Unlike [Alaska](../ak-al/), Nevada doesn't use ranked-choice voting here: a candidate can win with a plurality, so Chapman's votes won't transfer to either major nominee.

Through October 14, 2026. Flippo has raised almost three times as much, though cash is similar. Outside spending: about **$7.50 million** through October 30 ($6.25M in the general election, $1.26M in primaries), roughly $3.8M helping Flippo or opposing Benitez-Thompson and $2.4M the other way. Spenders include Invest in Tomorrow Coalition PAC, Freedom Caucus Fund, Club for Growth Action and Restoration of America PAC. Republicans have a meaningful outside-spending edge, though Democratic-aligned groups have committed millions too. Smaller groups are also spending to support Chapman while opposing Flippo, which could help Benitez-Thompson if it pulls conservative votes from Flippo, though the effect is uncertain.

## The issues

The [Nevada Independent's candidate questionnaire](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/where-do-the-candidates-in-northern-nevadas-2nd-congressional-district-stand-on-the-issues) is an unusually useful side-by-side.

**Public lands, housing and water.** Nevada has one of the highest shares of federal land in the country. Both candidates back legislation making some federal land available for housing and development. Flippo stresses returning more control to Nevada and easing land-use restrictions; Benitez-Thompson wants negotiated transfers with public input, tribal consultation and conservation protections. Water is inseparable as Reno and neighbors keep growing.

**Healthcare and Medicaid.** Benitez-Thompson supports reinstating enhanced ACA subsidies and opposes Medicaid work requirements, arguing coverage losses would burden emergency rooms and rural providers. Flippo opposes restoring the subsidies and supports work requirements to encourage employment. Neither supports raising the Social Security retirement age above 67.

**Immigration and voting.** Flippo supports the SAVE America Act and broad deportation policies with limited exceptions. Benitez-Thompson opposes the act, saying added documentation could obstruct eligible voters, and favors immigration reform combining enforcement with legal protections and due process. Nevada's separate voter-ID ballot question makes election administration especially visible this year.

**Tariffs and the Iran war.** She says tariffs have raised costs for households, businesses and Nevada's tourism economy. He defends tariffs as a negotiating tool against unfair trade practices while declining an unqualified assessment of their immediate economic effects. On Iran, he stresses the security threat and the rationale for weakening the government; she questions the objectives, the justification and the financial and human costs.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A four-decade Republican monopoly.** No Democrat has held NV-2 since 1983, so a Benitez-Thompson win would be historic.
- **Trump's primary influence.** Flippo beat a more experienced Republican backed by Lombardo and Amodei; the general election tests whether that coalition can add independents.
- **The Mountain West wave.** A Democratic win in a Trump +14 district would signal a substantial national shift, not just a swing-district change.
- **A Reno-area suburban test.** Technology, logistics, housing and services growth has changed the electorate, and Washoe County is central to any breakthrough.
- **A possible third-party effect.** Chapman could swing a close plurality race.
- **The whole delegation.** Nevada's other three districts are Democratic-held around Las Vegas, so a Democratic win would give the party the entire state House delegation.

## What to watch on election night

- **Reno and Sparks, Washoe County.** Benitez-Thompson needs strong turnout in the urban core and substantial suburban and independent support in the district's largest battleground.
- **Carson City.** A measure of whether she's cutting Republican margins outside Reno, especially among retirees and state employees.
- **Elko and northeastern Nevada.** Flippo needs large margins in mining, ranching and rural communities to offset Democratic votes in the Reno area.
- **Lake Tahoe communities.** Resort-area voting, housing concerns and independents may offer clues about affluent and suburban behavior.
- **Chapman's share.** A result near her 2024 5% would be significant when polls show the major candidates only a few points apart.

## Assessment

Flippo remains favored, but NV-2 has become a genuine Democratic pickup opportunity. The Republican case rests on historical voting, a substantial registration advantage, Trump's 2024 margin, Flippo's fundraising and more outside spending. Benitez-Thompson's rests on legislative experience, the absence of an incumbent, a possibly favorable national environment and polling suggesting the Republican edge has narrowed sharply. Caution is warranted: all three surveys have Democratic sponsorship, and nothing independent shows the race is as close as they imply. Lean Republican fits both the forecasters and the evidence; a Flippo win by several points is still likeliest, but a Benitez-Thompson upset would be plausible and historic.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
