Pennsylvania's 1st District covers Bucks County and part of Montgomery County in Philadelphia's northern suburbs, where recent presidential voting has been almost perfectly split. Yet Republican Brian Fitzpatrick keeps winning by much more than his party's presidential candidates. His 2026 opponent, Democratic Bucks County Commissioner Bob Harvie, is trying to change that.

The defining question is how Fitzpatrick's record of attracting crossover voters interacts with the national environment and Harvie's local experience.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brian Fitzpatrick | Republican | Incumbent since 2017; age 52; former FBI special agent and federal prosecutor; Problem Solvers Caucus; Levittown |
| Bob Harvie | Democrat | Bucks County commissioner since 2019 (board vice chair); former high-school social studies teacher; Bristol Borough |

## The district

PA-1 is centered on Bucks County, from the dense suburbs near Philadelphia to the small towns and countryside of Upper Bucks, plus a Montgomery County portion.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Lower Bucks (Bensalem, Bristol, Levittown) | Older suburbs; manufacturing, logistics; diverse working- and middle-class communities |
| Central Bucks (Doylestown, Warrington, Newtown) | Affluent suburbs; professionals; schools; commuters |
| Upper Bucks (Quakertown area) | Small towns, farming, manufacturing, exurban growth |
| Montgomery County portion | Philadelphia-area suburbs and employment centers |

Healthcare, education, pharmaceuticals, small business, logistics and professional services anchor the economy; housing affordability, property taxes, commuting, schools and federal taxes are salient.

| Measure (ACS 2020–2024, via VoteSee) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Population | about 767K |
| Median household income | about $114K |
| Bachelor's degree or higher (adults 25+) | 44.9% |

**The paradox.** In 2024 the district voted almost evenly for president, but Fitzpatrick won by about 13 points (about 56.4%), after roughly 10 in 2022. Candidate-specific considerations have clearly mattered, though past results alone can't say how voters will behave in 2026.

## Candidates

### Brian Fitzpatrick (R)

He succeeded his brother, Rep. Mike Fitzpatrick, in 2016. He's in the bipartisan Problem Solvers Caucus and differs from many House Republicans on climate, certain gun regulations and LGBTQ rights. His record includes efforts to ban congressional stock trading, regulate PFAS, lower drug costs and change shutdown rules.

An important vote: he voted against the final version of the 2025 Republican tax and spending law, citing healthcare and food-assistance cuts, after earlier voting to advance it. That distinguishes him from several other Republicans in competitive seats. His endorsements are cross-party too: Republican groups and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, plus the Pennsylvania AFL-CIO, SEIU's Pennsylvania council, the NEA and the League of Conservation Voters Action Fund. Those are organizational decisions, not evidence every member backs him.

### Bob Harvie (D)

A teacher for more than two decades, then in Falls Township government, Harvie won a county commissioner seat in 2019, giving Democrats a majority on the commission for the first time in nearly four decades. He stresses working with Republican colleagues on county administration, public safety, housing, mental health and economic development, and says he voted with his Republican colleague on more than 90% of county measures. His platform: expanded ACA subsidies, lower drug prices, middle-class tax relief, a higher minimum wage, ethics limits and opposition to Trump's tariffs. He has a local governing record where most of the district's voters live.

## The issues

**Healthcare and the 2025 law.** More complicated here than in most Republican-held seats. Fitzpatrick voted against final passage, distancing himself from the Medicaid and food-aid changes, and stresses drug affordability and medical research. Harvie wants enhanced ACA subsidies restored and expanded, Medicare enrollment from age 55 and broader drug-price negotiation.

**Taxes, tariffs and costs.** Fitzpatrick stresses tax relief, jobs and trade policy protecting American workers; Harvie backs middle-class tax cuts and calls Trump's tariffs a cost to consumers and businesses. Housing, energy, healthcare and property taxes are part of the picture, some federal, some not.

**Abortion.** Fitzpatrick voted against the 2022 Women's Health Protection Act but supported legislation protecting interstate travel for abortion services, so his positions vary by measure. Harvie supports abortion rights.

**Ethics.** Both support limits on congressional stock trading. Harvie also proposes banning corporate PAC money, lifetime lobbying bans for ex-members, an age limit for members and limits on partisan gerrymandering. Fitzpatrick has sponsored stock-trading and shutdown-pay bills.

**Environment and data centers.** Fitzpatrick has worked across party lines on PFAS and climate measures; Harvie backs clean-energy investment and limits on data-center overdevelopment over electricity and infrastructure costs.

**Campaign disputes.** The race has included allegations about county governance, competing ethics claims and a defamation lawsuit. They're part of the record, but allegations aren't established wrongdoing without findings; WHYY's voter guide documents the accusations without settling who is right.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Crossover voting.** A near-tied presidential district where the incumbent got about 56.4% for Congress.
- **A vote against the Republican budget law.** Sets Fitzpatrick apart from Ryan Mackenzie in [PA-7](../pa-07/) and Rob Bresnahan in [PA-8](../pa-08/).
- **A locally established challenger.** Harvie has won countywide in Bucks and served in local government for years.
- **Cross-party endorsements.** Labor and environmental groups complicate assumptions about coalitions.
- **High household incomes.** A different profile from the industrial and rural electorates of PA-7 and PA-8.
- **Pennsylvania's four seats.** PA-1, PA-7, PA-8 and [PA-10](../pa-10/) are all Republican-held and all drawing national attention.
