Michigan's 4th District in southwestern Michigan is another Republican-held seat drawing more attention in the closing weeks. Longtime Republican Rep. Bill Huizenga faces Democratic state Sen. Sean McCann of Kalamazoo, with Green Shafina Che Barnett also on the ballot.

The contrast is between history and trajectory. Huizenga won by more than 12 points in 2024, but Trump carried the district by only about six. The traditionally conservative western Michigan suburbs, especially in Ottawa County, have become more competitive in presidential elections.

## The district

MI-4 holds all of Allegan and Van Buren counties and parts of Ottawa, Kalamazoo, Calhoun and Berrien counties: Holland, parts of Kalamazoo and Battle Creek, and smaller communities along Lake Michigan and inland.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Holland and Ottawa County | Manufacturing, small business, agriculture; historically conservative |
| Kalamazoo area | Higher education, healthcare, pharmaceutical and life-sciences jobs |
| Battle Creek area | Food manufacturing, logistics, industrial jobs |
| Allegan and Van Buren | Agriculture, food processing, small towns, tourism |
| Lake Michigan communities | Tourism, seasonal housing, agriculture, manufacturing |

Western Michigan has a long Republican tradition shaped by religiously conservative communities and business-oriented politics, while Kalamazoo and the university-linked areas look different. Ottawa County is notable: Cook identifies it as one of the few Michigan counties where Harris improved on Biden's 2020 margin in 2024, despite the national shift toward Trump.

| 2024 House | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Huizenga (R) over Jessica Swartz (D) | 55.4% to 42.8% (R +12.6) |

Cook's PVI is R+3, based on presidential voting. Huizenga ran well ahead of the presidential result, and 2026 will show how much of that was personal support, the weakness of the earlier challenger, or other election-specific factors.

## Candidates

### Bill Huizenga (R)

A lifelong western Michigander, Huizenga served in the Michigan House and worked for former Rep. Pete Hoekstra before Congress, and co-owns his family's ready-mix concrete business. On Financial Services and Foreign Affairs, he handles financial institutions, digital assets and international affairs. His campaign emphasizes taxes, domestic energy, growth and reducing inflation, and he points to his record representing western Michigan's manufacturing and agricultural interests.

### Sean McCann (D)

A Kalamazoo state senator who previously served in the Michigan House and on the Kalamazoo City Commission, and who is term-limited, McCann has Gov. Gretchen Whitmer's endorsement. His priorities are healthcare access, household affordability and energy costs. Unlike Huizenga's 2024 opponent, he's an established legislator who has represented part of the district.

### Shafina Che Barnett (Green)

The Green nominee makes it a three-candidate ballot; polling doesn't show her districtwide support.

Through October 14, 2026. Huizenga has raised more, but McCann has far more unspent money. Outside spending dwarfs both: about **$20.56 million** in independent expenditures through October 22 ($19.28M in the general election), nearly evenly split between supporting McCann or opposing Huizenga (about $9.7M) and the reverse (about $9.6M). The largest general-election spender was No Going Back PAC at about $5.4M. These are not direct contributions.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A changing western Michigan.** Its Republican identity remains, but Ottawa County's presidential shift has analysts' attention.
- **A big gap between presidential and House margins.** Huizenga's 2024 win far outran Trump's district margin; 2026 is another observation without a presidential race.
- **A legislator against a veteran incumbent.** McCann has a legislative record and local ties; Huizenga has long federal experience.
- **Big money.** More than $19M in general-election outside spending.
- **A Michigan cluster.** Alongside [MI-7](../mi-07/) and open [MI-10](../mi-10/), MI-4 is part of the state's battleground picture.

Compare [CO-5](../co-05/): both have long Republican congressional histories and suburbs in flux, but MI-4 mixes university communities, farms and manufacturing towns, while CO-5 has a heavy military economy.
