Elise Stefanik has represented New York's 21st District since 2015 and is retiring, leaving an open seat in a heavily Republican, largely rural district in northern New York. Trump carried it by about 21 points in 2024.

Neither nominee has held federal office: Anthony Constantino (R) is a Trump-endorsed businessman, and Blake Gendebien (D) is a dairy farmer. A competitive-looking October poll, discussed [below](#polling-and-ratings), has led forecasters to treat the race as more contested than the district's partisanship suggests, though all three still rate it Republican-leaning.

## The district

NY-21 is one of the largest districts east of the Mississippi by area. It covers much of the North Country and the Adirondacks, from the Canadian border toward the Mohawk Valley, and includes Plattsburgh, Lake Placid, Glens Falls and Amsterdam. The economy is unusually varied for a rural region: dairy, forestry, tourism, manufacturing, military employment and trade with Canada all matter.

Local concerns include:

- **Agriculture:** farm consolidation, dairy prices, operating costs, market access.
- **Healthcare:** keeping hospitals and services open across a sparsely populated region.
- **Canada:** border security, tariffs, tourism and cross-border commerce.
- **Demography:** population loss, aging communities, difficulty retaining younger workers.
- **Infrastructure:** rural broadband, transportation, reliable energy.

The seat was not always safe for Republicans. Democrat Bill Owens won a 2009 special election and served until 2015, when Stefanik took over.

## Candidates

### Anthony Constantino (R)

Constantino, 43, founded Sticker Mule, a custom-printing company based in Amsterdam, New York. He won the Republican primary over Assemblymember Robert Smullen despite Smullen's backing from many county party organizations. Trump endorsed him, and he ties his campaign closely to the president. His priorities are manufacturing jobs, border security, economic development and a government-modernization proposal called Operation Overhaul.

His advertising has been unconventional, including AI-generated videos and political music. An August AI video showed Gendebien saying things he never said, which drew criticism; Constantino defended it as a response to earlier AI material from Gendebien's campaign.

### Blake Gendebien (D)

Gendebien farms in Lisbon, St. Lawrence County, and has served on a school board and in agricultural cooperative leadership. He beat Lake Placid entrepreneur Stuart Amoriell in the primary. He calls himself a conservative Democrat and free-market capitalist, and campaigns on household costs, rural healthcare, farmers and infrastructure.

He began organizing in late 2024, when Stefanik was briefly nominated as U.N. ambassador. The nomination was withdrawn, but his early start gave him a long head start once the open seat was confirmed.

### Christopher Schmidt (I, write-in)

Schmidt, a Washington County community organizer, did not qualify for a printed ballot line and is running as a write-in. He emphasizes jobs, infrastructure, utilities and rural healthcare.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Candidates versus partisanship.** NY-21 tests how far individual candidate appeal can move votes in a strongly partisan district.
- **Republican coalition tensions.** Constantino's win over a party-backed state legislator reflects a different constituency within the party than the one that supported Smullen.
- **Control of the House.** Every competitive Republican-held seat counts toward the majority, and NY-21 was not initially among the seats drawing the most attention.
- **Canada.** The district's economy is tied to Quebec and Ontario, which gives relations with Canada unusual local weight.

The most recent public poll is a Siena Research Institute survey (Sept. 30–Oct. 6; 496 likely voters; ±4.6 points) with Gendebien at 46%, Constantino at 42%, Schmidt at 5% and 8% undecided. The four-point gap is within the margin of error. Other findings from the same poll:

- Gendebien drew 21% of Republicans; Constantino drew 65%. Independents favored Gendebien, 51% to 30%.
- Favorability: Gendebien 46/29, Constantino 39/52.
- Voters in the district preferred the Republican for governor, 55% to 40%, and Republican control of Congress, 50% to 45%.
- On relations with Canada, respondents preferred Gendebien over Constantino, 52% to 35%.

Earlier August polls disagreed: J.L. Partners had Constantino ahead 53% to 40%, and SurveyUSA had Gendebien ahead 43% to 39%. Differences in sample, timing and method limit comparison.

## What to watch

Whether late deciders follow the district's usual partisan lean, whether Constantino's money changes the closing weeks, and how many votes Schmidt's write-in campaign actually draws, since survey support rarely converts fully into write-in ballots. A four-point poll lead from a sample under 500 is a long way from a result in a district with this much Republican history.
