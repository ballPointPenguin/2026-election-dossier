Texas's 34th District is another major South Texas battleground: Democratic incumbent Vicente Gonzalez against Republican Eric Flores, an attorney and former federal prosecutor, with Libertarian Chris Royal and Green Eddie Espinoza also on the ballot.

Three things converge here: Texas's 2025 Republican redistricting, the ongoing shift of South Texas's Latino electorate, and the national fight for the House. The key question is whether Gonzalez can hold on after the map swapped out Democratic territory near McAllen for more Republican-voting territory around Corpus Christi. Polling has been close and both parties have invested heavily.

## The district

The redrawn TX-34 runs north from Brownsville along the Gulf Coast toward Corpus Christi, covering Cameron, Willacy, Kenedy and Kleberg counties plus most of Nueces County, joining the Rio Grande Valley and the Coastal Bend.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Brownsville and Cameron County | Cross-border commerce, logistics, healthcare, manufacturing, Hispanic-majority communities |
| Harlingen and the Valley | Healthcare, agriculture, transportation, working-class households |
| Kingsville and Kleberg County | Agriculture, energy, university jobs, military ties |
| Corpus Christi and Nueces County | Port, petrochemicals, refining, manufacturing, coastal tourism |

**Redistricting.** The big change was trading Democratic-voting territory near McAllen for more Republican territory around Corpus Christi.

| Measure | Old map | New map |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trump 2024 vote | 51.8% (+4.5) | 54.7% (+10.2) |

(Reconstructed presidential results for each map.) It's a big shift, though smaller than in some other redrawn Texas districts. A complication: Gonzalez lives in the McAllen area, which is mostly out of the new district. That's legal, since members must live in the state, not the district, but it makes his ties to the newly added Corpus Christi voters a real campaign question.

## Candidates

### Vicente Gonzalez (D)

Gonzalez, 59, an attorney, entered Congress in January 2017 representing TX-15 and moved to TX-34 in 2022 after an earlier redistricting. A Blue Dog, he stresses border security, infrastructure, veterans, healthcare, energy production and federal investment in South Texas, and distances himself from the party's progressive wing. In March he beat democratic socialist Etienne Rosas in the primary by about 25 points.

### Eric Flores (R)

A South Texas attorney and former federal prosecutor with law-enforcement and military experience, Flores won a crowded primary that included former Rep. Mayra Flores, who briefly held TX-34 after a June 2022 special election. He stresses border enforcement, public safety, economic development, lower taxes and the Trump administration's priorities, and the Texas Tribune reports strong national Republican backing under the new lines.

### Eddie Espinoza (Green) and Chris Royal (L)

In the August Texas Southern poll Espinoza got 3% and Royal 2%. In a close plurality race that could matter, but their voters' second choices are unknown.

## History

TX-34 has had a run of tight elections. In June 2022 Republican Mayra Flores won a special election after Democrat Filemon Vela resigned; that November Gonzalez beat her in the regular general election under the redrawn lines. In 2024 Gonzalez won with 51.3% even though Trump carried the district. Presidential and House voting don't always move together here: a voter can back a Republican for president and still vote for a Democratic House member with a local record. 2026 tests that under yet another map.

## Issues

- **Border and immigration.** Tied to family networks, jobs, trade and law enforcement. Gonzalez's moderate record makes for a different contrast with Flores than a progressive would.
- **Energy and industry.** Corpus Christi's port, refining and petrochemicals make federal oil and gas, environmental and trade decisions directly relevant.
- **Healthcare and affordability.** Access, insurance costs, wages and household expenses, interacting with the candidates' broader disagreements on federal taxes, spending and healthcare.
- **Latino realignment.** Majority Hispanic, but not a uniform bloc. Trump gained in South Texas in 2024 while Gonzalez kept House support.
