North Carolina's 1st District in northeastern North Carolina is one of the most closely watched Democratic-held seats in the country. It's a rematch between Democratic incumbent Don Davis and Republican Laurie Buckhout, with Libertarian Tom Bailey also on the ballot.

The story is redistricting. In October 2025, North Carolina's Republican legislature redrew the district to boost Republicans, turning a nearly even seat into one Trump would have carried by about 12 points in 2024. Yet October polling is more competitive than that baseline suggests, and Cook moved the race from Lean Republican to Toss Up on October 16.

## The district

NC-1 is a large, mostly rural district in eastern North Carolina, including Rocky Mount, Elizabeth City, the Outer Banks, coastal-plain farm counties and communities along the waterways.

- **Agriculture:** tobacco, soybeans, sweet potatoes, livestock.
- **Rural healthcare:** hospital sustainability, Medicaid, access to physicians.
- **Coast:** tourism, fishing, hurricanes, erosion, flood insurance.
- **Military:** veterans and military families near the region's installations.
- **Black communities:** eastern North Carolina has a long history of Black political representation and organizing, particularly in Democratic-leaning counties.

**The 2025 map.** The legislature adopted new congressional lines in October 2025, mid-decade and without a new census. For NC-1:

| Removed | Added |
| --- | --- |
| More Democratic areas around Wilson | Republican-voting Outer Banks areas |
| Areas around Kinston | Parts of Craven County |

(Per Spectrum News, October 2026.) Recalculated for the 2026 lines, Trump carried the district by 11.6 in 2024, and Cook's PVI is R+5. Inside Elections' baseline moved from about D+0.3 to R+8, a different method but the same scale of shift.

Redistricting is itself a campaign issue. Davis introduced the RESET Act (Restoring Electoral Stability to Enhance Trust), which would restrict partisan mid-decade redistricting, arguing the practice undermines stability whichever party does it.

## Candidates

### Don Davis (D)

Davis is an Air Force veteran, former mayor of Snow Hill and former state senator, in Congress since 2023. He has a relatively independent record, including votes for some Republican-backed border-security and government-funding bills. He campaigns on rural hospitals, agriculture, affordable healthcare, household costs and federal attention for eastern North Carolina. He credits parts of Trump's border enforcement as effective while opposing portions of the tax-and-spending law over Medicaid and rural healthcare. On the Iran war he has questioned the planning and called for more congressional oversight.

### Laurie Buckhout (R)

Buckhout is a retired Army colonel, former defense contractor and business owner who worked on cybersecurity policy in the second Trump administration. She ran against Davis in 2024 and is back under the new lines. Her campaign stresses the America First agenda, border security, national defense, lower taxes, deregulation and growth. She attacks Davis for opposing the tax-and-spending law as inconsistent with delivering tax relief, defends the Iran policy, and wants less regulation, including of AI development.

### Tom Bailey (L)

Bailey got about 2.6% in 2024, nearly 10,000 votes, while Davis beat Buckhout by just over 6,000. That doesn't mean his voters would have gone to either major party.

## Why it matters nationally

**Mid-decade redistricting.** The 2025 map was designed to shift the balance, so NC-1 is a test case in the broader fight over mapmaking between censuses, though the result can't separate the map's effect from turnout, candidates and the national mood.

**A moderate Democrat on hostile ground.** Davis breaks with his party on immigration enforcement and some spending, which separates his profile from the district's presidential vote. The question is whether Republican-leaning voters split their tickets.

**Rural healthcare.** Davis says the tax-and-spending law hurts Medicaid and rural hospitals; Buckhout stresses its tax provisions and growth aims. In a region where rural hospitals are under pressure, national fiscal policy becomes local.

**Early voting.** Statewide North Carolina early voting through October 18 was ahead of the comparable point in 2022, with more ballots from registered Democrats than Republicans (per Axios). Those are statewide numbers, not NC-1 totals or a signal of who is ahead.

## What to watch

Whether late polls converge, turnout in the added and removed areas, whether Bailey's support holds, and how rural healthcare and cost of living play at the end.
