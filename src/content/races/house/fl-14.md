Florida's 14th District is one of the most consequential products of the 2026 redistricting cycle. Democrat Kathy Castor, who has represented the Tampa area for nearly 20 years, is running in a district redrawn to take in more Republican-voting communities. Her main challenger is Mike Beltran, a former Republican state representative and attorney. Libertarian Brian Lambert is also on the printed ballot, with two certified write-ins.

The striking fact: Kamala Harris carried the old FL-14 by nearly eight points in 2024, but Trump would have carried the new one by about eleven. That's a different electorate, not the same voters changing their minds.

## The district

FL-14 was historically centered on Tampa and parts of neighboring Pinellas County, with plenty of Democratic-voting urban neighborhoods. Florida's new map, signed by Gov. Ron DeSantis on May 4, 2026, moved it south and east into more of eastern and rural Hillsborough County, including the Plant City area. It now spans Tampa's urban communities, expanding suburbs, farmland and areas tied to the region's transportation and logistics economy.

## Candidates

### Kathy Castor (D)

Castor, 60, was first elected in 2006 and took office in 2007, after serving on the Hillsborough County Commission. A senior Energy and Commerce member, her record centers on healthcare, consumer protection, environment, energy and federal investment in Tampa Bay. She campaigns on household costs, healthcare access and regional infrastructure. Much of the new district hasn't been hers before.

### Mike Beltran (R)

Beltran, an attorney, served in the Florida House from 2018 to 2024. He won the August 18 primary with about 45.6% in a multicandidate field and has endorsements from Trump, DeSantis and leading congressional Republicans. He stresses conservative fiscal policy, less regulation, border enforcement and Republican economic and social approaches. His state-legislative record is the point of comparison with Castor's congressional tenure.

### Others

Libertarian Brian Lambert is the third printed-ballot candidate. Salomon Hernandez and Keith Varian qualified as write-ins and won't appear as regular printed choices.

Through September 30, 2026. The bigger story is outside spending: about **$19.6 million** reported through October 18, about $10.8 million of it in the last 14 days. In the general election roughly $11.4M opposed Castor and $5.2M opposed Beltran, plus support spending for each. The largest spenders include the Republican-aligned No Going Back PAC and Congressional Leadership Fund and the Democratic-aligned House Majority PAC, and environmental groups are in too. National organizations clearly see FL-14 as consequential for the House majority, however they rate it.

## The issues

- **Healthcare and federal spending.** Castor's Energy and Commerce work puts healthcare, insurance and consumer protection at the center. Beltran favors tax cuts, spending restraint and deregulation. Federal healthcare and budget law is a real area of disagreement.
- **Housing and affordability.** Tampa Bay has grown fast and faces housing-cost pressure: homeowners' insurance, property taxes, rent, transportation costs, wages.
- **Hurricanes, flooding, insurance.** Disaster aid, resilience, flood insurance and property-insurance costs are key federal questions.
- **Energy and climate.** Castor has a long climate and clean-energy record; Beltran stresses energy costs and regulatory burden. Environmental groups' spending reflects the divide.

## Why it matters

**Redistricting.** The swing from a Harris-won district to one Trump carried by double digits on reconstructed lines is unusually large.

**Incumbency.** Castor has represented Tampa for two decades, but the new map changed who is judging that record.

**National money.** Nearly $20 million of outside spending for one House district is a lot, much of it in October.

**Compared with [FL-13](../fl-13/) and [FL-27](../fl-27/).** Those feature Republican incumbents defending seats; FL-14 has a Democratic incumbent defending a district redistricting made much more Republican.
