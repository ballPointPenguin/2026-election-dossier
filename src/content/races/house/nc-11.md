North Carolina's 11th District, which covers Asheville and most of the state's western mountains, is a closely watched open seat after Republican incumbent Chuck Edwards unexpectedly withdrew from his reelection campaign in August. The candidates are Jamie Ager (Democrat), Jennifer Balkcom (Republican) and Travis Groo (Libertarian).

Edwards won in 2024 by about 14 points and the district has a Republican presidential history. But his departure, a huge fundraising gap and the aftermath of Hurricane Helene have drawn attention. As of mid-October all four major forecasters rate it a Toss Up, which is their assessment, not a new district poll.

## The district

NC-11 reaches from Asheville to the Tennessee, Georgia and South Carolina borders, taking in Buncombe, Henderson, Haywood, Jackson, Macon, Madison, Cherokee, Clay, Graham, Swain, Transylvania, Yancey and other counties.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Asheville / Buncombe | Tourism, healthcare, arts, hospitality, higher education |
| Hendersonville / Henderson | Retirees, manufacturing, agriculture, suburban growth |
| Haywood and Jackson | Mountain towns, manufacturing, Western Carolina University, tourism |
| Far-western counties | Rural; forestry, agriculture, outdoor recreation, small business |
| Yancey, Mitchell, Avery | Mountain communities hit hard by Helene |

Democratic-leaning Asheville sits inside a district full of strongly Republican rural areas, so the districtwide result blends very different local electorates. Tourism and hospitality coexist with manufacturing, farming, forestry and retirement services.

## Candidates

### Jamie Ager (D)

Ager runs a regional sustainable-meat business working with farmers across western North Carolina. His candidacy grows out of Hurricane Helene (September 2024), which damaged his own farm; he criticizes delays in federal disaster aid and rebuilding money. He campaigns on disaster recovery, rural economic development, healthcare access, housing affordability, small farms and a congressional stock-trading ban. In a May Washington Post interview he said national Democrats have sometimes failed to understand rural livelihoods and small communities, a theme shared with Lindsay James in [IA-2](../ia-02/).

### Jennifer Balkcom (R)

Balkcom has served in the NC House since 2023 and has deep family roots in western North Carolina. Her record includes conservative tax policy, opposing Medicaid expansion and restricting transgender participation in girls' and women's sports. She campaigns on Helene recovery, economic development, public safety and Republican approaches to taxes and regulation. She wasn't chosen in the ordinary March primary: district Republican officials picked her after Edwards withdrew, leaving little time to introduce her across the district.

### Travis Groo (L)

The Libertarian nominee favors less government involvement in healthcare and more flexibility in local housing development. Polling doesn't show his districtwide support.

## Why Edwards withdrew

On August 3, the bipartisan House Ethics Committee released findings that there was substantial reason to believe Edwards violated House rules on sexual harassment and hostile workplaces, concerning his conduct toward two female staff members, and recommended censure. Edwards disputed the findings and said he hadn't violated the rules, while expressing regret that his conduct caused discomfort. On August 5 he announced he would drop his reelection bid while finishing his term. He remains the sitting representative but isn't a November candidate.

Republicans no longer defend his conduct on the ballot, but they lost an incumbent's name recognition, organization and constituent relationships. Cook cited Balkcom's limited districtwide recognition and the short replacement timeline in its September assessment.

## Hurricane Helene

Helene hit western North Carolina in September 2024 with catastrophic flooding, landslides, infrastructure destruction and economic disruption. More than two years later, recovery is still politically live: damaged roads and bridges, rebuilding homes, restoring small businesses, stabilizing local government finances and federal reimbursement.

Both candidates say more recovery money is a priority. Ager stresses his experience rebuilding his farm and criticizes federal delays and obstacles; Balkcom stresses her ties to affected communities and her role as a state legislator. The Washington Post documents continuing frustration over reimbursement delays, damaged infrastructure and residents unable to return to permanent housing. The issue cuts across party lines: a closed road, a delayed FEMA payment or a missing home affects people regardless of national politics.

## Other policy differences

| Issue | Ager (D) | Balkcom (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Supports Medicaid, Medicare, affordable access | Voted against Medicaid expansion in the legislature |
| Taxes | Targeted help for households, homebuyers, working families | Has backed eliminating NC's state income tax |
| Agriculture | Small farms, right-to-repair, local markets | Economic development, conservative tax policy |
| Social policy | Endorsed by groups supporting abortion rights | Supports limits on transgender participation in girls' and women's sports |
| Helene | Federal funding, rebuilding, administrative delays | More recovery funding and support for affected communities |

Rural healthcare matters too: hospital finances, Medicaid policy, specialist access and long travel to treatment all weigh heavily in mountain communities.

## Why it matters nationally

- **An abrupt departure.** Edwards's withdrawal changed candidates and campaign organizations less than three months before Election Day.
- **A Republican-voting Appalachian district.** Democratic Asheville plus much more Republican rural and small-town areas.
- **A Democrat running on rural identity.** Ager's farming background is central, not incidental.
- **A disaster recovery in progress.** Federal performance is tangible where Helene's rebuilding continues.
- **House control.** Both parties see the seat as relevant to the majority.

Compare [NC-1](../nc-01/), the state's other competitive district, where redistricting rather than scandal changed the picture.

## What to watch

New polling, campaign finance developments, and Helene-related local reporting. Turnout in Asheville versus the rural western counties will matter.
