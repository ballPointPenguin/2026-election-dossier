Texas's 15th District pits Republican Rep. Monica De La Cruz, first elected in 2022, against Bobby Pulido, a Latin Grammy-winning Tejano singer and first-time Democratic candidate. Trump carried the district by about 18 points in 2024, and De La Cruz won with 57.1%, roughly 14 points. Yet all three national forecasters now rate it a Toss Up, based largely on Democratic-sponsored polling (see [below](#polling-and-ratings)).

The district was also redrawn in 2025 as part of Texas Republicans' mid-decade effort to improve their House position.

## The district

TX-15 is predominantly Hispanic and anchored in the Rio Grande Valley. Its mix of border communities, agricultural producers, small businesses, working-class households and rural areas farther north makes the border an economic matter as much as an immigration one: trade, cross-border families, farm labor, transportation and relations with Mexico all figure in the regional economy.

**The 2025 map.** The old district ran north from the Valley toward Seguin and Floresville, southeast of San Antonio. The new one:

- keeps its southern anchor in Hidalgo County;
- trades parts of McAllen for Donna and Weslaco;
- adds Coastal Bend areas, including Beeville;
- extends northeast into rural territory between San Antonio and Houston.

De La Cruz is therefore running in territory she has not represented before, although Trump's 2024 margin was about 18 points under both sets of lines.

## Candidates

### Monica De La Cruz (R)

De La Cruz won in 2022, the first Republican to hold the seat in its modern form, and widened her margin in 2024. She aligns closely with the Trump administration on immigration enforcement, border security, taxes and economic development, and has promoted parts of the 2025 Republican tax and spending law in her campaign.

### Bobby Pulido (D)

Pulido, 53, presents himself as a moderate Democrat focused on economic opportunity, affordability and working families. He says his music career, which involved listening to audiences and sustaining relationships with them, prepared him for politics. His fame gives him an unusual profile among Hispanic and Tejano voters. He held a farewell concert, *La Última Desvelada*, at Edinburg's Bert Ogden Arena on October 17, two days before early voting began.

## Why it matters nationally

**The Valley's realignment.** South Texas was long Democratic territory, built on local political networks, organized labor and Hispanic Democratic constituencies. Republicans made large gains in 2020 and 2024. Explanations are contested: immigration attitudes, economic concerns, social conservatism, religion, party identification and dissatisfaction with Democratic messaging have all been proposed. TX-15 is a test of whether those gains hold in a midterm electorate.

**Redistricting's limits.** A map built to help Republicans does not guarantee Republican congressional votes, since a district's presidential history is an imperfect guide when candidates, turnout and national conditions shift.

**A regional cluster.** TX-15 is one of three competitive South Texas contests. House Speaker Mike Johnson visited McAllen in mid-October and stressed their importance to the majority.

## What to watch

Whether Pulido's polling translates into votes, how turnout compares with the 2024 presidential electorate, and whether the newly added areas vote differently from the established Valley communities. The statewide U.S. Senate race between Republican Ken Paxton and Democrat James Talarico is drawing heavy attention and resources, and could affect Valley turnout, though its net effect on House races is unclear.

Texas early voting runs October 19–30. Election Day is November 3, 2026.
