Texas's 15th Congressional District has become a closely watched contest in the 2026 midterms. It pits Republican incumbent Monica De La Cruz against Bobby Pulido, a Latin Grammy-winning Tejano musician running as a Democrat.

The race is particularly interesting because TX-15 voted for Donald Trump by approximately 18 percentage points in 2024, yet multiple 2026 polls show Pulido ahead. The district was also redrawn in 2025 as part of Texas Republicans' effort to improve their congressional position.

## 2. The district: Rio Grande Valley and South Texas

TX-15 is a predominantly Hispanic district anchored in the Rio Grande Valley, a region whose electoral behavior has undergone remarkable changes over the past decade.

The district's 2025 redistricting is especially consequential.

Under the previous boundaries, TX-15 extended north from the Rio Grande Valley toward Seguin and Floresville, southeast of San Antonio. The new map:

- Retains its southern anchor in Hidalgo County.
- Trades portions of McAllen for Donna and Weslaco.
- Incorporates areas of the Coastal Bend, including Beeville.
- Extends northeast into rural territory between San Antonio and Houston.

This means De La Cruz is seeking reelection in a district with substantial territory she has not previously represented. Importantly, however, Trump's 2024 margin was approximately 18 points under both sets of boundaries.

The district combines several political and economic constituencies: border communities, agricultural producers, small businesses, working-class Hispanic households, and rural communities farther north.

The border is not merely an immigration-policy abstraction here. International trade, cross-border families, agricultural labor, transportation, and relationships with Mexico are integral to the regional economy.

## 3. The candidates

### Monica De La Cruz — Republican

De La Cruz, from Edinburg, previously worked in insurance and was first elected to Congress in 2022, becoming the first Republican to represent the 15th District in its modern configuration.

She is closely aligned with the Trump administration on immigration enforcement, border security, tax policy and economic development. She has highlighted provisions of the 2025 Republican tax and spending legislation as part of her reelection campaign.

Her political history is significant: she won the district in 2022, then expanded her margin in 2024. That trajectory illustrates the Republican Party's growing support in parts of the Rio Grande Valley during recent election cycles.

### Bobby Pulido — Democrat

Pulido, 53, is a well-known Tejano singer from Edinburg with a roughly 30-year musical career and a Latin Grammy Award. He is making his first run for Congress.

His campaign presents him as a moderate Democrat emphasizing economic opportunity, affordability, and representation of working families in South Texas.

His celebrity gives him a different public profile from most first-time congressional candidates, particularly among Hispanic and Tejano audiences.

In an unusual campaign milestone, Pulido performed his farewell concert, *La Última Desvelada*, at Edinburg's Bert Ogden Arena on October 17, just two days before early voting began.

Pulido has described his music career as preparation for politics, particularly the experience of listening to people and maintaining relationships with audiences.

## 5. Why TX-15 matters nationally

### The realignment of Hispanic voters

Perhaps the most important historical context is the changing partisan orientation of the Rio Grande Valley.

For generations, South Texas was associated with Democratic political dominance, often rooted in local political networks, organized labor, and longstanding Hispanic Democratic constituencies.

Republicans made substantial gains during the Trump era, especially in 2020 and 2024. The explanation remains contested: immigration attitudes, economic concerns, social conservatism, religious affiliation, party identification, and dissatisfaction with Democratic messaging have all been proposed as contributing factors.

TX-15 offers an opportunity to examine whether those changes persist in a midterm electorate and how they interact with voters' evaluations of individual candidates.

### The consequences of redistricting

Texas Republicans enacted a new congressional map in 2025 designed to increase Republican representation.

Yet TX-15 illustrates a limitation of redistricting as an electoral instrument: a district's historical presidential vote is not necessarily a reliable measure of its subsequent congressional vote, especially when candidates, turnout patterns, and national conditions change.

The distinction between a district's demographic composition, partisan voting history, and candidate-specific support is particularly important here.

### The South Texas congressional battleground

TX-15 is also part of a cluster of competitive congressional contests in the region.

House Speaker Mike Johnson visited McAllen in mid-October and emphasized the importance of these three races to congressional control.

The simultaneous competition across these districts creates an interesting comparison of Republican and Democratic strategies in Hispanic-majority constituencies.

## 6. What to watch through November 3

The most consequential outstanding questions are whether Pulido's polling support translates into votes, how turnout differs from the presidential electorate of 2024, and whether the newly incorporated areas of TX-15 behave differently from the district's established communities.

Texas's statewide elections add another dimension. The U.S. Senate contest between Republican Ken Paxton and Democrat James Talarico has drawn substantial attention and resources. The Senate race could affect overall voter participation in the Rio Grande Valley, although the net effect on individual House races remains uncertain.

For continuing coverage, useful sources include the [Texas Tribune's congressional election guide](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/30/texas-congress-house-elections-2026-midterms/) and [Cook Political Report's TX-15 analysis](https://www.cookpolitical.com/house/race/485291).

The central story in TX-15 is whether the Republican gains of the Trump era endure in a competitive midterm election. The district's recent Republican voting record, the 2025 redistricting, Pulido's unusual candidacy, and the polling divergence make this a particularly revealing contest for understanding the evolving politics of South Texas.
