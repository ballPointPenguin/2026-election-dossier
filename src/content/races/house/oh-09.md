Ohio's 9th District features the longest-serving woman in House history, Democrat Marcy Kaptur, seeking a 23rd term in a rematch with Republican Derek Merrin, a former state legislator who came within about 2,400 votes of beating her in 2024. Libertarian Matthew Althaus is also on the ballot.

The question is whether Kaptur's personal support can survive another shift in the district's partisanship. Republicans redrew OH-9 in 2025, and on the new lines Trump would have carried it by about 11 points in 2024. Even so, Cook and Inside Elections rate it a Toss Up, and Sabato's final ratings (Nov. 2) lean Democratic.

## The district

OH-9 is centered on Toledo, runs east along the Lake Erie shore toward Sandusky, and reaches inland into farm and small-town communities.

- **Toledo:** manufacturing, auto suppliers, organized labor, healthcare, the port.
- **Lake Erie communities:** tourism, fishing, water quality, shoreline infrastructure, restoration.
- **Rural northwest Ohio:** agriculture, manufacturing, small business, conservative Republican voting.
- **Industrial energy users:** electricity prices, industrial investment, data-center demand.

Toledo's unionized manufacturing economy long sustained Democratic representation, but many smaller industrial and farm communities in Ohio have moved Republican in presidential races.

**Redistricting.** Ohio's 2025 map added heavily Republican Paulding, Putnam and Henry counties.

For a decade OH-9 was nicknamed the "snake on the lake" for its long, narrow shape along the shoreline. The geography has changed again, but the underlying question is the same: how much of Kaptur's support is personal and how much is the district's presidential partisanship.

## Candidates

### Marcy Kaptur (D)

Kaptur, 80, was first elected in 1982 and took office in 1983. She grew up in a union household in Toledo and worked in urban planning before Congress. A senior Appropriations member, she has built her record around manufacturing jobs, industrial investment, Great Lakes protection, infrastructure and federal resources for northwest Ohio. She is long skeptical of trade deals she says have hurt domestic industry, and is a prominent congressional backer of Ukraine.

### Derek Merrin (R)

Merrin, 40, is a real estate investor who served in the Ohio House from 2016 through 2024, chaired its Ways and Means Committee, and was previously mayor of Waterville. He campaigns on lower taxes, less regulation, fiscal restraint, energy development, border enforcement and growth. In an October 7 interview with 13ABC he named cutting the cost of living through tax cuts and deregulation as his top priority. He has defended the administration's tariff approach while stressing domestic competitiveness.

### Matthew Althaus (L)

The Libertarian nominee. Since 2024 was decided by under a point, his votes could matter, though there's no reliable polling of his support.

## Issues and controversies

**Manufacturing, jobs and tariffs.** Both stress northwest Ohio's economic strength. Kaptur points to industrial investment, labor, supply chains and federal infrastructure and regional-development money. Merrin favors lower taxes, less regulation and private investment, and says her long tenure hasn't delivered enough.

**Data centers and electricity costs.** Kaptur questions whether big data-center projects raise electricity and water costs for existing customers while creating few permanent jobs. She and the DCCC attack Merrin's legislative record, including data-center tax incentives and a vote tied to the FirstEnergy bailout legislation. Merrin and other backers stress investment, jobs and growth. The dispute is over how costs, benefits and infrastructure obligations are shared.

**Abortion.** Kaptur supports abortion rights and opposes a national ban. Merrin identifies as anti-abortion and backed restrictive legislation in the legislature.

**Kaptur's transgender-sports ad.** In October Kaptur released an ad backing restrictions on transgender participation in girls' school sports. LGBTQ+ advocacy groups and some Democratic officials criticized it. It shows how a long-serving Democrat in a Republican-leaning district can part ways with the national party.

## Latest developments

On October 19, House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries campaigned with Kaptur in Toledo, stressing the race's importance to control of the House. The event also highlighted her Ukraine support and her disagreement with Trump's recent decision to waive certain sanctions on Russia. Kaptur has been recovering from a recent car accident, which has affected her schedule, though she has kept campaigning and working in Congress.

## Why it matters nationally

- **The history.** The longest-serving woman in House history, whose career spans more than four decades of changing political environments.
- **The industrial Midwest.** A case study in union-oriented communities where Republican presidential candidates have gained, with Kaptur as the counterexample.
- **Redistricting.** Repeatedly redrawn, most recently with strongly Republican rural counties added, so the race shows how maps interact with incumbency and local identity.
- **House control.** One of the Democratic-held seats Republicans are actively contesting, alongside [OH-7](../oh-07/) and OH-13 (Emilia Sykes) in Ohio, as the House is narrowly divided.
