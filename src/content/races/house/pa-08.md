Pennsylvania's 8th District, centered on Scranton and Wilkes-Barre in northeastern Pennsylvania, is one of the country's closely watched House battlegrounds. It mixes historically Democratic industrial communities with rural and suburban areas that have shifted Republican. Republican incumbent Rob Bresnahan faces Scranton Mayor Paige Cognetti, a Democrat.

Bresnahan narrowly flipped the seat in 2024, beating longtime Democrat Matt Cartwright, though Trump carried the district by a larger margin. 2026 tests whether the Republican congressional gain lasts without Trump on the ballot. Cook rates it a Toss Up (PVI R+4) and notes questions about Bresnahan's stock transactions as a recurring campaign issue.

## The district

PA-8 covers Lackawanna, Pike and Wayne counties and parts of Luzerne and Monroe counties, with Scranton, Wilkes-Barre and East Stroudsburg as its main centers.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Scranton / Lackawanna | Historically Democratic; healthcare, education, logistics, labor heritage |
| Wilkes-Barre / Luzerne | Former coal and industrial towns; logistics; big Republican gains in presidential races |
| Monroe County / Poconos | Tourism, residential development, New York and New Jersey commuters |
| Pike and Wayne | Rural and exurban; recreation, tourism, cross-state commuting |

This is anthracite country, where coal shaped the economy, settlement, the labor movement and political identity. More recently: manufacturing decline, warehouse and distribution growth, healthcare jobs and Poconos housing. The big political change is formerly Democratic, working-class communities moving toward Republican presidential candidates, even as local Democrats keep support in some of them. That presidential versus down-ballot split is central to PA-8. The boundaries are unchanged from the 2022 map, so 2024-to-2026 comparisons are more straightforward than in redrawn states.

## Candidates

### Rob Bresnahan (R)

Bresnahan was first elected in 2024, one of the Republicans who flipped a Democratic House seat that year. He stresses lower taxes, economic development, domestic energy, border security and support for local businesses and workers. He backed the 2025 Republican tax and spending law, arguing tax relief and investment incentives sustain jobs and growth.

### Paige Cognetti (D)

Cognetti has focused her mayoralty on economic redevelopment, infrastructure and local government administration. Her campaign stresses affordability, healthcare, jobs and accountability, with congressional ethics front and center: limits on stock trading, corporate political influence and lobbying. She argues federal policy should do more about the immediate cost pressures on working households. Neither candidate had a contested May primary.

| Candidate | Vote | Votes |

The House margin was R+1.6 (6,252 votes) against Trump +8.4 in the same district. Cartwright's crossover appeal after twelve years in Congress held up better than the Democratic presidential ticket; Bresnahan won, but by far less than Trump.

## Stock trading and ethics

The campaign's most distinctive controversy, per an October 15 Spotlight PA investigation and candidate comparison:

- Bresnahan reported nearly 650 individual stock trades in 2025.
- His disclosures included trades around Trump's April 2025 tariff announcements.
- He sold up to $130,000 in shares of four Medicaid managed-care companies before voting for the 2025 Republican tax and spending law, which cut Medicaid spending.
- He says a financial adviser made the trades and that he has since stopped trading.

These are documented transactions and statements, not evidence that he traded on nonpublic information or broke the law.

Both candidates back limits on congressional stock trading; Bresnahan has sponsored or supported legislation, and Cognetti supports a ban and pledges not to trade. They differ on other reforms:

| Issue | Bresnahan (R) | Cognetti (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stock trading | Supports restrictions; has sponsored legislation | Supports a ban; pledges not to trade |
| Term limits | Three-term House limit | 18-year House limit |
| Corporate PAC money | Accepts it | Proposes banning it |
| Ex-members as lobbyists | No public position found | Permanent ban |

Spotlight PA also noted that although Cognetti says she rejects PAC influence, her campaign has taken money from political committees funded partly by corporate PACs.

## The economy

| Issue | Bresnahan (R) | Cognetti (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes | Backed the 2025 Republican law | Opposes parts of it; proposes a working-families tax credit |
| Tariffs | Generally supports Trump's policy, acknowledging costs | Opposes broad tariffs on allies; backs targeted industry protections |
| Healthcare | Backed the budget law but voted to extend enhanced ACA subsidies | Restore ACA subsidies; reverse healthcare cuts |
| Unions | Supported selected bipartisan pro-union bills | Supports the PRO Act and expanded bargaining protections |
| Manufacturing | Technical training, apprenticeships | Reshoring and apprenticeships |
| Iran war | Opposed a House resolution to end it | Supports ending it; criticizes its economic costs |

There's more overlap than the rhetoric suggests. Bresnahan has sometimes broken with his party, voting to restore federal employees' collective-bargaining rights and to extend enhanced ACA subsidies. Both back apprenticeships and workforce training in a region shaped by coal, manufacturing and organized labor; the differences are over taxes, spending, trade and the government's role in protecting workers.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A recent flip.** Unlike [OH-9](../oh-09/) and [ME-2](../me-02/), where Democrats held seats despite Republican presidential voting, PA-8 actually flipped Republican in 2024.
- **A mayor, not a federal recruit.** Cognetti runs on her Scranton record.
- **Working-class realignment.** The history resembles parts of northeastern Ohio and eastern Iowa, where Democratic labor communities moved Republican nationally.
- **Ethics.** The stock-trading issue adds a candidate-specific dimension beyond the national environment.
- **House control.** PA-8 is one of Pennsylvania's closely watched Republican-held seats, alongside [PA-1](../pa-01/), [PA-7](../pa-07/) and [PA-10](../pa-10/).
