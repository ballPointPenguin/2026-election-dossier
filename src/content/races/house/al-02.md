Alabama's 2nd District is one of the most consequential, and legally unusual, House contests of 2026. Democratic Rep. Shomari Figures, elected in 2024 from a district designed to give Black voters an opportunity to elect their preferred candidate, is defending his seat under dramatically different boundaries. His opponent is Republican state Rep. Rhett Marques of Enterprise.

The new district would have voted for Trump by about 14.3 points in 2024. Even so, recent surveys have the candidates within two points, and Sabato's Crystal Ball moved the race to Toss Up in late October and to Lean Democratic in its final ratings on Nov. 2.

| Rhett Marques | Republican | Alabama House since 2018; Enterprise business owner (formerly auto repair); former city councilman; Trump-endorsed |

## The district

The new AL-2 combines Montgomery with a broad swath of southeastern Alabama: Dothan, Enterprise, Troy, Eufaula and rural eastern Black Belt communities. It holds all of Lowndes, Montgomery, Macon, Russell, Bullock, Barbour, Pike, Crenshaw, Butler, Coffee, Dale, Henry, Houston and Geneva counties and parts of Covington and Elmore counties.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Montgomery | State government, education, healthcare, civil-rights history, large Black electorate |
| Eastern Black Belt | Historically Black rural communities, agriculture, poverty, rural healthcare challenges |
| Dothan and the Wiregrass | Agriculture, healthcare, retail, manufacturing, regional commerce |
| Enterprise and Dale County | Fort Novosel, military aviation, defense jobs, military families |
| Troy and surrounding counties | Higher education, agriculture, small-town economies |

Heavily Democratic urban and Black Belt communities sit with strongly Republican rural and suburban areas.

## The redistricting story

- In 2023, the Supreme Court's decision in *Allen v. Milligan* upheld a Voting Rights Act challenge to Alabama's congressional map, and federal courts then implemented a map with a second district where Black voters had a real opportunity to elect their preferred representative. It ran from Montgomery toward Mobile and the Gulf Coast, and Figures won it in 2024 with about 54.6%, becoming Alabama's second Black House member alongside Terri Sewell.
- In 2026, following *Louisiana v. Callais*, which per the source substantially weakened Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act as applied to race-conscious congressional districting, Alabama was allowed to return to a previously rejected map. The legislature adopted new boundaries in May 2026, replacing much of the Mobile-oriented district with territory in the more Republican Wiregrass.

The dispute isn't merely conventional: it involves a major reversal in federal voting-rights jurisprudence, a district created as a remedy for racial vote dilution, and a new map imposed while its first representative is still in his first term.

## Candidates

### Shomari Figures (D)

His mother, Vivian Figures, has also served in the Alabama Senate. His campaign stresses economic opportunity, rural healthcare, infrastructure, education and affordable childcare: keeping rural hospitals open, mental-health access, broadband, workforce development and jobs for underserved communities. He supports reproductive rights and has opposed the Trump administration's immigration and healthcare policies. He criticizes the Iran war, linking its costs to gasoline and fertilizer prices, opposed the 2025 tax and spending law over cuts to social programs, and has raised concerns about large data centers where communities object over electricity, water or infrastructure demands.

### Rhett Marques (R)

He represents the Wiregrass, which carries much more weight in the new district. He stresses lower taxes, less government spending, small-business development, immigration enforcement, gun rights and abortion restrictions, and supports Trump's trade policies and mass-deportation approach. He frames the election as a referendum on Congress's national direction, arguing a Republican would back Trump's agenda and oppose Democratic efforts to reverse it.

### The August primary

Because of the midyear redistricting, Alabama held special congressional primaries on August 11. Marques won the Republican nomination outright with 50.3%, just over the majority needed to avoid a runoff; Figures had no contested primary. The compressed calendar left both parties little time to campaign across the new district.

## The issues

**Healthcare and Medicaid.** Rural healthcare is central: smaller communities struggle to keep hospitals, attract professionals and offer specialty care. Figures opposed the 2025 law, saying its Medicaid and other changes would damage rural access; Marques backs its tax cuts and argues lower taxes, restrained spending and private growth help household finances.

**Affordability, farming and the Iran war.** The Wiregrass grows peanuts, cotton, poultry and other commodities. Figures ties the Iran war to higher fuel and fertilizer costs and criticizes tariffs that raise costs for farmers and consumers; Marques supports Trump's trade strategy, lower taxes, deregulation and small-business growth.

**Immigration and social policy.** Marques backs the administration's enforcement, including mass deportations, and abortion restrictions. Figures supports reproductive rights and criticizes federal enforcement practices.

**Infrastructure and data centers.** Figures stresses broadband, schools, workforce training and federal infrastructure investment, and worries about data centers in communities that may lack water or power. Marques stresses regulatory relief, business development and lower government costs.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A consequence of voting-rights jurisprudence.** The change follows litigation that altered the treatment of race-conscious redistricting, so the election matters beyond Alabama.
- **An incumbent in a very different district.** Figures won under the 2024 boundaries; the 2026 electorate adds much more Republican territory.
- **A rare competitive Alabama House race.** Most of the state's districts have strong partisan patterns, and AL-2 is drawing intense national investment.
- **Black Belt–Wiregrass.** Communities with different demographics, economic histories and voting patterns share a district.
- **A short record.** Figures has served less than one full term, so it's a referendum on his early service and on the new map.
- **Money.** Nearly $16M in outside spending in a district several analysts first treated as much less competitive.
- **A southern test.** It shows how national conditions play where Republican presidential support is far above the national average.

## What to watch on election night

- **Montgomery and the eastern Black Belt.** Turnout and margins in Montgomery, Lowndes, Macon, Bullock and nearby counties show participation in the heavily Democratic communities.
- **Dothan, Enterprise and the Wiregrass.** Houston, Coffee, Dale, Geneva and neighboring counties hold substantial Republican constituencies whose totals and margins matter to the count.
- **Troy, Eufaula and smaller centers.** Pike, Barbour, Russell and neighbors mix urban, small-town and rural populations, another measure of turnout across the newly assembled district.

The result won't resolve the broader legal debate over minority representation and redistricting.
