Arizona's 6th District is a Republican-held seat in southeastern Arizona, stretching from the Tucson area into rural communities near the Mexican border. By recent presidential voting it's one of the country's most closely divided districts.

Republican incumbent Juan Ciscomani faces Democrat JoAnna Mendoza, a retired Marine Corps drill instructor and Navy veteran. Gary Swing (Green) and Jereme Peters (Libertarian) are also on the ballot. It features two earlier Ciscomani wins over the same Democratic opponent and a new nominee whose military background changes the character of the contest.

## The district

AZ-6 covers parts of Pima, Pinal, Cochise, Graham and Greenlee counties, including much of eastern Tucson and communities toward the border and the state's eastern mountains.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Eastern Tucson and suburbs | Healthcare, University of Arizona-related jobs, technology, retirees |
| Oro Valley and nearby | Suburbs, medical technology, professional services |
| Casa Grande and Pinal portions | Manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, fast residential growth |
| Cochise County and Sierra Vista | Fort Huachuca, defense and intelligence jobs, border activity |
| Graham and Greenlee counties | Mining, agriculture, ranching, public lands, small communities |

Affluent Tucson-area suburbs join more rural and working-class communities, with military installations, mining, agriculture, tourism, manufacturing and retirement services. Border security is a direct local issue, as are water, public lands, healthcare access and economic ties to Mexico.

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Ciscomani R +1.5 (over Kirsten Engel) |
| 2024 House | Ciscomani R +2.5 (over Engel again; about 50.0%) |
| 2024 president | Trump R +0.7 |

Biden won it by under a point in 2020, and Cook's PVI is R+0. The district has also gone Democratic in statewide races: Ruben Gallego carried it for Senate in 2024, and Mark Kelly and Katie Hobbs carried it in 2022. That makes it a case of candidate-specific, split-ticket voting.

## Candidates

### Juan Ciscomani (R)

He stresses tax cuts, border security, economic development, water infrastructure and veterans' services. KJZZ notes that the nonpartisan Bridge Grades rated him among Arizona's more bipartisan members of Congress; critics point to his support for the 2025 Republican tax and spending law as alignment with national Republican priorities.

### JoAnna Mendoza (D)

She stresses working-family affordability, veterans' services, healthcare, labor rights and border security. Her economic proposals include expanding the Child Tax Credit, preserving tax relief for tips and overtime, and the PRO Act. She has faced criticism over earlier statements about redirecting some law-enforcement funding and over removing past social-media posts, and emphasizes her military service and support for border security now.

Both have personal ties to immigrant or working-class communities in southern Arizona but differ greatly in political experience.

### Third parties

Swing (Green) and Peters (Libertarian) are on the ballot; Michael Dorland is a write-in. In 2024 Green candidate Athena Eastwood got about 2.5% when Ciscomani won with 50.0%, which doesn't show how Swing or Peters will do.

## The issues

The candidates debated on September 10 in an Arizona Clean Elections debate (economy, healthcare, immigration, veterans, water rights, election integrity), and Arizona Public Media fact-checked claims by both.

**Healthcare and the 2025 law.** Ciscomani voted for it; he says its Medicaid changes strengthen program integrity, cut fraud and preserve help for those who qualify. Mendoza says they'll cut coverage and strain hospitals, especially rural ones. Arizona's Medicaid program, AHCCCS, is a major source of coverage and hospital funding. The Arizona Medical Association says the law will cut the state's Medicaid provider-tax rate over time; the federal Rural Health Transformation Program adds funding, but healthcare groups dispute whether it can offset the broader reductions.

**Immigration and the border.** Both name border security a priority. Ciscomani stresses barriers, federal enforcement and stronger controls; Mendoza backs border security while arguing for policies that recognize border communities and long-term residents. He's an immigrant who became a citizen; she grew up in a farmworker family.

**Taxes and wages.** Ciscomani wants to extend Republican tax cuts and lighten regulation; Mendoza backs targeted help (the Child Tax Credit, tips and overtime relief), changes to provisions benefiting high earners, and stronger union rights.

**Water.** Groundwater, agriculture, development and climate variability create long-term challenges; both discuss water security and differ on federal infrastructure spending, environmental regulation and government's role.

**Veterans and installations.** Fort Huachuca and Sierra Vista give veterans' services, military funding and national security local weight. Ciscomani stresses veterans' services and appropriations; Mendoza draws on a long military career and veteran-transition advocacy.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Equal presidential support.** Trump won by under a point in 2024; Biden won narrowly in 2020.
- **A new challenger after two repeat contests.** Mendoza brings a different background and organization than Engel.
- **Border-region biographies.** Both are defined by immigration, military service and southern Arizona.
- **A bipartisan reputation alongside major Republican votes.** Voters get a concrete record to weigh.
- **Spending.** The candidates raised nearly $19M with about $19.5M more in outside spending.
- **Compare [AZ-1](../az-01/).** Both narrowly voted Trump; AZ-1 is an open Scottsdale–Phoenix seat, AZ-6 has an incumbent and spans Tucson suburbs, military communities and the borderlands.
