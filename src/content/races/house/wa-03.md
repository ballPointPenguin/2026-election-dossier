Washington's 3rd District is one of the country's notable Democratic-held districts that voted for Trump in 2024. It covers southwestern Washington, including Vancouver, communities along the Columbia River, and rural and coastal counties to the north and west.

Democratic incumbent Marie Gluesenkamp Pérez faces Republican John Braun, minority leader of the Washington State Senate. Unlike many races in this dossier, this is a Democratic-held seat Republicans are contesting, with an incumbent who has won twice in an electorate that voted Republican for president.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marie Gluesenkamp Pérez | Democrat | Incumbent since 2023; small-business owner (auto repair); Blue Dog Coalition |
| John Braun | Republican | State senator since 2013 and Senate Republican leader; retired Navy captain; owns Braun Northwest, which builds emergency vehicles; Centralia |

## The candidates

Gluesenkamp Pérez ran an auto-repair shop with her husband before Congress and emphasizes vocational education, skilled trades, rural communities and political independence. Braun, a manufacturing executive and longtime legislator, has worked on budgets, taxation, business policy and education. Both have substantial experience outside electoral politics and talk about practical economic concerns, not national ideological labels.

## The district

WA-3 includes Clark, Cowlitz, Lewis, Pacific, Wahkiakum and Skamania counties and a small part of Thurston County.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Vancouver / Clark County | Portland-area suburbs, healthcare, technology, manufacturing, commuting |
| Longview / Cowlitz County | Timber, paper, ports, industry |
| Centralia / Chehalis / Lewis County | Manufacturing, logistics, farming, rural communities |
| Pacific and Wahkiakum | Fishing, forestry, tourism, small coastal towns |
| Skamania County | Columbia River Gorge; recreation, forestry, conservation |

Vancouver has grown fast and integrates with Portland, while many rural communities lean Republican. The economics also differ from suburban swing seats: timber, fisheries, hydropower, ports, skilled trades and federal land management are especially relevant.

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Pérez D +0.8 (over Joe Kent) |
| 2024 House | Pérez D +3.8 (over Kent) |
| 2024 president | Trump R +3.3 |

Pérez first won by defeating Kent, who had beaten Republican incumbent Jaime Herrera Beutler in the top-two primary, and beat him again in 2024. Braun is a different kind of opponent, with a long state-government record, whereas Kent's campaigns were marked by disputes over his associations and statements about the 2020 election.

## The August primary

Washington uses a top-two primary: everyone appears on one ballot and the two highest vote-getters advance regardless of party.

| Candidate | Party | Vote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| John Braun | Republican | 39.7% |
| Marie Gluesenkamp Pérez | Democrat | 36.4% |
| Brent Hennrich | Democrat | 16.7% |
| Others | Various | 7.2% |

Pérez finished second individually even though Democratic candidates together got about 55.9% versus roughly 42% for Republicans. The primary also showed a split: Hennrich ran to her left, criticizing her bipartisan record and positions on immigration and the Trump administration, and after losing said he wouldn't support her in November. That complicates reading the primary as a pure partisan measure, and raises questions about how progressives, moderate Democrats and independents will treat her record.

## Money

| Candidate | Raised | Cash on hand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gluesenkamp Pérez | $9.47M | $235K |
| Braun | $3.25M | $672K |

Through October 14, 2026. Pérez has raised nearly three times as much; Braun has more cash. An October 21 Washington State Standard investigation (republished by OPB) found combined campaign and outside spending already exceeded $20 million, with Republican-aligned groups including the Congressional Leadership Fund and the Trump-aligned No Going Back PAC spending especially heavily and Democratic-aligned House Majority PAC spending millions against Braun. Some trackers show much lower outside totals because they haven't fully incorporated late 24- and 48-hour FEC notices, so the local reporting is the better guide to scale.

## The issues

**A Democrat who breaks with her party.** Pérez stresses bipartisan cooperation, local manufacturing and repair businesses, vocational education and opposing polarization, and has departed from Democratic leadership on immigration and other issues. Supporters say that reflects a diverse district; progressive critics say some positions undercut Democratic priorities. Braun says voters should judge her actual votes and the effects of federal policy, not her rhetoric about independence.

**Taxes and affordability.** Braun supports the 2025 Republican tax law, stressing its provisions on tips, overtime, seniors and the child tax credit, plus less regulation and lower energy costs. Pérez criticizes parts of federal tax and spending policy and stresses affordability for working families and small businesses. Both campaign heavily on cost of living and differ on tariffs, federal spending and economic development.

**Immigration.** Tricky for Pérez, who has backed some enforcement measures other Democrats oppose. Braun stresses enforcement and restrictions; she argues for border security combined with practical immigration policy.

**Natural resources.** Forest management, wildfire prevention, salmon recovery, Columbia River infrastructure and federal land rules affect communities throughout. Braun brings state budgeting and manufacturing; Pérez, small business and federal legislation.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A Democratic-held Trump district.** The split result makes it a study in how candidate identity and local issues shape congressional voting.
- **A new Republican opponent.** Braun's state record changes the comparison with the two earlier Kent races.
- **Pressure from her left.** Hennrich's 16.7% and his refusal to endorse her are concrete evidence of intra-party disagreement, though they don't show how those voters will behave in November.
- **National money.** Both parties' groups have invested heavily.
- **Compare [WI-1](../wi-01/).** Both have industrial communities, rural areas and ticket-splitters, but in WI-1 a Republican defends a Trump-won seat and in WA-3 a Democrat does.

## What to watch

Turnout in Clark County versus the rural counties, whether supporters of other Democrats in the primary consolidate behind Pérez, and whether late advertising changes voters' priorities.
