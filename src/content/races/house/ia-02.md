Iowa's 2nd District is an open Republican-held seat across much of northeastern Iowa, including Cedar Rapids, Waterloo and Dubuque. Republican Ashley Hinson is leaving to run for the U.S. Senate. The nominees to replace her are Republican Joe Mitchell and Democrat Lindsay James, with independent Dave Bushaw and Libertarian Rick Stewart also on the ballot.

The district has voted Republican in recent federal elections but includes several historically Democratic industrial and university communities. Unlike the redistricting-driven contests in Texas and Florida, this one is about an open seat, changing working-class voting patterns and whether Democrats can win back northeastern Iowa.

## The district

IA-2 covers all or parts of 22 counties, with its biggest population in Linn and Black Hawk counties. Its main communities are Cedar Rapids, Waterloo, Cedar Falls, Dubuque and surrounding rural counties.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Cedar Rapids / Linn County | Manufacturing, food processing, healthcare, professional jobs |
| Waterloo / Cedar Falls | Industrial and farm-equipment manufacturing, university jobs |
| Dubuque | Mississippi River commerce, manufacturing, healthcare, higher education |
| Rural northeastern Iowa | Corn, soybeans, livestock, small manufacturing, small towns |

Democrat Dave Loebsack represented much of this region for years, but Republicans have made big gains among rural and blue-collar voters along the Mississippi, a trend Cook calls central to recent voting.

## Candidates

### Joe Mitchell (R)

Mitchell was first elected to the Iowa House from Clear Lake at 21 and served two terms. He later worked as a HUD regional director in the Trump administration and in homebuilding. Trump endorsed him in the primary, which he won over state Sen. Charlie McClintock, about 61% to 38%, in June. His campaign emphasizes manufacturing, skilled trades, rural economic development and keeping workers in Iowa; Trump's trade approach and stronger border enforcement; year-round E15 gasoline (key for corn and ethanol); and term limits, a congressional stock-trading ban and less corporate PAC influence. He calls his economics more worker-focused than traditional Republican policy and says past free-trade deals hurt manufacturing workers.

### Lindsay James (D)

James, a four-term state representative from Dubuque, minister, chaplain and mother, won the June primary with about 58% over Cedar Rapids nonprofit leader Clint Twedt-Ball and retired Army nurse Kathy Dolter. Her platform: lower household costs (healthcare, housing, childcare), protecting Medicaid and expanding access, lowering the Medicare age to 55, immigration reform pairing border security with a path to citizenship, and anti-corruption measures. In an October 18 AP interview she said national Democrats neglected the Midwest and stressed campaigning across the whole district. She talks often about faith and public service, a different face of Democratic politics for a region with many socially moderate or conservative voters.

### Others

Bushaw (independent) and Stewart (Libertarian) round out the four-candidate ballot. In a close plurality race they could matter, but polling doesn't show how much support either has.

## The October debates

On October 1 and 8, both candidates stressed economic issues for working families.

| Issue | Mitchell (R) | James (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tariffs | Defends Trump's shift from traditional free trade | Roll back tariffs that raise costs |
| Manufacturing | Protect domestic production and skilled trades | Industrial jobs, workers, household purchasing power |
| Social Security | Prevent fraud and improper payments | Lift the payroll-tax income cap |
| Deficit | Supports a balanced-budget amendment | Also supports a balanced-budget amendment |
| Healthcare | Costs, rural access, economic policy | Expand access; oppose Medicaid cuts |
| Ethics | Term limits; stock-trading limits | Accountability; less corruption |

On tariffs, Mitchell says Trump's trade policy answers decades of disadvantage for American manufacturing workers; James says tariffs raise costs for consumers and businesses, including Iowa manufacturers and farmers. Both prize manufacturing jobs, so the dispute is over which policies protect workers best.

## Why it matters nationally

**Rural Democratic coalition.** An October 18 AP report on Democrats' bid to regain rural Iowa, partly in IA-2, found frustration over farm costs, tariffs, fuel prices and the economy, along with continued Republican loyalty among some farmers despite dissatisfaction. IA-2 shows whether economic discontent changes votes or partisan identity wins out.

**Two versions of working-class politics.** Mitchell offers manufacturing, trade protection and economic nationalism inside the Republican coalition; James offers wages, healthcare, affordability and social programs while criticizing her party's Midwest record.

**Iowa's busy ballot.** An open Senate seat, a competitive governor's race and several House contests could interact through ads, mobilization and turnout, though strength in one race won't carry uniformly into the others.
