Wisconsin's 1st District, covering Racine, Kenosha, Janesville and part of the Milwaukee suburbs, is a Republican-held seat drawing far more national attention than it did earlier in the cycle. Republican incumbent Bryan Steil faces Democrat Mitchell Berman, an emergency-room nurse and former Veterans Affairs hospital employee.

The contrast: Republicans have held WI-1 since 1995, including 20 years under former Speaker Paul Ryan, but Trump carried it by only 4.5 points in 2024, and recent Democratic-sponsored polls show an extremely close race.

## The district

WI-1 includes all of Racine and Kenosha counties, parts of Rock County and a piece of Milwaukee County: older industrial cities, farm communities and Milwaukee-area suburbs.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Racine | Manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, diverse urban electorate |
| Kenosha | Industrial jobs, warehousing, commuters tied to Chicago |
| Janesville / Rock County | Auto-manufacturing history, healthcare, small industrial employers |
| Milwaukee County portion | Suburbs with distinct voting patterns |
| Rural communities | Farming, small manufacturing, commuters |

Janesville hosted a major General Motors assembly plant, and Racine and Kenosha have long manufacturing and labor traditions, so trade policy and household costs are tied closely to politics here, as in [MI-10](../mi-10/) and [PA-8](../pa-08/).

Steil ran well ahead of Trump, which raises the central 2026 question: how do voters separate their views of national politics from their evaluation of Steil's record?

## Candidates

### Bryan Steil (R)

Steil worked for Paul Ryan and served on the University of Wisconsin Board of Regents before winning in 2018. He chairs the House Administration Committee, which oversees federal election administration, and the Financial Services subcommittee on digital assets, fintech and AI. His campaign stresses tax relief, energy, immigration enforcement, growth and fiscal restraint. He has backed tighter federal voter-registration rules, including the SAVE Act requiring documentary proof of citizenship; supporters call it election integrity, opponents say documentation could create barriers for eligible voters.

### Mitchell Berman (D)

Berman grew up in Peshtigo and lives in Raymond. He worked 11 years as an ER nurse at VA hospitals in Milwaukee and North Chicago, then at an Ascension hospital, and resigned from his federal VA job because of limits on partisan activity by federal employees. He campaigns on healthcare affordability, workers' economic security, immigration reform and household costs, and supports extending enhanced ACA subsidies, fully funding Medicaid and a public option. He won the August 11 primary over Miguel Aranda and Lorenzo Santos.

## Money

Through September 30, 2026, per FEC. Steil has nearly four times Berman's receipts and over twelve times his cash. Outside groups reported about $7.08 million in independent expenditures through October 16 (later filings are still arriving): $2.5M supporting Steil, $2.1M opposing Berman, $865K supporting Berman and $1.6M opposing Steil.

**Crypto money.** The Fairshake super PAC has reported about $1 million supporting Steil, whose committee work includes digital-asset regulation. It shows an industry's interest, not improper coordination or conduct.

**Contrast with WI-3.** In [WI-3](../wi-03/), Democrat Rebecca Cooke has outraised Republican Derrick Van Orden; in WI-1, the Republican incumbent has the large money advantage.

## The October 20 debate

Their only scheduled face-to-face debate was on October 20, hosted by Janesville radio station WCLO. Both named the cost of living as a top concern.

| Issue | Steil (R) | Berman (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordability | Tax relief; more domestic energy | Repeal broad tariffs, which he says raise prices |
| Healthcare | Medicaid work requirements; limits on eligibility for undocumented immigrants | Fund Medicaid; restore enhanced ACA subsidies; public option |
| Immigration | Stronger enforcement; deport violent offenders; tie legal immigration to workforce needs | Comprehensive reform; faster green cards; path to citizenship |
| Federal debt | Debt commission; two-year budget cycle | Cut waste; tax higher earners more |
| Manufacturing | Business investment, taxes, energy costs | Household costs, workers, effects of tariffs |

**Healthcare.** Berman's nursing background drives his criticism of federal healthcare cuts, drawing on treating patients, including veterans. Steil defends the 2025 Republican tax and spending law and says work requirements and eligibility limits are appropriate. The fight is over who gets subsidized coverage, how much to spend, and whether competition or a public option lowers costs.

**Election administration.** Steil's chairmanship puts federal election rules at the center of his record. His SAVE Act support links the race to debates over citizenship verification, voter access and election security; these are federal legislative questions, not evidence of problems in Wisconsin's administration of the 2026 election.

**Data centers.** They came up at Berman's September town halls, as in WI-3, with concerns about electricity demand, infrastructure and utility costs.

## Why it matters nationally

- **The Ryan legacy.** Ryan held the seat from 1999 to 2019; its history is tied to establishment Republican economic policy, fiscal conservatism and entitlement debates.
- **House versus presidential results.** Steil's ten-point win far exceeded Trump's margin, which is why polls and history tell different stories.
- **A health professional against a finance-policy chair.** Emergency medicine and veterans' care against manufacturing, law and financial-services legislation.
- **Incumbent versus first-timer.** Steil has won four straight House elections; Berman is on his first run.
- **National support.** The DCCC added Berman to its Red to Blue program in September.

The key evidence points in different directions: close presidential results, larger past House wins, Democratic polls at or within a point of a tie, analysts moving toward competitive but disagreeing on how far, and a large Steil money advantage with outside groups spending on both sides.

## What to watch

New independent polling, late spending disclosures, and turnout in Racine, Kenosha, Janesville and the Milwaukee suburbs.
