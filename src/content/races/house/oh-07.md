Ohio's 7th District pits Republican incumbent Max Miller against Democrat Brian Poindexter, a union ironworker and former local councilman. Three things set the race apart: new 2026 boundaries, allegations against the incumbent that have drawn criticism from fellow Republicans, and a challenger running on his working-class background.

On the new lines Trump would have carried the district by about 11 points in 2024, and it's normally Republican territory. But summer polling showed strong support for Poindexter, all three major forecasters rate it a Toss Up, and both parties have committed major advertising money.

## The district

OH-7 is a transition zone between metropolitan Cleveland and more rural north-central Ohio. Under the map adopted in October 2025 it covers parts of Cuyahoga, Medina and Wayne counties plus all of Ashland County, and no longer includes Holmes County. Communities include Parma and Brunswick, the city of Medina and the Wooster area. That mixes Cleveland-area suburban households, manufacturing and construction workers, small cities and farm communities, with concerns about healthcare, taxes, energy prices and regional economic development.

## Candidates

### Max Miller (R)

Miller has been in Congress since January 2023. He worked in the first Trump administration and is closely tied to Trump's political operation. His record stresses border security, tax cuts, deregulation, support for Israel and Republican economic policy. Trump still endorses him, though his relations with other Republican officeholders are now a main part of the story.

### Brian Poindexter (D)

Poindexter is a union ironworker and former local councilman who leans on the building trades and the pressures on working households: wages, organized labor, healthcare costs, manufacturing jobs, and the effects of tariffs and higher fuel prices. He also makes government accountability, and the allegations against Miller, prominent.

Two independent write-in candidates, Thahbia Asad and Andrey Martinichin, are listed in election reporting but are not on the printed ballot.

## The allegations against Miller

This is the most consequential candidate-specific development, so it needs care about what is alleged, under investigation and established.

In July, Miller's former wife, Emily Moreno, accused him of domestic abuse involving her and their young daughter, and former Trump White House press secretary Stephanie Grisham made her own allegations of physical abuse. Miller denies them, calls them false claims from a contentious family dispute, and has sued Moreno for defamation. According to October 17 reporting, local police and child-welfare investigations did not establish who caused an injury to Miller's daughter, and a prosecutor declined to file charges for insufficient evidence. The House Ethics Committee has opened a separate investigation, which is not a finding of wrongdoing.

Political fallout so far:

- Republican Sens. Bernie Moreno and Jon Husted publicly called for Miller to resign.
- Trump continues to support him.
- Poindexter has used the allegations in ads.
- Miller released an October ad on the effects of divorce on families, denying abuse and criticizing his opponent's use of the allegations.

In August Cook moved the race from Likely Republican to Toss Up, citing Miller's decision to stay in despite the allegations. That makes OH-7 unlike a race whose rating shifts mainly on the national environment. How voters will react is uncertain: Ideastream spoke with Republicans who were troubled by the allegations but wouldn't back Poindexter, including one considering leaving the race blank.

## Why it matters nationally

**Party versus nominee.** Do voters separate loyalty to a party from their view of its candidate? Public allegations, Republican criticism of Miller and a continuing Trump endorsement make this unusually tangled.

**Working-class economics.** Poindexter's ironworker background is central in a district of industrial suburbs and small towns, where wages, construction, tariffs, fuel prices and manufacturing jobs matter locally. AP reporting on October 19 found voters raising both the allegations and the broader economy.

**Redistricting and the national mood.** The lines changed for 2026, and national frustration over inflation and the Iran war also shapes the midterms, so any change from 2024 is hard to pin on one cause.

**House control.** OH-7 is one of several Ohio seats drawing national attention, along with Marcy Kaptur's [OH-9](../oh-09/), Emilia Sykes's OH-13 and [OH-15](../oh-15/).
