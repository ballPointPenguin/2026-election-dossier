South Carolina's 1st District runs along the Atlantic coast from the Charleston area toward Hilton Head. It's an open seat: Nancy Mace, who won in 2022 and 2024 (58.2% last time), ran for governor and did not win. Republican Jenny Costa Honeycutt, a Charleston County councilwoman and attorney, faces Democrat Nancy Lacore, a retired Navy vice admiral whom Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed from her post in 2025. Trump carried the district by about 13 points in 2024, and two early-October polls disagreed sharply on the congressional race.

## The district

SC-1 covers much of the coastal Lowcountry: Charleston, Mount Pleasant, James Island, Beaufort, Hilton Head, and areas toward Georgetown. It mixes affluent coastal suburbs, fast-growing developments, military communities, historic urban neighborhoods and rural coastal areas.

- **Growth and affordability:** population boom, housing prices, congestion, land use.
- **Coastal resilience:** flooding, hurricanes, sea-level rise, wetlands, insurance.
- **Military and veterans:** Joint Base Charleston and Marine Corps Recruit Depot Parris Island.
- **Healthcare:** hospital access, Medicaid, insurance costs.
- **Tourism and commerce:** hospitality, the port, small business.

The district has crossed party lines before: Democrat Joe Cunningham beat Republican Katie Arrington in 2018, the first Democrat to hold the seat in nearly four decades, then lost to Mace in 2020. Boundaries and conditions have changed since.

## Candidates

### Jenny Costa Honeycutt (R)

A lifelong Lowcountry resident, attorney and small-business owner, Honeycutt has been on Charleston County Council since 2018. She won a crowded primary, beat state Rep. Mark Smith in the June 23 runoff, and got Trump's endorsement. Her campaign stresses fiscal conservatism, border security, public safety, economic development and protecting Lowcountry character. In the October 7 debate she contrasted her lifelong residency with Lacore's recent move to South Carolina, and she has tried to carve out an identity apart from Mace's loud, confrontational national style.

### Nancy Lacore (D)

Lacore spent about 35 years in the Navy, starting as a helicopter pilot and rising to three-star admiral and chief of the Navy Reserve. Hegseth removed her in 2025 without a public explanation; she retired and entered politics. A former Republican, she presents as a moderate Democrat focused on affordability, healthcare, military service and bipartisanship. Her background is central to her campaign amid the ongoing U.S. war with Iran and debates over civilian control of the military. She moved to Mount Pleasant fairly recently, which Honeycutt has pressed; Lacore answers with her leadership record and family ties to the region.

### Bill Reeside Jr. (L)

Libertarian, on the general-election ballot. His platform is fiscal restraint, less government spending and criminal justice reform, including drug-law changes. He got 4% in the early-October co/efficient poll.

## The issues

At the October 7 WCBD debate in Mount Pleasant:

| Issue | Honeycutt (R) | Lacore (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordability | Fiscal restraint, cutting waste, supporting business | Housing costs, wages, childcare, healthcare |
| Healthcare | Cost containment, prevention, accountability | Protecting Medicaid, restoring ACA subsidies, lowering drug costs |
| Immigration | Border security, enforcing immigration law | Border security, with more emphasis on due process |
| Abortion | Opposes, with exceptions for rape, incest and the mother's life | Supports reproductive rights |
| Congressional ethics | Term limits, stock-trading limits | Term limits, stock-trading limits |

**Development.** Democrats attack Honeycutt's local-government record and ties to developers, arguing it invites overdevelopment and favors business interests. She disputes that and points to her experience managing growth. The DCCC has folded these accusations into its advertising; they are campaign allegations, not established findings.

## Why it matters nationally

**Veteran Democrats.** Lacore is one of several Democrats with deep military résumés, including Jessica Killin in Colorado's [5th](../co-05/) and Leela Gray in [Florida's 13th](../fl-13/). Democrats are leaning on military leadership, the Iran war and the administration's treatment of senior officers.

**Coastal growth politics.** Charleston's boom raises issues that don't track party lines: infrastructure costs, traffic, environmental protection, property rights and the balance between tourism and residents.

**An open seat.** Two candidates are introducing themselves to a wider electorate, which also becomes a contest over which kind of public service voters value: local elected office or senior military leadership.
