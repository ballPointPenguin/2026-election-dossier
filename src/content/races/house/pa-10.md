Pennsylvania's 10th District, centered on Harrisburg and York, is another closely watched Republican-held seat. It's a rematch between Republican incumbent Scott Perry and Democrat Janelle Stelson, a former TV news anchor who came within about 1.2 points of beating him in 2024.

Perry has served since 2013 and is a prominent House Freedom Caucus figure tied to the disputes over the 2020 election. His narrow 2024 result made this one of Pennsylvania's tightest House races.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scott Perry | Republican | Incumbent since 2013; retired Pennsylvania Army National Guard brigadier general; former Freedom Caucus chair |
| Janelle Stelson | Democrat | Decades as a central Pennsylvania TV journalist (WGAL); first ran in 2024 |

## The district

PA-10 includes Dauphin County and parts of Cumberland and York counties: Harrisburg, York and growing suburbs.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Harrisburg / Dauphin | State government, healthcare, education, services |
| York County | Manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, growing suburbs |
| Cumberland County | Harrisburg suburbs, transportation and distribution, military-connected jobs |
| Smaller towns and rural areas | Agriculture, small manufacturing, regional commerce |

It joins Democratic-voting urban centers with more Republican suburban and rural areas. Harrisburg's state-government economy and York's manufacturing history give it a different feel from [PA-8](../pa-08/), though both have working-class and suburban electorates.

| Election | Republican | Democrat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Perry 53.8% | Shamaine Daniels 46.2% |
| 2024 House | Perry 50.6% | Stelson 49.4% |

Perry won in 2024 by about 5,100 votes. Ballotpedia reports Trump carried PA-10 by about 5.2 points in 2024, and Cook's PVI is R+3. The district leans Republican but less than some earlier figures suggested, and Perry ran well below the presidential vote. <!-- TODO: source first said Trump +9 / R+5, then corrected to +5.2 / R+3; confirm. -->

## Candidates

### Scott Perry (R)

A retired Army National Guard brigadier general and former business owner who served in the Pennsylvania House before Congress, Perry formerly chaired the Freedom Caucus. His record stresses limited federal government, tax cuts, immigration enforcement, military affairs and opposition to many federal regulations. His campaign emphasizes his experience, small business, and lower taxes and energy costs. He backed the 2025 Republican tax and spending law and defends the administration's tariffs as securing longer-term trade benefits.

### Janelle Stelson (D)

A familiar face on regional TV news, Stelson had never held elected office before challenging Perry in 2024. She stresses affordability, healthcare access, reproductive rights, accountability and congressional independence. She criticizes Perry's support for federal spending cuts affecting healthcare and his involvement in efforts to contest the 2020 election. She supports making enhanced ACA subsidies permanent and stronger campaign-finance disclosure and anti-corruption measures.

## The issues

| Issue | Perry (R) | Stelson (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes | Backed the 2017 and 2025 Republican tax laws | Middle-class relief; criticizes benefits to higher earners |
| Tariffs | Backs the administration, citing longer-term benefits | Opposes broad Trump tariffs; stresses prices |
| Healthcare | Opposed extending enhanced ACA subsidies; health savings accounts and interstate insurance purchasing | Permanent ACA subsidies; affordability |
| Iran war | Defends the operation; wants a swift end | End the war; criticizes economic costs |
| Campaign finance | Supports Citizens United; opposes donation caps | Stronger disclosure and FEC enforcement; reverse Citizens United |
| Stock trading | Supports restrictions on members' purchases | Supports a ban |

**Healthcare.** Perry voted against a three-year extension of the enhanced ACA premium subsidies, arguing the ACA hasn't delivered affordable care and proposing market-based reforms. Stelson says the subsidies' expiration has cost coverage and raised household costs. Spotlight PA reported Perry was the only vulnerable Pennsylvania Republican to oppose the extension.

**After the 2020 election.** Perry was involved in efforts to challenge the 2020 presidential result and backed replacing Justice Department leadership with officials more receptive to Trump's fraud claims; his conduct became part of federal investigations into attempts to overturn the election. Stelson repeatedly raises it as a question of accountability and democratic institutions. Perry defends his actions and rejects accusations of impropriety. Involvement in those events isn't a criminal conviction.

**The October 14 debate.** The only scheduled debate, hosted by ABC27 in Harrisburg. They clashed on the Iran war, gas and diesel prices, healthcare costs and election integrity. Perry argued Iran's threat required a response but said the conflict should end quickly, and proposed temporarily suspending federal gasoline taxes. Stelson called for ending the war and linked its effects to higher costs for households and farmers.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A narrow rematch.** Perry won by roughly 5,100 votes in 2024, and both campaigns know the other.
- **A prominent national record.** Freedom Caucus leadership and the post-2020 disputes bring presidential power, congressional accountability and the Republican Party's direction into the race.
- **Presidential versus House voting.** The district leans Republican but Perry ran well behind the top of the ticket.
- **Money.** Combined candidate receipts exceed $16M, with nearly $16M more in outside spending.
- **Compare [PA-8](../pa-08/) and [VA-1](../va-01/).** Both are Republican-held Pennsylvania seats with Democrats stressing affordability and accountability; Bresnahan is a freshman while Perry has a long record and a bigger role in national ideological fights.
