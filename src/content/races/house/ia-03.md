Iowa's 3rd District is a closely contested Republican-held seat covering Des Moines, its suburbs and much of south-central and southwestern Iowa. Republican incumbent Zach Nunn is seeking a third term against Democratic state Sen. Sarah Trone Garriott.

The district has changed hands several times since 2010. Nunn beat Democratic incumbent Cindy Axne by fewer than 2,200 votes in 2022, then won by about four points in 2024. Analysts have recently revised their ratings: Cook on October 9 and Inside Elections on October 1.

## The district

IA-3 covers 21 counties, from the Des Moines metro across southwestern and south-central Iowa.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Polk County / Des Moines | State government, finance, insurance, healthcare, higher education, urban neighborhoods |
| Dallas County | Fast-growing western suburbs (Waukee) |
| Warren County | Indianola, suburbs, exurbs, farms |
| Pottawattamie County | Council Bluffs; ties to the Omaha economy |
| Southern and southwestern rural counties | Agriculture, small manufacturing, small towns |

Nunn won every county except Polk in both 2022 and 2024, per KCCI's October analysis, so Polk's share of the vote, Dallas County's suburbs and margins in the small rural counties are the key places to watch.

Cook's PVI is R+2. Republicans have recently won both House and presidential contests here, but narrowly.

## Candidates

### Zach Nunn (R)

An Air Force Reserve colonel and former intelligence and cybersecurity professional, Nunn served in both chambers of the Iowa Legislature before Congress. He sits on Agriculture, Financial Services and the select China committee, with work on agricultural finance, fintech, national security and rural economics. He campaigns on tax cuts, border security, farm policy and federal investment in rural healthcare, and backed the 2025 Republican tax and spending law, highlighting its tax provisions and rural-health funding.

### Sarah Trone Garriott (D)

A Lutheran minister, she entered the Iowa Senate in 2021 and has served as minority whip. She emphasizes healthcare, affordability, education, rural communities and protecting social programs, and makes Nunn's vote for the 2025 law central, arguing Medicaid and other cuts could squeeze rural hospitals and low-income households. She became the nominee when state Rep. Jennifer Konfrst suspended her campaign, avoiding a long primary.

## The issues

**Medicaid and rural healthcare.** Per Iowa Public Radio, about 19% of the district is on Medicaid. Trone Garriott says the 2025 law reduces federal Medicaid spending and pressures rural providers; Nunn stresses its tax cuts and targeted rural-health funding. It's partly a dispute over weighing the law as a whole.

**Agriculture.** Commodity prices, exports, tariffs, ethanol and biofuels, and the finances of smaller farms. Nunn's Agriculture Committee seat gives him a legislative record; Trone Garriott stresses what federal policy does to rural families and communities.

**Affordability.** Nunn: tax relief, growth, less regulation. Trone Garriott: healthcare costs, housing, and who benefits from federal tax and spending choices.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Frequent turnover.** Per Iowa Public Radio, no incumbent has held IA-3 for more than two terms since 2010; Nunn is seeking a third.
- **Des Moines suburbs.** Growing Dallas County versus rural counties that vote reliably Republican.
- **Iowa's other races.** [IA-1](../ia-01/) is a Miller-Meeks–Bohannan rematch and [IA-2](../ia-02/) is an open seat; IA-3 is a second-term Republican against a sitting Democratic state senator.
- **A busy statewide ballot.** The Senate race (Ashley Hinson vs. Josh Turek) and governor's race (Zach Lahn vs. Rob Sand) share the ballot and may affect turnout and issues, though that doesn't tell how individuals will vote for the House.
- **Many unaffiliated voters.** KCCI reports about 168,000 active Republicans, 153,000 Democrats and 141,000 no-party voters in October, so the electorate can't be read from party registration alone.
