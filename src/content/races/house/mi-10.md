Michigan's 10th District is a competitive open Republican-held seat in the northern Detroit suburbs. John James is leaving Congress to run for governor, leaving Democrat Christina Hines, a former prosecutor, against Republican Michael "Mike" Bouchard Jr., an Army veteran and businessman.

It's a real battleground because of three features: recent close congressional elections, a presidential electorate that has shifted Republican, and no incumbent in 2026.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christina Hines | Democrat | Former Wayne and Washtenaw county prosecutor; law professor; lost 2024 Macomb prosecutor race |
| Michael Bouchard Jr. | Republican | Army veteran (82nd and 101st Airborne, Michigan Guard); businessman; Trump-endorsed; son of Oakland County Sheriff Michael Bouchard |

## The district

MI-10 includes southern Macomb County and part of Oakland County, with Warren and Sterling Heights. It's auto-manufacturing, defense-industry, union and suburban country. Macomb County is the home turf of the "Reagan Democrat," the traditionally Democratic, often union-connected voter who backed Republican presidents. It voted for Trump in 2016, 2020 and 2024 even as some Democrats stayed competitive in local and congressional races.

| Election | Republican | Democrat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2022 House | John James 48.8% | Carl Marlinga 48.3% |
| 2024 House | John James 51.1% | Carl Marlinga 45.0% |
| 2024 presidential | Trump 52.2% | Harris 45.7% |

James won by under a point in 2022 and by about six in 2024; Trump carried the district by about 6.5. Cook's PVI is R+3. Congressional and presidential results haven't always moved together, and neither party has an incumbent running.

## Candidates

### Christina Hines (D)

A former prosecutor in Wayne and Washtenaw counties and a law professor, Hines lost the 2024 race for Macomb County prosecutor to Republican incumbent Peter Lucido, so unlike a newcomer she has campaigned across much of this electorate. She emphasizes affordability, healthcare, childcare, public safety and protecting Social Security and Medicare, plus reproductive rights and accountability. She won the August primary with about 47%, ahead of Eric Chung (29.7%) and former Pontiac mayor Tim Greimel (23.3%).

### Michael Bouchard Jr. (R)

Bouchard served in the 82nd and 101st Airborne Divisions and the Michigan Army National Guard and has worked in business. He's the son of the Oakland County sheriff, a long-time Republican officeholder with name recognition in southeastern Michigan; the candidate is the son, not the sheriff. Trump endorsed him in the primary, which he won with about 72%. He emphasizes border security, public safety, economic growth, lower taxes and veterans.

### Other candidates

The certified ballot also has Mike Saliba (Libertarian), Kwabena Nkromo (Green) and Andrea L. Kirby (Working Class Party). They matter for interpreting polls that test only Hines and Bouchard.

## Money

| --- | --- | --- |
| Hines | $4.16M | $1.01M |
| Bouchard | $2.67M | $1.56M |

Through October 14, 2026. Hines raised about $1.5M more; Bouchard has about $550K more cash. Outside spending dwarfs both: about **$21.35 million** in independent expenditures through October 22 (about $20.52M in the general election), roughly $11.8M supporting Hines or opposing Bouchard and $8.7M the other way. House Majority PAC leads at about $7.3M. These aren't donations to the candidates.

## The issues

| Issue | Distinctions |
| --- | --- |
| Affordability and manufacturing | Hines: groceries, healthcare, childcare. Bouchard: tax cuts, business growth, manufacturing jobs. |
| Immigration and public safety | Bouchard: border enforcement, law enforcement. Hines: her prosecutor background, with immigration policy alongside other public-safety priorities. |
| Healthcare | Hines: protect coverage and federal programs. Bouchard: Republican approaches to cost and less regulation. |
| Abortion | Hines supports abortion rights; Bouchard's positions differ, especially on federal protections and restrictions. |
| Federal taxes | Bouchard backs the Republican tax agenda; Hines stresses who benefits and the costs for working families. |

Guides.vote's MI-10 comparison has more detail on abortion, immigration, labor, guns, climate and the Iran conflict.

**Macomb economics.** Autos, unions and independent-minded working-class voters have made Macomb a recurring national campaign focus. Trade, manufacturing jobs, inflation and household costs matter here, and both parties try to tie national economic agendas to the industrial identity, differing on taxes, regulation, tariffs and spending.

**The Trump turnout question.** Oakland University political scientist David Dulio told Michigan Public that two considerations compete: the district's recent Republican presidential voting, and the historical tendency for presidential-party turnout to drop in midterms. Those are general patterns, not evidence of anyone's advantage.

## Why it matters nationally

- **An open Republican seat.** James's run for governor ties the race to Michigan's statewide environment.
- **Shifting results.** A sub-point win in 2022 and about six points in 2024 show how much it can swing in two cycles.
- **Macomb realignment.** A measure of how historically Democratic industrial communities respond to current economic appeals.
- **Money.** More than $20M in general-election outside spending.
- **Disagreeing forecasts.** Unlike districts where analysts agree, ratings range from Tilt Republican to Lean Democratic, which makes the lack of fresh independent polling matter.

Compare [PA-8](../pa-08/) (and [MI-4](../mi-04/)): both hold historically Democratic industrial communities that moved Republican for president, but PA-8 has a Republican incumbent, while MI-10 is an open seat with two candidates who haven't served in Congress.

## What to watch

A new independent likely-voter poll, late finance disclosures, and turnout in southern Macomb versus the Oakland County portion.
