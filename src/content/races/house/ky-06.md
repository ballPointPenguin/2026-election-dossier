Kentucky's 6th District, centered on Lexington, is an open seat: Republican Andy Barr, who has held it since 2013, is running for the U.S. Senate. Trump carried the district by about 15 points in 2024, and Barr won reelection by 27.

The major-party nominees are Republican Ralph Alvarado, a physician and former state senator, and Democrat Zach Dembo, an attorney and first-time congressional candidate. Cook rates the race Lean Republican. Several recent polls show Dembo ahead, but all were sponsored by Democratic campaigns or allied groups (see [below](#polling-and-ratings)).

## The district

KY-6 covers much of central Kentucky, including Lexington, Richmond, Georgetown and Winchester. It combines Lexington's urban and university population, suburbs around Fayette County and more conservative rural Bluegrass areas. The economy rests on higher education (University of Kentucky, Eastern Kentucky University), healthcare, manufacturing (including Georgetown's auto sector), agriculture and the horse industry.

**Redistricting.** The district was more competitive before 2022, when Kentucky's Republican legislature overrode Democratic Gov. Andy Beshear's veto of a congressional map that made it more Republican. It was not redrawn for 2026.

**Registration versus voting.** Republicans hold a registration edge of under half a percentage point, yet Republican candidates have won by wide margins, so registration is a poor guide to results here.

## Candidates

### Ralph Alvarado (R)

Alvarado, a physician from Winchester, served in the Kentucky Senate from 2015 to 2023, as the state's first Hispanic state senator, and was Tennessee's health commissioner from 2023 to 2025. He was Gov. Matt Bevin's running mate in 2019, when Bevin lost to Beshear. He won the May 2026 primary with Trump's endorsement.

His messaging has shifted since the primary. He ran on Trump alignment, border security and opposition to progressive social policy; after winning the nomination, his website moved toward independent leadership, affordability, healthcare and jobs. Local reporting noted that some prominent Trump references were removed, though he has not repudiated the endorsement. On healthcare he emphasizes his medical background, insurance reform, more state authority over healthcare programs and physician and patient decision-making.

### Zach Dembo (D)

Dembo, 39, is a former Navy JAG officer and federal prosecutor who has also taught and worked in Beshear's administration. He won a seven-way May primary, defeating former state Rep. Cherlynn Stevenson among others. He campaigns against political corruption and rising household costs, and has Beshear's endorsement. His platform includes protecting Medicaid, lowering healthcare costs, public education, reproductive rights, and regulating AI and data-center development. He has criticized the Trump administration's immigration enforcement and opposed the ongoing U.S. war with Iran.

### Other candidates

Per Kentucky's official filing records: Jay J. Bowman (Independent) and Pete Lynch (Kentucky Party) are on the printed ballot; Robert Quigley is a registered write-in.

## Why it matters nationally

**The Beshear model.** Kentucky votes heavily Republican for president but has repeatedly elected Beshear. Dembo's tie to him makes KY-6 a test of whether that cross-partisan appeal, built on public services, healthcare and local economics, carries to a House race, where control of Congress is at stake and voters may split their tickets differently.

**Healthcare.** Both candidates bring relevant experience but different emphases. The DCCC has made Alvarado's record on Medicaid and nursing-home legislation central to its attacks; those are partisan claims, distinct from independently established policy effects.

**A broader signal.** KY-6 is among the Republican-held seats outside the traditional swing districts drawing late attention. With Republicans holding a narrow House majority, results that diverge sharply from recent presidential and congressional patterns would raise questions about turnout, national conditions, candidate quality and the effects of redistricting.

## What to watch

Whether independent polling corroborates the Democratic-sponsored surveys, whether Alvarado and Dembo agree to more debates (both were invited, and participation in a shared forum was unresolved in earlier reporting), and whether the Senate race drives central Kentucky turnout.
