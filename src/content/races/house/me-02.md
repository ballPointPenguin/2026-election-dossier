Maine's 2nd District is a large, mostly rural district that Trump has carried three times but that has repeatedly elected a Democrat to Congress. This year Democratic incumbent Jared Golden is retiring, setting up an open-seat race between two familiar Maine figures: Republican Paul LePage, the former two-term governor known for a confrontational style and conservative fiscal policy, and Democrat Matt Dunlap, the state auditor and former secretary of state.

The race matters because it removes Golden's unusual personal electoral advantage from a district that otherwise moved toward Republicans. Yet independent polling has shown Dunlap with measurable support, and forecasters have revised their views during the fall.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Paul LePage | Republican | Governor 2011–2019; age 78; former Waterville mayor |
| Matt Dunlap | Democrat | State auditor; former secretary of state; age 61 |

## The district

ME-2 covers nearly 80% of Maine's land area: Bangor, Lewiston–Auburn, the interior, the northern farm counties and the Down East coast.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Bangor and Brewer | Healthcare, education, regional services, transportation |
| Lewiston–Auburn | Manufacturing history, healthcare, immigrant communities, working-class neighborhoods |
| Aroostook County | Potatoes, forestry, cross-border trade with Canada, rural healthcare |
| Western and northern interior | Logging, paper mills, recreation, tourism |
| Down East coast | Fishing, lobstering, tourism, small coastal towns |

Traditionally Democratic labor communities and university towns sit alongside socially conservative rural areas, forestry and farm counties. Cook calls it a district shaped by fishing and logging, with a PVI of R+4.

**Presidential voting.** Maine and Nebraska are the only states that award some presidential electoral votes by congressional district. ME-2 has gone to Trump in 2016, 2020 and 2024 even when Maine voted Democratic statewide, making it a national symbol of the divide between urban southern Maine and the rural north.

## Candidates

### Paul LePage (R)

LePage was mayor of Waterville and an executive at the discount retailer Marden's before serving as governor. His tenure was marked by fights with the Democratic Legislature over taxes, social spending, Medicaid expansion, public-sector jobs and budgets. He vetoed several MaineCare expansion bills, arguing they'd impose unsustainable costs; critics said his opposition limited access to healthcare. He lost a 2022 rematch bid for governor to Janet Mills. His congressional campaign stresses fiscal restraint, lower energy costs, immigration enforcement and Republican economic priorities, and his gubernatorial record has featured heavily in the debates.

### Matt Dunlap (D)

Dunlap was secretary of state from 2005 to 2011 and again from 2013 to 2021, after serving in the Maine House from the Old Town area, and is now state auditor. His career is tied to Maine's administrative and election institutions, plus outdoor recreation and hunting groups. He won a competitive June primary over Bangor state Sen. Joe Baldacci, Jordan Wood and Paige Loud, decided by ranked-choice voting. He emphasizes healthcare access, affordability, Social Security, Medicare and government accountability, and backs Medicare for All, a clear difference from Golden's more moderate healthcare stance.

**The contrast.** Both are established Maine politicians, not national recruits: LePage's record is conservative fiscal and social-policy fights, Dunlap's is state administration, election oversight and legislative service.

## The Golden factor

Golden, a Marine veteran, first won in 2018 and built a reputation for independence from Democratic leadership, sometimes voting with Republicans, while Trump carried the district.

| Election | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 2018 | Golden first elected after ranked-choice tabulation |
| 2020 | Reelected despite Trump winning the district |
| 2022 | Reelected by about 6 points |
| 2024 | Reelected by under 1 point, beating Republican Austin Theriault |

The open question: how much of Golden's crossover appeal was personal, and how much reflected an enduring willingness among ME-2 voters to send Democrats to Congress?

## The October debates

October 1 and 15, centered on Maine household issues.

| Issue | Dunlap (D) | LePage (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Medicare for All; broader public coverage | Opposes Medicaid expansion as previously proposed; provider reimbursement and cost control |
| Heating aid | Federal assistance; blames policies for higher fuel prices | Also supports aid; energy supply and fiscal policy |
| Social Security | Protect benefits; raise the taxable earnings cap | Says he'll protect benefits; has discussed raising the cap |
| Immigration | Immigration-court capacity; enforcement accountability | Enforcement; limits on benefits for unauthorized immigrants |
| Government fraud | Defends his office's audit work | State and federal programs need stronger fraud prevention |
| Iran | Criticizes the administration; favors diplomacy | Opposes an Iranian nuclear weapon; no U.S. ground troops |

**Healthcare** matters because Maine's rural hospitals face money and staffing strain, distances are long and the population is aging. LePage says inadequate reimbursement threatens services; Dunlap says expanding coverage is necessary and a universal system could cut costs now borne by employers and households. **Heating oil** is a major household expense, and both candidates backed fully funding LIHEAP in the October 15 debate, differing on who is responsible for higher fuel prices: Dunlap links them to the Iran war, while LePage acknowledges the war's role and cites other factors.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Ticket-splitting.** Golden showed a Democratic House candidate could draw support beyond the district's Democratic presidential vote. The open seat tests whether that outlasts him.
- **Rural economic policy.** Forestry, fishing, farming, manufacturing and tourism have distinct priorities; Canadian trade tariffs, federal healthcare spending and energy costs hit them directly.
- **Two Maine traditions.** A former governor with an established conservative record against an administrator with a different profile from Golden.
- **National money.** House Speaker Mike Johnson campaigned for LePage in Bangor on October 14; national Democrats are also investing heavily.
- **Maine's Senate race.** Sen. Susan Collins faces Democrat Troy Jackson. The races share voters, ad markets and turnout operations, though voters can split their tickets.

## What to watch

Another independent district poll, late outside-spending reports, and turnout across Lewiston–Auburn, Bangor, the Down East coast and the northern rural counties.

Maine uses ranked-choice voting for federal elections, so if no candidate wins a majority of first choices, the count could involve more rounds; with only Dunlap and LePage identified on the ballot, that may not matter.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
