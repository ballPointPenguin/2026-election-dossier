Florida's 9th District is one of the most consequential Democratic defensive races of 2026. Democratic Rep. Darren Soto, who has held the Orlando-area seat since 2017, is seeking a sixth term against Republican Dan Green, a military veteran and businessman from Indian River County.

The defining issue is Florida's 2026 redistricting. Soto won by about 13 points in 2024, but the Republican-controlled legislature turned his Democratic-leaning, majority-Hispanic district centered on Osceola County into a much larger, more Republican one stretching east toward the Atlantic. The redrawn district would have voted for Trump by about 18 points in 2024, yet the limited public polling has twice found Soto and Green tied. So a Democratic incumbent is the underdog in his own district, but his history and campaign resources give him a plausible path.

## The district

The new FL-9 is huge by Central Florida standards, about 5,500 square miles and roughly three times its predecessor's size, from the Orlando metro toward Lake Okeechobee and the Atlantic coast.

- **Osceola County and Kissimmee:** heavily Hispanic, especially Puerto Rican; tourism, hospitality, healthcare, Orlando commuters.
- **Indian River County and Vero Beach:** coastal communities, retirees, tourism, agriculture; a much more Republican electorate.
- **The interior agricultural region:** ranching, citrus, farming, small towns, economic concerns unlike metro Orlando's.
- **Lake Okeechobee communities:** agriculture, water management, environmental concerns, rural infrastructure.

Soto must now introduce himself to many voters who weren't his constituents, while Green can campaign where Republicans already do well for president.

## Candidates

### Darren Soto (D)

His base is Kissimmee and Osceola County, where he has built ties with Puerto Rican communities, local governments, healthcare providers and the tourism industry. He presents as a pragmatic Democrat focused on federal infrastructure, disaster recovery, economic development and constituent services. His 2026 campaign has three priorities: lower costs, protect healthcare and stop corruption. He wants Medicaid and ACA funding restored, more affordable housing, protection for Social Security and Medicare and lower drug prices; he opposes the administration's tariffs and the Iran war as raising household costs, and criticizes mass deportations, arguing that immigration restrictions and lost work authorization have caused labor shortages in agriculture, tourism and other industries. His greatest asset is nearly a decade representing Central Florida; his greatest liability is that much of the new district has never voted for him.

### Dan Green (R)

Green holds degrees from American University and Florida State and a doctorate from George Washington. He stresses military and national-security credentials, but his domestic priorities are affordability, fiscal restraint, lower property-insurance costs and less regulation, plus domestic energy, a strong military and conservative approaches to housing and economic development. In an October WKMG interview he called for lower tariffs and more price transparency while broadly supporting Trump's economic direction, giving him some rhetorical overlap with Soto on trade costs. He has never held elected office.

Through October 14, 2026. Green has personally loaned his campaign about $2.24M, so his receipts aren't equivalent donor fundraising. Soto raised more; Green has more cash. Independent expenditures reached about **$7.27 million** through October 31 (about $6.94M in the general election, $334K in primaries): roughly $4.3M supporting Soto or opposing Green and $2.6M the other way, with House Majority PAC the largest at about $3M. The late spending against Green suggests Democratic groups think Soto still has a real chance.

## The issues

**Affordability and property insurance.** Housing, insurance and utility costs are central. Soto backs housing assistance, federal insurance reform and tax relief for working families; Green stresses cutting regulations and federal spending, domestic energy and addressing insurance costs. Both call affordability a priority but prefer very different mechanisms.

**Healthcare and federal spending.** Soto opposes cuts to Medicaid, ACA subsidies and other programs, arguing they'll raise the uninsured count and strain hospitals and employers. Green stresses debt reduction, waste elimination and long-term reform; in October interviews they disagreed on the debt and on financing Medicare and Social Security.

**Tariffs, immigration, the Iran war.** Soto says tariffs, Iran war spending and mass deportations have raised prices and caused labor shortages. Green stresses regulatory reform, energy and economic competition, and has called for lower tariffs despite backing much of Trump's agenda. It matters in a district of farms, hospitality workers and large immigrant populations.

**Environment and development.** The new district includes sensitive areas around Lake Okeechobee, farmland and coastal communities, so water quality, stormwater, hurricane resilience, invasive species and development pressure matter locally. Soto stresses environmental protection and infrastructure; Green stresses curbing overdevelopment and lowering housing and insurance costs while encouraging growth.

## Why it matters nationally

- **An incumbent in a transformed district.** Soto is campaigning in a very different constituency from the one that kept electing him.
- **A test of Florida's mid-decade map.** It shows how changed boundaries can change an incumbent's prospects without any change in his positions or performance.
- **Hispanic and Puerto Rican participation.** Soto's Osceola base remains important, but the district now holds many more Republican-leaning communities.
- **Incumbent versus newcomer.** Legislative experience and constituent ties against military and executive-branch credentials but no elected-office record.
- **A Democratic defensive priority.** National spending shows the party is contesting, not conceding.
- **A possible Republican pickup.** Unlike the many Republican-held seats turning competitive, FL-9 is a chance for Republicans to gain a Democratic seat.

Quantitative models give Green an edge without treating Soto's survival as implausible: Polling Forecast's November 2 estimate is a 65% Republican win probability with Green by 2.2 points, and Scrutinel's is 74% with Green by 3.9. ## What to watch on election night

- **Kissimmee and Osceola County.** Soto needs strong turnout and large Democratic margins from his established constituents, especially Puerto Rican and other Hispanic communities.
- **Vero Beach and Indian River County.** Green's home region is an important Republican base; his margin here could decide whether Soto's Osceola advantage is enough.
- **Highlands, Okeechobee and Glades counties.** Rural Republican margins and interior turnout matter to the districtwide result.

## Assessment

Green is favored, but Soto has a credible reelection chance. The structural case for Green is strong: a Trump +18 district, lots of Republican territory and a national party eager to gain a Democratic seat. Soto has incumbency, a decade of service, stronger donor fundraising and heavy outside spending on his behalf, and both public polls show ties. Scarce independent polling makes it hard to assess, which fits a spread from Lean to Likely Republican. A Soto win would be one of the more striking cases of an incumbent beating an unfavorable mid-decade redistricting, and a Green win would show how well the map turned a Democratic-held Central Florida seat into a Republican opportunity.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
