Ohio's 10th District is centered on Dayton and its suburbs, with a big concentration of military, aerospace, healthcare and manufacturing jobs. Longtime Republican Rep. Mike Turner faces Democrat Kristina Knickerbocker, an Air Force veteran and oncology nurse practitioner. Thomas McMasters, a Libertarian and former mayor of Huber Heights, is also running.

What's notable is how late this race arrived on the national map. Turner has held the seat since 2003 and won by more than 18 points in 2024, but Democrats have recently invested, and Cook has twice moved its rating toward competitive since August.

## The district

After Ohio's October 2025 redistricting, OH-10 holds all of Montgomery and Greene counties and part of Butler County: Dayton, Kettering, Beavercreek, Centerville and communities toward Middletown.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Dayton / Montgomery County | Urban neighborhoods, healthcare, higher education, manufacturing, logistics |
| Kettering and Centerville | Established suburbs; professional and middle-income |
| Beavercreek / Greene County | Aerospace, defense, technology, military families |
| Wright-Patterson area | Major federal employment, research and defense contracting |
| Butler County portion | Suburban and industrial; the Dayton–Cincinnati corridor |

Wright-Patterson Air Force Base is a major center of Air Force research, development, acquisition and logistics, so defense spending hits the regional economy directly. Dayton also has a long industrial history, especially in autos and engineering, so redevelopment, wages, housing and healthcare matter alongside national security.

## Candidates

### Mike Turner (R)

Turner is seeking a 13th term. His career centers on national security, defense appropriations and intelligence oversight; he chaired the House Intelligence Committee until removed from that post in January 2025. He emphasizes federal defense investment, Wright-Patterson and regional economic development, plus Republican positions on taxes, immigration enforcement, domestic energy and federal spending. His long tenure gives voters a big record to examine, including defense work and votes on major Republican legislation.

### Kristina Knickerbocker (D)

An Air Force major and nurse at Wright-Patterson who later became a neuro-oncology nurse practitioner at Ohio State, she won the May 5 primary against five opponents with about a third of the vote. Turner was unopposed. Her campaign focuses on healthcare affordability, veterans' services, reproductive rights, household costs and government ethics, drawing on the district's large defense community and major healthcare employers.

### Thomas McMasters (L)

A former Huber Heights mayor with a local-government background, he polled 4% in the most recent Democratic-sponsored poll.

Candidate money is modest, but independent groups buy ads without passing money through campaigns: Quiver Quantitative estimates about $11.24 million in cumulative spending tied to the contest over the two-year cycle, including outside activity, with a caution that recent disclosures may be incomplete. That's a different measure from candidate receipts. The AP reported in mid-October that the DCCC was expanding its ad plans to more Republican-held districts, including Turner's.

## The issues

| Issue | Turner (R) | Knickerbocker (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Republican approaches to spending and regulation | Coverage affordability; protect access to care |
| Defense and veterans | Congressional experience; Wright-Patterson support | Military service; veterans' healthcare |
| Taxes and spending | Republican tax cuts and fiscal priorities | Criticizes policies she says raise household costs |
| Abortion | Supports restrictions | Supports reproductive rights |
| Ethics | His record and public-service experience | Stronger accountability and ethics reforms |

**Ethics complaints.** Complaints have been lodged concerning both candidates; an October 1 voter guide says neither had been resolved. Allegations aren't findings.

**Seniority versus a fresh résumé.** Turner's votes, committee work and constituent service can be judged directly; Knickerbocker's congressional priorities are campaign proposals.

## Why it matters

- **Not a frontline seat.** Turner's 2024 win far exceeded the Republican presidential margin and he's held the seat since 2003.
- **The Dayton region's direction.** Montgomery County's urban electorate, Greene County's defense communities and part of Butler County put different interests in one district.
- **A military economy.** Wright-Patterson and its employers give defense policy immediate local stakes.
- **An expanding battlefield.** [OH-7](../oh-07/) and [OH-9](../oh-09/) have been watched longer; [OH-15](../oh-15/) and now OH-10 have drawn attention as national groups reassess spending.
- **Polls versus history.** The DCCC survey shows a close race, while Turner's record and the reconstructed presidential vote point the other way, and there's little independent polling.
