Colorado's 4th District has become an unexpectedly interesting Republican defensive contest. Incumbent Lauren Boebert, one of the country's most recognizable MAGA-aligned members of Congress, faces Eileen Laubacher, a retired two-star Navy admiral and former Republican who has raised an extraordinary amount of money.

CO-4 is one of Colorado's most Republican districts: Trump carried it by more than 20 points in 2024. Yet Boebert won her 2024 race by only about 12, and the one published 2026 poll has her five points ahead. Cook and Sabato recently moved it to Lean Republican, though other forecasters remain more skeptical of an upset.

## The district

CO-4 covers a large part of eastern Colorado, from the fast-growing suburbs south and east of Denver across the Eastern Plains to the Kansas and Nebraska borders.

- **Douglas County** (Highlands Ranch, Parker, Castle Rock, Lone Tree): affluent suburbs, traditionally Republican but increasingly competitive among college-educated voters. It supplies a large share of the electorate, so suburban shifts there move districtwide margins far more than similar changes in sparse eastern counties.
- **Eastern Weld County:** agriculture, energy, exurbs.
- **Elbert County:** rural and exurban, strongly Republican.
- **The Eastern Plains:** farming, ranching, small towns, agricultural processing, energy across many sparsely populated counties.

No Democrat has won since Markey's single term. CO-4 isn't just a marginal seat with a controversial incumbent; it has a substantial structural Republican advantage.

## Candidates

### Lauren Boebert (R)

She gained national prominence through outspoken support for Trump, a confrontational style, gun-rights advocacy and Freedom Caucus alignment, and her career has had its share of controversy, including efforts to challenge Republican leadership and frequent attacks on Democratic officials. Her most consequential electoral decision was geographic: after winning reelection in CO-3 in 2022 by just 546 votes, she ran in the much more Republican 4th in 2024, when Ken Buck retired, and beat Democrat Trisha Calvarese. Her 2026 campaign stresses border security, immigration enforcement, gun rights, lower taxes, less federal spending and domestic energy, and strongly backs Trump's agenda. Her vulnerability is that her national identity may appeal less to suburban Republicans and unaffiliated voters than a more conventional conservative would.

### Eileen Laubacher (D)

Her military career, ending as a two-star admiral, gives her national-security and leadership credentials, and her status as a former Republican is central to her appeal. She isn't a progressive counterpart to Boebert: she stresses fiscal responsibility, lower living costs, protecting rural hospitals, border security and bipartisan governance, and supports congressional term limits and a ban on members trading individual stocks. The positioning is deliberate, aimed at voters who usually prefer Republicans but are dissatisfied with Boebert's style or performance.

### Other candidates

Douglas Mangeris (Libertarian) could matter in a close race, especially if he draws limited-government voters reluctant to back Boebert. Luis Galvan (Republican) is a qualified write-in.

## Money

| Candidate | Raised | Cash remaining |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laubacher | $13.70M | $1.85M |
| Boebert | $1.23M | $274K |

Through October 14, 2026. Laubacher has raised more than eleven times as much, extraordinary for a challenger in a heavily Republican district. It suggests opposition to Boebert has become a major national fundraising cause and that Laubacher has turned her military background and moderate positioning into donor support, though money and electoral success aren't linearly related and ads can't necessarily overcome a district's partisan composition.

Outside spending is modest by comparison: FEC-derived trackers report about $2.4 million in independent expenditures, including substantial anti-Boebert spending by Invest in Tomorrow Coalition and support for Boebert from conservative groups such as the Freedom Caucus Fund. Unlike many battlegrounds, here the challenger herself has the dominant financial resources.

## The issues

**Affordability and federal spending.** Boebert wants lower taxes, less federal spending, deregulation and more energy production. Laubacher stresses fiscal responsibility while criticizing policies she says raise household costs or threaten essential services, with a focus on rural hospitals that matters on the Eastern Plains, where facilities often run on thin margins.

**Immigration and the border.** Boebert strongly backs the administration's enforcement, including tighter border control and expanded deportations. Laubacher also stresses border security but pairs it with pragmatic governance, contesting a traditionally Republican issue rather than conceding it.

**Energy, agriculture and water.** The district has major farm production and energy infrastructure. Boebert supports more oil and gas development, fewer environmental restrictions and federal support for extraction. Laubacher stresses economic stability, infrastructure, public services and the effects of federal policy on rural communities. Water, farm input costs and federal land management matter regionally but have drawn less national attention than the candidates' identities.

**Ethics and conduct.** Laubacher backs term limits and stock-trading limits and stresses leadership, institutional competence and bipartisan cooperation. Boebert says her confrontational approach is necessary to challenge Washington's establishment and advance conservative priorities. It's partly ideological and partly a dispute over what effective representation means.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A referendum on a prominent MAGA politician.** Her showing will be read partly as a measure of confrontational Trump-aligned politics.
- **A former Republican running as a Democrat.** An effort to recruit military-credentialed moderates who might appeal beyond the Democratic base.
- **A fundraising experiment.** Over $13M raised in a district with a big Republican advantage.
- **Possible suburban breakthrough.** If suburban Republicans and unaffiliated voters in Douglas County move sharply toward Laubacher, the race could get much closer.
- **Boebert's earlier switch.** She left a highly competitive Western Slope district for a safer seat, so another difficult election would be notable even if she wins.
- **House control.** CO-4 isn't among the likeliest Democratic pickups, but an upset would cost Republicans a seat once considered secure.

## What to watch on election night

- **Douglas County.** The key indicator: Laubacher needs substantial improvement over earlier Democratic showings in Highlands Ranch, Parker, Castle Rock and nearby suburbs.
- **The Eastern Plains and rural counties.** Boebert will look to keep big Republican margins among farm and energy voters.
- **The Libertarian vote.** If Mangeris gets anything near September's 9%, how those votes break could affect the plurality needed.
- **Gap with other Democrats.** A large gap between statewide Democratic support and Laubacher's would help show candidate-specific appeal or resistance to Boebert.

## Assessment

Boebert remains the favorite, but this is no longer an entirely routine Republican defense. Her district's presidential margin, incumbency and the thin polling favor her, with Decision Desk HQ at an 80% chance. Laubacher's opening rests on an unusual fundraising edge, a military and former-Republican biography designed for crossover voters, and potential Democratic gains in the Denver suburbs. A Democratic win would be a big upset, while even a narrow Republican win could be informative about a strongly Republican district becoming more competitive under particular candidates and conditions.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
