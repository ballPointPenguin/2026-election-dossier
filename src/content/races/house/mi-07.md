Michigan's 7th District is a Republican-held seat centered on Lansing and surrounding mid-Michigan communities. Its recent presidential vote is nearly even, and it has changed parties in each of the last two congressional elections.

First-term Republican Tom Barrett faces Democrat William "Will" Lawrence, a climate organizer and co-founder of the Sunrise Movement, with the Green Party's Shane Dedrick also on the ballot. The two come from sharply different backgrounds: Barrett is a military veteran and former state legislator with a first-term record, Lawrence comes from progressive organizing, not elected office.

## The district

MI-7 includes all of Ingham, Livingston, Clinton and Shiawassee counties, most of Eaton County, and parts of Genesee and Oakland counties.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Lansing / East Lansing | State government, Michigan State, healthcare, auto manufacturing |
| Livingston County / Howell / Brighton | Growing suburbs and exurbs between Lansing and Detroit |
| Clinton and Eaton counties | Agriculture, manufacturing, government jobs, suburbs |
| Shiawassee County | Small cities, farming, manufacturing |
| Oakland and Genesee portions | Detroit-area suburbs and exurbs, including South Lyon |

Democratic-leaning Lansing and East Lansing sit alongside more Republican rural areas and exurbs, with Livingston County central to the district's Republican vote. The economy links state government and higher education with the auto supply chain, farming and suburban development.

| Election | Republican | Democrat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Barrett 46.3% | Elissa Slotkin 51.7% |
| 2024 House | Barrett 50.3% | Curtis Hertel 46.6% |
| 2024 president | Trump 49.9% | Harris 48.6% |

Slotkin beat Barrett in 2022, then left to win a Senate seat in 2024. With her off the ballot, Barrett beat former state Sen. Curtis Hertel, and Trump carried the district narrowly. Cook's PVI is even (R+0). Presidential results are nearly even, but the last two House elections involved different Democrats and different outcomes.

## Candidates

### Tom Barrett (R)

He stresses border security, veterans' services, domestic manufacturing, tax policy and lower energy costs, and backed the 2025 Republican tax and spending law. His campaign asks voters to judge his first-term record, not just national politics.

### William Lawrence (D)

Lawrence also helped organize the Michigan Rent Is Too Damn High Coalition and the Mid-Michigan Tenant Resource Center. He stresses housing, healthcare, climate and energy, labor rights and opposition to parts of Trump's agenda, and has never held elected office.

**The August 4 primary.** He beat former U.S. ambassador to Ukraine Bridget Brink, ex-Navy SEAL and Obama administration official Matthew Maasdam and another contender. Sanders endorsed him; Brink and Maasdam stressed diplomatic, military and national-security experience. So the nominee is a climate and housing organizer rather than a former officer, prosecutor or moderate local official.

### Shane Dedrick (Green)

In a district with a recent history of close races, his vote is worth recording, but there's no reliable basis to assign it an expected share.

## The October 6 debate

At WKAR's East Lansing studios, covering healthcare, tariffs, the Iran war, immigration, AI data centers and surveillance. ([Michigan Advance recap](https://michiganadvance.com/2026/10/06/iran-war-trump-tariffs-and-michigan-data-centers-dominate-questions-during-barrett-lawrence-debate/))

**Healthcare.** Lawrence backs single-payer for continuous coverage and less exposure to premiums and deductibles. Barrett opposes it over its projected federal cost and points to his proposed Care Over Profits Act, requiring insurers to spend more premium revenue on medical claims. Both say insurance is expensive; they differ on government's role.

**Tariffs and autos.** Lawrence says unpredictable trade policy raises costs and complicates planning. Barrett stresses domestic manufacturing and economic security. It matters locally because the district has auto and supplier businesses with cross-border production chains.

**The Iran war.** Partial agreement: Barrett, a combat veteran, said he opposed continuing the war without congressional authorization and stressed fuel prices; Lawrence also opposed it but criticized Barrett's earlier statements about Trump's decision to start it. Both want it ended while disputing responsibility.

**AI data centers.** Lawrence strongly opposes hyperscale data centers over electricity, water and community impacts. Barrett stresses economic development and technology infrastructure, with attention to local costs and regulation.

**Surveillance.** Both voiced reservations about unrestricted law-enforcement access to license-plate-reader data such as Flock cameras, a notable point of agreement.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A seat that changed parties in 2024.** Slotkin held it before winning the Senate, and Barrett took the open seat. This tests whether the change holds under a different national environment.
- **A progressive nominee in a near-even seat.** Lawrence's primary win over candidates with diplomatic and military credentials drew national attention, without proving a general rule.
- **A record versus a program.** Barrett has votes; Lawrence has a platform built through organizing.
- **Spending.** Over $35M, more than the other races here.
- **Michigan's House battlegrounds.** [MI-4](../mi-04/) and [MI-10](../mi-10/), plus Senate and governor races on the same ballot.
- **A Green candidate.** A third option where margins have been small.
