Pennsylvania's 7th District, centered on the Lehigh Valley (Allentown, Bethlehem, Easton), is a Republican-held seat that changed parties in 2024 and is again heavily funded. First-term Republican Ryan Mackenzie faces Democrat Bob Brooks, a longtime Bethlehem firefighter and head of the statewide firefighters' union.

Recent House races here have been decided by narrow margins, the presidential vote is almost evenly split, and several forecasters revised their ratings in October.

| Ryan Mackenzie | Republican | Elected 2024; Pennsylvania House 2012–2024 |
| Bob Brooks | Democrat | About 20 years a Bethlehem firefighter; president of the Pennsylvania Professional Fire Fighters Association; owns a lawn-care business; endorsed by Gov. Josh Shapiro |

## The district

PA-7 holds all of Lehigh, Northampton and Carbon counties and part of Monroe County.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Allentown / Lehigh County | Healthcare, manufacturing, distribution, diverse neighborhoods and suburbs |
| Bethlehem | Higher education, healthcare, industrial heritage, redevelopment |
| Easton / Northampton County | Manufacturing, logistics, commuting, small business |
| Carbon County | Rural and small-town; tourism; southern Poconos |
| Monroe County portion | Pocono communities, tourism, housing, commuting |

Historically a steel and heavy-industry center, the Lehigh Valley now combines manufacturing, healthcare, warehousing and logistics, universities and growing suburbs. It's within commuting range of New York and Philadelphia, which puts housing and transportation into local politics.

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Wild (D) +2 |
| 2024 House | Mackenzie (R) +1.0 (203,688 to 199,626 votes, 50.4% to 49.4%) |
| 2024 president | Trump +3 (51% to 48%) |

Mackenzie won by 4,062 votes, ending Susan Wild's three terms. Cook's PVI is R+1.

## Candidates

### Ryan Mackenzie (R)

In Washington he serves on Education and Workforce, Foreign Affairs and Homeland Security, with interests in taxes, workforce policy, public safety and economic development. His campaign stresses tax relief for families, housing affordability, growth and border security. In his first term he backed some bipartisan measures, including an extension of enhanced ACA subsidies and housing-affordability bills.

### Bob Brooks (D)

Brooks has never served in Congress or the Pennsylvania Legislature. He stresses labor rights, healthcare access, affordability and ethics, with a platform of stronger collective-bargaining protections, a higher minimum wage, restoring Medicaid cuts and moving toward universal coverage. He won the May four-candidate primary, which involved heavy outside spending:

The November ballot has two candidates: Green Party candidate Andrew Tupone was removed, and two other prospective independents didn't qualify.

That scale is striking in a district whose last House race was decided by about 4,000 votes.

## The policy disputes

| Issue | Mackenzie (R) | Brooks (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes | Supported the 2025 Republican law, including child and childcare provisions | Higher corporate taxes; a minimum tax on billionaires |
| Healthcare | Voted to advance a three-year enhanced ACA subsidy extension; drug-pricing transparency | Restore Medicaid cuts; move toward universal coverage |
| Tariffs | Supports some, but criticizes the uncertainty from shifting trade policy | Opposes broad tariffs over consumer and business costs |
| Housing | Homebuyer tax credits; bipartisan housing bills | Starter homes; first-time buyer aid; limits on corporate landlords |
| Labor | Workforce training; fewer regulatory burdens on employers | PRO Act; stronger union protections; higher minimum wage |
| Iran war | Supported continued military action; opposed resolutions to end it | Opposes the war; stresses its economic costs |

**Healthcare.** Mackenzie was one of 17 House Republicans who backed a January 2026 effort to restore enhanced ACA premium subsidies for three years, which sets him apart from Republicans like Scott Perry in [PA-10](../pa-10/). He says the ACA needs broader reform; Brooks wants subsidies and Medicaid funding restored and wider coverage.

**Housing.** Both acknowledge rising costs: the median home sale price in Lehigh and Northampton counties hit about $379,000 in August, nearly 10% above a year earlier. Both back reducing some barriers to construction.

**Ethics.** Spotlight PA found both support term limits and limits on members' stock trading, but with different proposals: Mackenzie, a constitutional amendment for three House terms and a bill restricting stock ownership and trading; Brooks, a five-term limit, stronger campaign-finance rules and an amendment reversing Citizens United. Disclosures show Brooks and his wife hold individual stocks managed by a professional adviser, and his campaign says they'd divest if he won. Mackenzie reported no individual stocks. Disclosures alone don't establish wrongdoing.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A recently flipped seat.** Mackenzie beat three-term incumbent Wild in 2024; this is his first reelection.
- **No partisan margin to speak of.** R+1 and razor-thin House results make it a clean case study of a mixed electorate.
- **A labor leader and a former state legislator.** Union and emergency-services experience against state legislative experience and a first-term record.
- **A third Pennsylvania type.** [PA-8](../pa-08/) has Scranton and coal-region economics, PA-10 has Harrisburg and York; PA-7 combines industrial heritage with logistics, healthcare and fast-growing residential areas.
- **The statewide ballot.** Gov. Josh Shapiro is seeking reelection and endorsed Brooks, relevant to organization and turnout, though it doesn't show how voters will choose.
- **Money.** Nearly $21M in outside spending including the primary.
