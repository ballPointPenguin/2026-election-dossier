Colorado's 5th District is centered on Colorado Springs and most of El Paso County, including Cimarron Hills, the Fort Carson area and major military installations. No Democrat has won it since its creation in 1973. But Colorado Springs has grown and changed a lot, and recent presidential results are closer than the district's old reputation suggests.

**The military.** The district includes or abuts Fort Carson, the U.S. Air Force Academy, Peterson and Schriever Space Force Bases and Cheyenne Mountain. Per the AP, nearly one in nine residents is a veteran, among the highest shares of any district. So federal military policy bears directly on local jobs, households and identity, which makes the Space Command headquarters decision especially significant.

## Candidates

### Jeff Crank (R)

Crank succeeded longtime Rep. Doug Lamborn in 2024. Before Congress he was a conservative radio host, an aide to Rep. Joel Hefley and Colorado director of Americans for Prosperity. His agenda: tax cuts, domestic energy, border security, military readiness and spending restraint. He has generally backed the Trump administration's legislation, including the 2025 tax and spending law, but publicly opposes relocating Space Command headquarters from Colorado to Alabama. On the Iran war he told the AP on October 15 that once operations began the United States should accomplish its objectives and bring service members home.

### Jessica Killin (D)

Killin grew up in Colorado Springs, served as an Army captain and paratrooper (including in the Balkans), worked for USAA and served in the Biden administration. She won the June primary over fellow veteran Joe Reagan with about 61%. She stresses housing and healthcare affordability, veterans' services, reproductive rights and congressional oversight of the executive branch. She criticizes the administration's conduct of the Iran war and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's leadership, saying military institutions should stay professionally independent of partisan politics. The AP reported on her outreach to Republican and unaffiliated veterans dissatisfied with federal military policy, alongside veterans who strongly back Crank and Trump.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Colorado Springs is changing.** The city has grown and diversified, and Cook flags the district's leftward presidential trend as unusual among House seats.
- **Veteran politics.** A long-time conservative organizer against a career-service veteran, debating the Iran war and military leadership in a district where that's personal.
- **Independence from the president.** Crank's Space Command stand is a concrete break with an administration decision; Killin argues Congress needs to exercise broader oversight.
- **A wider map.** CO-5's attention shows the 2026 House battlefield reaching constituencies that have always elected Republicans. Killin is one of several Democratic veteran candidates, with Lacore in [SC-1](../sc-01/) and Gray in [FL-13](../fl-13/).
