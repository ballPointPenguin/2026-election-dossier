Texas's 28th District is an unusual contest: a long-serving conservative Democrat defending a South Texas seat that voted for Trump in 2024, against a former Democratic county judge who switched parties. The candidates are incumbent Henry Cuellar (D), former Webb County Judge Tano Tijerina (R) and Marlón Durán (Green), all on the certified November ballot.

The big recent news came on October 14, when the Republican-aligned Congressional Leadership Fund pulled about $3 million in planned advertising against Cuellar and redirected it to other Texas races. That's notable because Republicans redrew TX-28 in 2025 to help win House seats.

## The district

TX-28 is anchored in Laredo on the U.S.–Mexico border and extends into the Rio Grande Valley. Its character comes from overwhelmingly Hispanic communities, cross-border trade, logistics, agriculture, energy and border enforcement. The 2025 map moved it farther toward McAllen and dropped territory around San Antonio.

(Reconstructed presidential results.) This was once a Democratic stronghold: Hillary Clinton carried the earlier version by a wide margin in 2016. By 2024 Trump carried it and Cuellar was reelected, so South Texas's presidential shift hasn't translated evenly into Republican wins for Congress.

## Candidates

### Henry Cuellar (D)

A Laredo native in Congress since January 2005, Cuellar served in the Texas Legislature and briefly as secretary of state. He sits on the conservative wing of his party: constituent services, border security, economic development, infrastructure money and working with Republicans on selected issues. He often criticizes Democratic immigration policy from the right while stressing the value of legal cross-border commerce. He has long opposed abortion rights, though not always in line with Republican proposals.

### Tano Tijerina (R)

A former professional baseball player and former Webb County judge (the county's top elected executive), Tijerina has Laredo-area name recognition to match Cuellar's. He was elected as a Democrat and became a Republican in 2024, an example of South Texas's partisan realignment. He emphasizes border enforcement, economic development, public safety, lower taxes and his local-government record. So the contest isn't liberal versus conservative so much as two politicians from the same border community, one a conservative Democrat who stayed and one who switched.

### Marlón Durán (Green)

A third ballot option. In a close plurality race third-party votes could matter, but there's no evidence of which major candidate his voters would otherwise back.

## The pardon

In May 2024 the Justice Department indicted Cuellar and his wife on federal charges including alleged bribery, money laundering and acting on behalf of foreign interests, alleging payments from an Azerbaijani energy company and a Mexican bank were tied to official actions. Cuellar denied wrongdoing. In December 2025 President Trump pardoned both, ending the federal exposure with no trial establishing guilt or innocence.

So a Republican president pardoned a Democratic congressman whose district Republicans then targeted in redistricting. The pardon doesn't establish whether the allegations were true, and Cuellar didn't change parties. Politically it scrambles party messaging: Cuellar can point to cross-party cooperation, and opponents can keep raising the original allegations.

**The October reversal.** On October 14 the Congressional Leadership Fund cancelled about $3 million in future reserved ads against Cuellar. That doesn't undo money already spent: per AdImpact, the Texas Tribune reported CLF had already put roughly $4.6 million into the race. The freed money is going to TX-15 and TX-34. House Speaker Mike Johnson appeared with Tijerina the day before and disputed the available polling, a sign that different parts of a party operation can make different resource calls.

## Why it matters nationally

**Map versus the person.** Republicans redrew TX-28 to raise its Republican presidential vote, while Cuellar argues the new lines still hold plenty of voters who backed Democrats for the House in 2024. Presidential partisanship is an imperfect guide to the strength of individual House candidates.

| 2024 election (old district) | Result |
| --- | --- |
| President | Trump by about 7 points |
| U.S. House | Cuellar by about 5.6 points |

That's roughly a 13-point gap between presidential and House margins, though it can't say exactly which voters split tickets.

**Latino realignment isn't one-way.** Cuellar kept his party and often takes conservative stances; Tijerina, an established Laredo politician, switched. The contest pits two kinds of local identity and partisan loyalty, not two different communities.

**The border economy.** Laredo is one of North America's key land ports for Mexico trade: freight, warehousing, customs, supply chains. Trade and immigration policy matter enormously, and enforcement is related to but distinct from moving lawful commercial traffic. Healthcare access, wages, energy costs and infrastructure matter across the rural and urban parts.

## What to watch

Whether new district polls appear, early voting in Webb and Hidalgo counties, whether the spending withdrawal is followed by more changes, and how voters weigh local ties against national party preference. Texas early voting runs October 19–30.
