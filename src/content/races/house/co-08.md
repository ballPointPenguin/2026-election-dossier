Colorado's 8th District is a great House battleground because both of its elections have been decided by less than a point, and each was won by a different party. First-term Republican Gabe Evans faces Democrat Manny Rutinel, a former Colorado state representative. Libertarian Dave Wood is also on the ballot.

CO-8 is Colorado's newest district, created after the 2020 census, combining the growing northern Denver suburbs with farm and industrial communities farther north.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gabe Evans | Republican | Incumbent since January 2025; Army helicopter pilot; Arvada police officer; one term in the state House; Fort Lupton |
| Manny Rutinel | Democrat | Attorney; former state representative; environmental-policy advocate; Commerce City |
| Dave Wood | Libertarian | Retired computer programmer from Westminster |

## The district

CO-8 stretches north from metro Denver into Weld County and a bit of Larimer County.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Thornton, Northglenn, Commerce City | Diverse, growing Denver suburbs; healthcare, logistics, services |
| Brighton and Adams County | Suburban growth, agriculture, distribution |
| Greeley | University of Northern Colorado; agriculture, meatpacking, healthcare, manufacturing |
| Fort Lupton and southern Weld | Agriculture, oil and gas, manufacturing, residential growth |
| Smaller Weld communities | Farming, energy, small business, rural services |

Colorado Public Radio describes strongly Democratic northern Denver suburbs, more conservative rural Weld County and politically mixed Greeley. About 40% of residents are Hispanic or Latino, well above the state share, and both nominees have Hispanic or Latino family backgrounds, which doesn't show how Hispanic voters will divide. Suburban housing and transportation pressures meet agriculture, oil and gas, industry and water concerns.

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2022 House | Caraveo (D) +0.7 (about 1,600 votes over Barbara Kirkmeyer) |
| 2024 House | Evans (R) +0.8 (under 2,500 votes over Caraveo) |
| 2024 president | Trump +2 (Biden had carried it by about 5 in 2020) |

Neither House winner reached 50% because third-party candidates drew votes. These are different electorates and candidates, so it isn't a simple linear trend.

## Candidates

### Gabe Evans (R)

He stresses public safety, border security, agricultural and energy production, veterans' healthcare and household costs. He has generally backed Trump's agenda, including the 2025 tax and spending law, but occasionally differs, such as calling for immigration enforcement to prioritize people convicted of or suspected of crimes over undocumented immigrants without criminal histories. He was unopposed in the Republican primary.

### Manny Rutinel (D)

Rutinel worked on legal matters involving corporate accountability and environmental protection, and in the legislature on economic policy, the environment and consumer protection. He emphasizes healthcare, Social Security and Medicare, housing affordability, immigration reform and oversight of the administration, plus stronger AI safeguards. He won the June 30 primary over state Rep. Shannon Bird with about 63%.

### Dave Wood (L)

His platform is limited government intervention in personal decisions and less U.S. military involvement abroad. He resisted the Vietnam draft in 1966, was jailed, and was pardoned in 1977. Third-party votes have been present in both prior elections, in which neither winner reached 50%.

Rutinel through September 30, Evans through October 14. Outside spending is about **$29.85 million** through October 22, with $22.39M in the general election and $7.46M in the primaries. The general-election money is nearly even: about $11.8M supporting Rutinel or opposing Evans and $10.6M the other way. The Republican-aligned Congressional Leadership Fund is the biggest at about $6M. Remarkable for a district that has existed for two election cycles.

## Policy differences and debates

Their only scheduled head-to-head debate was October 7 in Berthoud, covering immigration, affordability, healthcare and the Trump administration. They also did separate interviews at the University of Northern Colorado in Greeley on October 20.

| Issue | Evans (R) | Rutinel (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Backed the 2025 law; healthcare access and veterans' care | Opposes Medicaid and SNAP cuts; protect Medicare; affordability |
| Immigration | Stronger border enforcement; prioritize removing people involved in crime | Reform; protect Dreamers; path to citizenship for long-term undocumented residents |
| Energy | Oil and gas production; domestic supply; lower prices | Environmental protections; costs of energy and pollution |
| Housing | Reduce inflation and regulatory burdens | Expand affordable housing; cut household costs |
| Trump administration | Generally supportive, citing selected disagreements | Criticizes policy on immigration, tariffs and spending |

**Immigration.** A major issue around Denver. Evans draws on law-enforcement experience for tougher border and public-safety policy, yet has voiced concern about enforcement targeting undocumented people not accused of crimes. Rutinel backs reform and protections for established immigrant communities and criticizes the administration's enforcement. In a district with a large Hispanic population, that population is not a uniform constituency.

**The 2025 law.** Evans supports it and its tax and economic provisions; Rutinel says its Medicaid and food-aid changes threaten vulnerable households. The same fight shows up in [IA-3](../ia-03/), [WI-3](../wi-03/) and [PA-7](../pa-07/).

**Agriculture, energy and growth.** Weld County is a hub for farming and oil and gas, so debates extend to land use, water, energy, regulation and agricultural jobs.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Colorado's newest district.** It has already changed parties once, and no incumbent has yet won a second term; Evans is trying to be the first.
- **Suburban and rural together.** Adams County communities against Weld County show how metro growth meets farming and energy.
- **Razor-thin margins.** Both elections were decided by under 2,500 votes, which doesn't determine a new matchup.
- **A third-party vote.** Wood is on the ballot where third parties have left winners below 50%.
- **Money.** Nearly $30M in outside spending.
- **Compare [CO-5](../co-05/).** Both are Republican-held Colorado seats with national attention, but CO-5 is Colorado Springs and military; CO-8 is northern Denver suburbs, Greeley, agriculture and energy.
