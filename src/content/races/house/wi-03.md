Wisconsin's 3rd District, covering much of western and southwestern Wisconsin, is one of the country's most closely watched Republican-held House seats. It's a rematch between Republican incumbent Derrick Van Orden and Democrat Rebecca Cooke, who came within three points in 2024. Independents Alexander Valiensi Kent and Rustin Provance are also on the ballot.

A very recent development: on October 23 Cook moved WI-3 to Lean Democratic as part of a broader revision of its House ratings, while Inside Elections still calls it a Toss Up. These are outside assessments, not predictions.

## The district

WI-3 includes Eau Claire, La Crosse, Stevens Point and small towns, farms and rural counties along the Mississippi and toward central Wisconsin.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Eau Claire | University, healthcare, services, manufacturing, regional commerce |
| La Crosse | University, healthcare, river commerce, surrounding farms |
| Stevens Point | Higher education, insurance, manufacturing, paper-industry history |
| Rural western Wisconsin | Dairy, livestock, small manufacturing, forestry, tourism |
| Mississippi River communities | Agricultural trade, transportation, small industrial towns |

It isn't a suburban swing district; agriculture, small-town economies, manufacturing and the ties between rural areas and regional cities shape it. Western Wisconsin once backed Democrats with strong rural and labor ties, including longtime Rep. Ron Kind. The district voted for Obama in 2008 and 2012 but for Trump in 2016, 2020 and 2024. Cook's PVI is R+3.

## 2024

| 2024 House, WI-3 | Vote |
| --- | --- |
| Van Orden (R) | 51.3% |

About 2.7 points apart, while Trump carried the district by roughly seven. So the presidential and congressional electorates didn't divide identically even though Republicans won both.

## Candidates

### Derrick Van Orden (R)

A retired Navy SEAL senior chief, he first won in 2022 after Kind retired. He sits on the Agriculture, Armed Services and Veterans' Affairs committees, and emphasizes military and veterans' services, agriculture, border security, tax cuts and Trump's economic priorities. He presents his military service and rural Wisconsin roots as central qualifications and stresses mobilizing conservative voters who don't vote consistently.

### Rebecca Cooke (D)

Cooke grew up on a dairy farm near Eau Claire and has worked in nonprofit fundraising, runs a small business and still waits tables. She talks about her farm and working-class background on healthcare costs, farm policy, wages and affordability. She lost the 2022 primary, then was the nominee in 2024 and again in 2026, beating Eau Claire City Council President Emily Berge on August 11 by 60.5% to 39.4% (56,546 to 36,832 votes). She stresses reaching beyond Eau Claire, La Crosse and Stevens Point, saying towns of a few hundred people deserve as much attention. She explicitly rejects several progressive positions: sanctuary cities, an assault-weapons ban and broad student-loan forgiveness.

### The independents

Valiensi Kent (La Crosse) and Provance (Pepin) make it a four-candidate ballot, though polling doesn't show how much support either has.

## Money

| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooke | $11.96M | $901K |
| Van Orden | $8.79M | $2.12M |

Through September 30, 2026. Cooke raised about $3.2M more; Van Orden has more cash. Outside spending is bigger: about **$22.43 million** in independent expenditures through October 22 (about $22.01M in the general election), roughly $11.7M supporting Cooke or opposing Van Orden and $10.3M the other way. House Majority PAC is the largest general-election spender at about $5.6M. That's a major investment in a district of under 750,000 residents.

## The October 20 debate

At Chippewa Valley Technical College in Eau Claire, hosted by the Eau Claire Area Chamber of Commerce and WEAU. It was their first debate against each other and Van Orden's first congressional general-election debate since his 2020 campaign against Kind.

| Issue | Van Orden (R) | Cooke (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025 tax and spending law | Defended it and its tax cuts | Criticized Medicaid and SNAP changes and who benefits from the tax cuts |
| Tariffs | A tool for fairer trade | Broad tariffs cost farmers and consumers |
| Immigration | Stronger enforcement; undocumented farm workers leave, pay a fine and apply for visas | Border security plus a path to citizenship for qualifying immigrant workers |
| Rural healthcare | Federal rural-health funding from Republican legislation | The funding doesn't make up for Medicaid cuts and hospital financial strain |
| Iran war | Defended the military actions | Criticized the conflict and its economic costs |
| Education | Trades, STEM, interest-free federal student loans | Pell Grants, work-study, vocational options |

Both acknowledged immigrant labor's importance to dairy farms but proposed different routes to legal status.

## Local controversies

**Rural hospitals.** Western Wisconsin has lost HSHS Sacred Heart in Eau Claire and St. Joseph's in Chippewa Falls. Cooke ties healthcare trouble to federal funding decisions and Van Orden's vote for Medicaid cuts; Van Orden cites rural-health funding. The closures came before the 2025 law, so blaming it entirely would be chronologically wrong; the dispute is over how federal policy affects an already strained system.

**Data centers.** Residents worry about electricity, water, land development and utility costs, and a proposed Menomonie development was dropped after opposition. Each campaign tries to tie the other to data-center interests: Van Orden's highlights Cooke's past work with organizations that backed data centers, while Cooke and Democratic groups cite his industry-linked contributions and tax-incentive votes. Neither association shows how either would vote on a future proposal.

**AI videos.** On October 1, Wisconsin Public Radio reported that Cooke's attorneys sent Van Orden a cease-and-desist letter over AI-generated videos on his social-media account showing a synthetic Cooke saying things she didn't say. The videos carried an automatic label naming the tool. The letter alleges defamation; that is an allegation, not a finding.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A rural Democratic tradition against Republican presidential voting.** Like [ME-2](../me-02/) and parts of [PA-8](../pa-08/), the district has historically Democratic communities that now vote Republican for president.
- **A rural-identity Democrat.** Cooke's farm and small-business background parallels Jamie Ager in [NC-11](../nc-11/) and Matt Dunlap's appeal in ME-2.
- **A military-identity Republican.** Van Orden's SEAL background is central, alongside his record and Trump support.
- **A very expensive rematch.** Tens of millions in a district decided by under three points.
- **Bellwether.** UW–Green Bay political scientist Aaron Helpap told WPR it's a national bellwether given its competitive history and the narrow House.

## What to watch

More independent polling, updated outside-spending disclosures, and turnout in Eau Claire, La Crosse, Stevens Point and the small rural communities. The two independents are another reason for caution with two-candidate polls.
