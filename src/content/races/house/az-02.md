Arizona's 2nd District covers an enormous swath of northern and eastern Arizona, and Republican Rep. Eli Crane faces a rematch with Democrat Jonathan Nez, a former Navajo Nation president, whom he beat by about nine points in 2024. Trump carried the district by about 15. Nez has made the contest unexpectedly competitive: an October Emerson poll had him narrowly ahead, and Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections have all moved the race from Likely to Lean Republican.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eli Crane | Republican | Incumbent since 2023; former Navy SEAL (five wartime deployments); co-founder of Bottle Breacher; House Freedom Caucus |
| Jonathan Nez | Democrat | Navajo Nation president 2019–2023 (previously vice president); born in Tuba City, raised in Shonto |
| Curtis Goodwin | Libertarian | Limited federal government, school choice, individual liberties |

## The candidates

### Eli Crane (R)

Crane first won in 2022, beating Democratic incumbent Tom O'Halleran. A close Trump ally, he stresses border enforcement, spending limits, expanded resource development and opposition to federal environmental regulation. He was one of eight Republicans who voted to remove Speaker Kevin McCarthy in October 2023, which his campaign presents as independence from Washington's establishment.

### Jonathan Nez (D)

Nez has extensive experience with tribal governance, federal relations, infrastructure and water-rights negotiations. He lost his 2022 Navajo Nation reelection bid to Buu Nygren and lost to Crane by about nine points in 2024. His 2026 campaign stresses affordability, rural infrastructure, water security, healthcare, agricultural resilience and ethics, presenting himself as a pragmatic executive who can negotiate across party lines, including with the Trump administration while he led the Navajo Nation. If elected he would be the first Native American from Arizona in Congress, in a state with 22 federally recognized tribes.

### Curtis Goodwin (L)

Unlikely to win but could matter in a close plurality race.

## The district

AZ-2 covers all of Apache, Coconino, Gila and Navajo counties and parts of Graham, Maricopa, Mohave, Pinal and Yavapai counties: Flagstaff, Prescott, Payson, Show Low, Winslow, Holbrook and many tribal communities.

- **Flagstaff and Coconino County:** Northern Arizona University, environmental politics, tourism, education; relatively strong Democratic support.
- **Prescott and much of Yavapai County:** retirement communities, exurban growth, conservative voters, large Republican margins.
- **Navajo and Apache counties:** extensive Navajo Nation and other tribal lands, small towns, big differences between Native and non-Native precincts.
- **Gila County and the White Mountains:** forestry, mining, recreation, retirees, rural Republican voters.
- **Eastern Arizona:** agriculture, ranching, energy infrastructure, public lands, water-security concerns.

Native political participation can be decisive, but tribal communities aren't a monolithic bloc: turnout, candidate relationships and local issues differ among the Navajo Nation, Hopi communities, White Mountain Apache communities and others. Distances are huge, transportation and broadband limited in places, and language assistance and election services matter.

Crane's nine-point 2024 win was narrower than Trump's margin, so Nez has shown he can beat the Democratic presidential baseline, though winning would take much more.

Through October 14, 2026. Crane has about twice Nez's receipts, a large sum for an incumbent in a Trump +15 district, while Nez's $5.8M is also significant for a Democratic challenger there. Outside spending is about **$11.91 million** through October 31 (about $10.94M in the general election and $968K in primaries). Big spenders include No Going Back PAC, House Majority PAC, SOMOS PAC, Freedom Caucus Fund, Club for Growth Action and environmental groups. Per Axios, national Democrats have reportedly cut some planned spending here to focus on more promising districts, which suggests strategists still see it as a difficult pickup despite the October poll.

## The issues

**Water security and tribal sovereignty.** Arguably the most distinctive issue. Northern Arizona faces chronic scarcity, aging infrastructure, drought and complex disputes over Colorado River allocations and tribal water rights. Nez draws on water agreements he negotiated as Navajo Nation president and stresses reliable drinking water, tribal sovereignty, farm water and infrastructure. Crane stresses cutting federal obstacles to infrastructure and resource development while supporting state and local control. For many communities it's about running water at home, livestock, farming and whether settlements survive.

**Federal land, forests and wildfire.** Much of AZ-2 is federal or tribal land, so Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management decisions affect logging, grazing, recreation, wildfire and mining. Crane generally wants fewer federal restrictions and more aggressive resource development; Nez stresses conservation, wildfire resilience, infrastructure and consultation with tribal governments.

**Immigration and the border.** AZ-2 isn't mainly a border district, but the issue is big statewide. Crane strongly backs Trump's enforcement agenda: border barriers, surveillance, stricter enforcement. Nez stresses accountability, public safety and community economic needs while opposing the administration's more aggressive policies.

**Healthcare, housing and rural affordability.** Nez stresses healthcare, housing and utility costs and rural roads, bridges and services, which matter where people travel far for specialists, housing is scarce and utilities are costly to maintain. Crane stresses tax cuts, deregulation, energy production and private-sector development.

**Conduct and ethics.** Nez criticizes Crane's ideological confrontations and focus on national partisan fights; Crane presents his Freedom Caucus record, including the McCarthy removal, as willingness to challenge entrenched leadership. Nez offers executive experience, negotiation and local problem-solving.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A possible breakthrough in a Trump +15 district.** A Nez win would show the 2026 environment reaching deep into Republican territory.
- **A historic candidacy.** He'd be Arizona's first Native American in Congress, and tribal governance and federal–tribal relations are central to his record.
- **A Freedom Caucus test.** How a confrontational style fares outside the base in a difficult environment.
- **A rematch with a baseline.** His nine-point loss gives a reference for measuring change.
- **Money in an ostensibly safe seat.** Combined fundraising exceeds $17M, plus nearly $12M in outside spending, in a district first rated Likely Republican.
- **A varied coalition.** Large tribal nations, a university town, retirement communities and strongly Republican rural areas may respond differently to national politics.

## What to watch on election night

- **Flagstaff and Coconino County.** Nez needs strong Democratic margins and high participation.
- **Prescott and Yavapai County.** A reduction in Crane's usual margins here could change the districtwide result.
- **Navajo Nation and Apache County.** Turnout and margins in tribal communities, and precinct comparisons with 2024, may show whether Nez's local connections produced more support.
- **Gila County and the White Mountains.** A further test of whether Nez can improve on 2024 outside the Democratic strongholds.

## Assessment

AZ-2 has moved from relatively secure Republican to the outer tier of competitive House races. Crane has the stronger structural position: incumbency, Trump +15, about twice the fundraising and favorable forecasts. Nez has a strong personal connection to tribal communities, executive experience, a previous run against Crane and one poll showing him narrowly ahead. Lean Republican with a credible but difficult Democratic pickup chance is the most reasonable characterization; an upset would be nationally significant, but the polling is too limited to call Nez the favorite.
