Virginia's 1st District is a closely watched Republican-held seat. Longtime incumbent Rob Wittman faces Shannon Taylor, the Democratic commonwealth's attorney for Henrico County.

Three things make it distinctive: a redistricting dispute that ultimately left the existing map in place, a strong Democratic showing in Virginia's 2025 governor's race, and recent congressional polling far closer than Wittman's comfortable 2024 win.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rob Wittman | Republican | Incumbent since a 2007 special election; senior Armed Services member |
| Shannon Taylor | Democrat | Henrico County commonwealth's attorney since 2012; sought the 2025 attorney general nomination |

## The district

VA-1 stretches across central and eastern Virginia, linking part of the Richmond metro with the Northern Neck, Middle Peninsula and Historic Triangle. The largest voter concentrations are in Henrico, Chesterfield, Hanover and James City counties.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Henrico and Chesterfield portions | Growing Richmond suburbs; healthcare, professional services, commuting |
| Hanover County area | Suburban and exurban, north of Richmond |
| Northern Neck | Rural; agriculture, fisheries, tourism, retirees |
| Middle Peninsula | Small towns, maritime industries, military-connected jobs |
| Williamsburg / James City | Tourism, higher education, retirees, professionals |

Richmond-area suburbs share a representative with Chesapeake Bay communities over 100 miles away, so the issues range from suburban housing and healthcare costs to water quality, fisheries, farming and military installations.

**Redistricting.** Virginia voters approved a constitutional amendment in April 2026 allowing mid-decade redistricting, and a proposed map would have replaced much of VA-1 with a more Democratic configuration. A court order blocked certification and the map didn't take effect for 2026, so the race is run on the lines drawn after the 2020 census. Some earlier coverage describes a proposed district reaching toward Northern Virginia, which is not the district voting in November.

## Three different results

| Contest | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2024 House | Wittman R +12.5 |
| 2024 president | Trump R +5 |
| 2025 governor | Spanberger D +2.5 |

(Approximate district-level margins from the Richmond Times-Dispatch's October 2 analysis.) Wittman ran well ahead of Trump in 2024, yet the district then voted for Democrat Abigail Spanberger for governor. Voters respond differently to candidates and offices, which doesn't mean a trend can be projected mechanically into November. Cook's PVI is R+3.

## Candidates

### Rob Wittman (R)

Wittman served in the Virginia House of Delegates and worked in environmental health and conservation before Congress. He's a senior member of House Armed Services, has sought its chairmanship, and stresses shipbuilding, naval readiness, procurement and military families. He also campaigns on tax cuts, domestic energy, immigration enforcement and constituent service.

### Shannon Taylor (D)

Taylor has been Henrico County's elected prosecutor since 2012. She won a seven-candidate primary on August 4 over Salaam Bhatti, Jason Knapp and others. She emphasizes healthcare affordability, household costs, reproductive rights and ethics, with her prosecutorial experience front and center on accountability and public safety.

## The issues

| Issue | Wittman (R) | Taylor (D) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthcare | Cost transparency, telehealth, rural access | Restore enhanced ACA subsidies; rural-hospital funding; Medicare drug-price negotiation |
| Taxes | Republican tax cuts; business-oriented policy | Relief for working families and small business; no extra benefits for top earners |
| Abortion | Opposes abortion; supported overturning Roe | Restore federal abortion-rights protections |
| Defense | Investment, shipbuilding, seniority | Accountability and spending priorities alongside national security |
| Ethics | Supports restricting congressional stock trading | Stock-trading ban, term limits, limits on ex-members lobbying |

**Stock trading.** An October Associated Press report examined Taylor's accusations that Wittman has benefited financially in Congress. She makes trading and alleged self-enrichment central to her message. Wittman denies wrongdoing, says outside advisers manage his investments, attributes much of his wealth growth to inheritances, and has supported legislation restricting congressional stock trading. The allegations aren't a finding of illegal trading; the dispute is over whether ethics rules are adequate and how to read his disclosures. It parallels the issue in [PA-8](../pa-08/).

## Why it matters nationally

- **Decades of Republican representation.** Per the AP, the district hasn't elected a Democrat to Congress since the 1970s, so competitive ratings are historically noteworthy.
- **Virginia's 2025 shift.** Spanberger's showing is a more recent reference point than the 2024 presidential vote.
- **Two kinds of incumbency.** Wittman has nearly two decades in Congress and senior defense expertise; Taylor is an incumbent local prosecutor who hasn't served in Congress.
- **The map that wasn't.** The failed 2026 map decides which communities actually vote.
- **Money.** Over $12M in outside spending, mostly pro-Taylor or anti-Wittman.
- **Compare [PA-10](../pa-10/).** Longstanding Republican incumbents, Democrats stressing accountability and healthcare, and 2024 results more Republican than current polls; Wittman's 2024 margin was much bigger than Scott Perry's.
