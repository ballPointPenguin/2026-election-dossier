Indiana's 5th District has become a surprisingly competitive Republican defensive seat in the Indianapolis suburbs and east-central Indiana. Republican incumbent Victoria Spartz, a combative fiscal conservative with an unusually turbulent relationship with her own party, faces Democratic state Sen. J.D. Ford, an experienced legislator stressing affordability, healthcare and accountability.

Trump carried the district by about 16 points in 2024 and Republicans have held the area for decades. Even so, a Democratic-sponsored summer poll had Spartz up only two. Cook and Sabato now say Lean Republican (it was Likely Republican earlier in the fall), and Decision Desk HQ estimates an 82% chance of a Spartz win. The question is whether Ford can combine the suburbs' changing preferences with dissatisfaction over Spartz's style to produce an upset.

## The district

IN-5 lies north and northeast of Indianapolis, from some of Indiana's most affluent suburbs into smaller industrial cities and rural counties, with about 791,000 residents.

| Region | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Hamilton County suburbs (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville) | Affluent, highly educated, historically Republican, increasingly competitive |
| Anderson and Madison County | Manufacturing heritage, working-class, mixed urban-rural |
| Kokomo and Howard County | Auto manufacturing, industrial jobs, labor history |
| Muncie and Delaware County | Ball State University, healthcare, manufacturing, Republican rural surroundings |
| Marion and Grant County | Industrial history, smaller-city economic challenges, conservative surrounding communities |

Suburban voters who've become less reliably Republican nationally sit alongside industrial and rural communities where Republicans have consolidated. The 2021 redistricting made the seat much safer for Republicans than in 2020, so Ford needs more than a strong Carmel and Fishers: he also has to limit Republican margins in the smaller cities and rural areas.

## Candidates

### Victoria Spartz (R)

She was born in Soviet Ukraine, immigrated in 2000, served in the Indiana Senate and was elected to Congress in 2020. Her identity combines strong fiscal conservatism with a confrontational approach to party leadership: she has repeatedly criticized spending, federal debt and dysfunctional governance. Her career has also been unusually unsettled: she announced she wouldn't seek reelection, reversed that, discussed a three-term limit on herself and raised the possibility of leaving Congress, which has made her one of the House Republican Conference's less predictable members. Her 2026 priorities are healthcare reform, cutting federal debt, and making homeownership and opportunity more attainable. She generally backs Republican fiscal and immigration policy but has sometimes criticized the Trump administration or party leaders over spending and institutions. Independence can be an asset with some voters and a liability with those expecting consistent constituent service and legislative effectiveness.

### J.D. Ford (D)

He won reelection to the state Senate in 2022. His campaign stresses affordability, healthcare, public education, reproductive rights and accountability, in a less confrontational style, pointing to his legislative record as evidence he can work across divisions. He has won competitive Indianapolis-suburb races, though his state Senate district is only part of the much larger congressional district. He came out of a seven-candidate Democratic primary.

### The Republican primary

Spartz faced attorney Scott King in the May 5 primary.

A 20-point win is decisive, but about four in ten Republican primary voters preferred someone else. That doesn't mean they'll defect in November, but it shows a real constituency of Republican dissatisfaction.

Through October 14, 2026. Spartz has the conventional fundraising edge, but outside spending runs the other way: about **$5.52 million** in independent expenditures through October 31 (about $5.49M in the general election), roughly $4.2M helping Ford or opposing Spartz versus $1.3M the other way, more than three times as much. The largest spender is Invest in Tomorrow Coalition PAC at about $2M. This helps explain why a seat considered safe earlier in the cycle has become a real Democratic target. Independent groups operate separately from campaign committees, so that money isn't available to the candidates.

## The issues

**Affordability, healthcare, childcare.** Ford stresses housing, utility bills, healthcare, childcare and elder care, and wants limits on large corporate home purchases, lower household energy costs and better access to medical services. Spartz names healthcare reform, debt reduction and economic opportunity, and argues excess spending and government dysfunction raise costs. They agree affordability matters but differ sharply on whether federal intervention or fiscal restraint is the remedy.

**Tariffs, manufacturing, agriculture.** The district has manufacturing communities, auto jobs and farm areas exposed to tariffs, supply chains and export markets. Ford says Trump's tariffs raise consumer costs and hurt farmers who depend on trade. Spartz stresses manufacturing competitiveness, energy policy and fiscal discipline, more supportive of the administration's direction.

**Immigration and elections.** Spartz backs stricter immigration enforcement and stronger proof-of-citizenship rules for federal elections. Ford stresses protecting access to voting and criticizes proposals he says could burden eligible voters.

**Data centers and electricity.** Data-center growth and the power infrastructure it needs is increasingly prominent locally. Both candidates have addressed development, utility costs and community effects. Ford has criticized Spartz's record on data-center policy and raised questions about her financial disclosures; she rejects accusations of wrongdoing and disputes his account of her finances. The allegations are central to the campaign's advertising, but disputed claims aren't established findings.

**Congressional conduct and stock trading.** Ford attacks Spartz over financial transactions and stock trading in her congressional records; she disputes his characterization and says his campaign is misleading voters. The contrast: Ford stresses institutional accountability and constituent service, Spartz an outspoken fiscal watchdog willing to challenge leaders.

## Redistricting

Unlike several other states, Indiana didn't enact a new congressional map for 2026. In December 2025 lawmakers rejected a Trump-backed effort to redraw the districts to further favor Republicans. Spartz supported the proposal; Ford, in the state Senate, opposed it. The 2022 and 2024 boundaries stay in place, which already made IN-5 more Republican after the 2020 census.

## Why it matters nationally

- **The reach of a Democratic wave.** A Ford win would be a major overperformance in a Trump +17 district, not a routine pickup of a marginal seat.
- **Suburban change.** Carmel, Fishers and Noblesville show whether Republican support among affluent, college-educated suburbanites keeps eroding.
- **An unconventional incumbent.** Her confrontational style, changing plans and colleague conflicts make her unlike a conventional incumbent.
- **A possible breakthrough.** Republicans have held the area since the early 1990s.
- **Experienced legislators.** Both have records voters can evaluate, unlike many 2026 battlegrounds.
- **A money asymmetry.** Spartz raised more directly; Ford has far more outside help.

FLIPR lists the Quantus poll as 634 LV with Spartz 47%, Ford 45%, a different cut from the 601-LV topline above. In the Quantus poll 9.8% were undecided and 3.3% other. It found Spartz favorability 36.9% positive and 49.0% negative, Ford 36.1% and 37.5% (with more voters unfamiliar with him), Trump job approval 44.7% positive and 54.4% negative, and independents preferring Ford 39% to 32% among those with a preference. The 3.2-point lead is within sampling error. All three polls put Spartz within one to three points despite a Trump +17 district, but the polls are few, two are Democratic-sponsored, and undecided voters could break toward the district's usual Republican preference. The ratings suggest forecasters see vulnerability but not enough to overcome strong Republican fundamentals.

## What to watch on election night

- **Hamilton County: Carmel, Fishers and Noblesville.** The decisive battleground. Ford needs to win or come very close in these populous suburbs, not just trim Spartz's margins.
- **Anderson and Madison County.** Whether Ford can improve Democratic support in an industrial, working-class region.
- **Kokomo and Howard County.** A test of the candidates' economic messages on tariffs, jobs and affordability.
- **Muncie and Delaware County.** University precincts and urban areas, against Republican rural surroundings.

## Assessment

The most recent quantitative forecast gives Spartz about an 82% chance of victory, consistent with a race where an upset is possible but not the expected result. IN-5 is worth watching as an indicator of whether Democrats can expand the 2026 map beyond conventional swing districts, particularly by combining suburban gains with dissatisfaction toward a polarizing Republican incumbent.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
