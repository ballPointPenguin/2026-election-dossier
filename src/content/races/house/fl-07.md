Florida's 7th District is an unexpectedly competitive open Republican seat in the Orlando area. The incumbent, Republican Cory Mills, lost his own party's August primary after a string of controversies and ethics investigations.

The general election pits Republican Ryan Elijah, a former television news anchor, against Democrat Bale Dalton, a Navy veteran and former NASA chief of staff, with Libertarian Christopher Dennison also on the ballot. The district stays Republican-leaning, but an October Democratic-sponsored poll had the candidates tied at 45%, and Cook reportedly moved it from Likely to Lean Republican on October 30. Other forecasters still say Likely Republican. Both nominees are newcomers to Congress, and the district's boundaries were changed in Florida's 2026 redistricting.

## The district

The new FL-7 holds all of Seminole County and southern Volusia County, northeast of Orlando: Sanford, Lake Mary, Altamonte Springs, Oviedo and Winter Springs, plus southern Volusia communities around Deltona and DeBary. It's largely suburban and exurban: established neighborhoods, growing developments, technology and professional jobs, and commuters across metro Orlando.

Seminole County has many college-educated suburban voters, including independents and historically Republican voters who've become more willing to back Democrats in some elections. Southern Volusia mixes working-class, retirement and commuter communities with generally stronger Republican tendencies.

## The Republican primary

Mills, first elected in 2022, became embroiled in allegations of personal misconduct and financial improprieties, and the House Ethics Committee opened investigations including into possible campaign-finance violations. Mills denied wrongdoing, and contemporaneous reporting didn't show him charged with a crime over those allegations. His support among influential Republicans eroded: Gov. Ron DeSantis publicly declined to back him, and Republican Reps. Anna Paulina Luna and Mike Haridopolos backed Elijah. Trump, who had earlier endorsed Mills, left him off a later endorsement list and endorsed Elijah after the primary.

Elijah won the August 18 primary by about 13 points, ending Mills' career after two terms.

The result cuts both ways. For Republicans, replacing Mills removed an incumbent whose controversies might have complicated the general election; for Democrats, it opened a seat without incumbency's advantages and let Dalton run against a less-established nominee. Beating Mills isn't by itself evidence Republicans will lose FL-7.

## Candidates

### Ryan Elijah (R)

His background includes consumer-advocacy reporting, community-board service and local civic organizations, and he has never held elected office. He stresses fiscal restraint, domestic energy, deregulation, tax cuts, border security and public safety. He supports much of the Trump administration's economic agenda but, in a September WKMG interview, acknowledged tariffs can raise consumer costs while arguing they may encourage domestic manufacturing. On healthcare he wants more price transparency, competition and regulatory reform, and he says the federal government should generally defer to state and local authorities on siting data centers.

### Bale Dalton (D)

His service includes Afghanistan, Iraq and rescue operations after Hurricane Katrina. He stresses affordability, healthcare access, veterans' services, reproductive rights, housing costs and ethics. He opposes tariffs as raising costs for families and small businesses, criticizes the expiration of Affordable Care Act subsidies, wants more housing construction and limits on large institutional investors buying single-family homes, and wants local control over data centers with protections against higher utility bills and heavy water use.

**A crossover endorsement.** On October 15 former Republican U.S. Sen. Mel Martinez, senator 2005–2009 and George W. Bush's HUD secretary, endorsed Dalton, citing long personal knowledge of him and his military service. It gives Dalton a recognizable Central Florida Republican's backing, though it doesn't show how many Republicans will cross over.

## The issues

**Affordability, housing and insurance.** Probably the defining local issue: housing costs, property-insurance premiums, utility bills and transportation. Dalton backs housing construction, limits on institutional home purchases and more oversight of insurers; Elijah stresses fewer regulatory burdens, domestic energy and lower taxes. Congress can't directly control Florida's insurance market, but disaster policy, insurance regulation proposals, tax policy and housing legislation offer avenues.

**Healthcare.** Dalton stresses the expiration of ACA subsidies, rising premiums and coverage access; Elijah favors competition and transparency in healthcare markets and measures to reduce premiums.

**Data centers and energy.** Both address data centers' electricity and water demands. Elijah favors state and local decisions; Dalton wants explicit community consent and a guarantee residents won't bear higher utility costs, especially relevant given Central Florida's technology and aerospace sectors.

**Tariffs and the economy.** Elijah backs the administration's approach but acknowledges short-term consumer costs; Dalton opposes tariffs as raising construction, household and small-business costs. It's more nuanced than pro versus anti, but they disagree on whether the benefits justify the costs.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A Republican incumbent beaten in a primary.** One of the more unusual open-seat contests of 2026.
- **Suburban Republican strength.** Seminole County and the Orlando suburbs test whether the national environment is reaching Republican-leaning metro districts.
- **A veteran versus a media personality.** Both have nontraditional biographies and no elected-office experience.
- **A Republican endorsing a Democrat.** Martinez's backing shows the potential of crossover voters, with uncertain effect.
- **A possible pickup in a Trump +12 district.** A Dalton win would be a meaningful overperformance.
- **Florida's mid-decade redistricting.** Changed boundaries complicate comparisons with earlier House races.

## Election-night indicators

- **Lake Mary, Oviedo and the Seminole suburbs.** Dalton's ability to attract college-educated suburban voters and Republican crossover support is key.
- **Sanford and central Seminole County.** Turnout across more diverse communities could affect the countywide margin.
- **Deltona and southern Volusia.** Republican margins and working-class and suburban turnout are the counterweight to Seminole.

## Assessment

FL-7 is a real Democratic pickup chance, though Republicans keep the advantage. Fundamentals favor Elijah: the district went comfortably Republican in 2024, Florida has shifted right, and he has Trump and leading state Republicans behind him. Dalton has more money, military and federal experience, a Republican crossover endorsement and a public poll showing a tie. The scarcity of polling is the main limit: a single Democratic-sponsored survey can't show the race is truly even. Lean Republican seems reasonable, with a wider uncertainty range than in better-polled districts.
