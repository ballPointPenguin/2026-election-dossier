The crucial point in California's 48th District is that this is an open seat, not a Darrell Issa reelection: Issa is retiring, and California's 2025 Proposition 50 redistricting substantially changed the constituency. The old district voted for Trump by roughly 15 points in 2024; the new boundaries would have voted for Harris by about three.

The candidates are Democrat Marni von Wilpert, a San Diego City Council member, and Republican Jim Desmond, a San Diego County supervisor. Both are established local officeholders, not congressional incumbents, and CalMatters describes their approaches as relatively centrist compared with some of the more ideologically distinctive candidates elsewhere in California this year.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marni von Wilpert | Democrat | San Diego City Council since 2020; former prosecutor; Obama administration alumna; backed by Kamala Harris and Sen. Adam Schiff |
| Jim Desmond | Republican | San Diego County supervisor; former San Marcos mayor; former airline pilot; Trump-endorsed |

## The district

The new CA-48 straddles San Diego and Riverside counties, with Oceanside, Fallbrook, Temecula and Palm Springs: coastal, inland, suburban and desert populations in one district.

| Area | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Oceanside and North County San Diego | Military families, tourism, coastal development, housing affordability |
| Fallbrook and Temecula | Suburban and exurban; agriculture, wineries, commuting |
| Palm Springs and Coachella Valley portions | Tourism, hospitality, retirees, LGBTQ communities, desert water and energy |
| Inland communities | Healthcare, construction, small business, transportation |

Proposition 50 added more Democratic-voting territory to what had been Issa's heavily Republican district, creating a coalition neither candidate has represented in its entirety. That makes comparisons with Issa's 2024 result (59.3%) misleading without adjusting for the map. The estimated presidential margin shifted about 18 points toward Democrats, a change in the electorate, not in individual voters.

## Candidates

### Marni von Wilpert (D)

She won a council district that had previously elected Republicans. She stresses affordability, healthcare, housing, abortion rights and accountability, criticizes Trump's tariffs and the Iran war as pressures on household finances, and highlights her work on fentanyl enforcement and addiction treatment, treating public safety as not exclusively a Republican issue.

### Jim Desmond (R)

He emphasizes lower taxes, fewer regulations, lower energy costs and more housing supply through faster permitting. He has Trump's endorsement but has publicly disagreed with the president, including over remarks about the consequences of Iranian attacks on American cities. He presents himself as a fiscal conservative focused on local issues who would act independently of the president when district interests require.

Both emphasize practical governing experience; they disagree on federal regulation, taxation, immigration enforcement and the Trump administration.

## The June primary

## The issues

**Housing and affordability.** Both name housing costs a major issue. Desmond stresses streamlining permitting and cutting regulations and fees, and has backed limits on large investors buying homes. Von Wilpert stresses faster affordable-housing development, cutting household expenses and addressing tariffs. They differ over which federal policies best cut costs and increase supply.

**Immigration.** The region is tied to the border. Desmond backs stronger enforcement and has opposed some local limits on cooperation with federal immigration authorities. Von Wilpert supports border security but criticizes aggressive federal enforcement and favors stronger legal protections for immigrant communities. The Los Angeles Times voter guide calls it one of the clearest disagreements.

**Energy, climate, infrastructure.** Coastal North County faces housing and transportation pressure; inland communities face wildfire, heat, water and electricity costs. Desmond stresses lower energy prices and opposing infrastructure he considers needlessly expensive, including a proposed power line in the Temecula area. Von Wilpert stresses clean energy and environmental protections while addressing utility costs. These matter especially around Palm Springs for summer power demand and desert water.

**The Iran war.** Von Wilpert criticizes the military campaign and supports congressional efforts to end it. Desmond has supported Trump politically but publicly rejected remarks suggesting American cities could be treated as expendable. It touches the district's military communities, security concerns and fuel prices.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A direct test of Proposition 50.** A roughly 18-point shift toward Democrats under the new map.
- **An open Republican-held seat.** Issa's retirement means neither candidate is the sitting representative.
- **Local governing experience on both sides.** A city council member against a county supervisor, not first-time politicians.
- **Conventional party differences.** CalMatters calls it a mainstream Democrat against a fiscally conservative Republican, not insurgent movements.
- **Very different communities.** North County coast, Temecula suburbs and Palm Springs put distinct interests in one district.
- **Part of California's redistricting story.** Along with [CA-22](../ca-22/), it shows how Proposition 50 changed Republican-held seats.
