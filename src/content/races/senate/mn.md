Minnesota's Senate race is an open-seat contest following Democratic Sen. Tina Smith's retirement. Minnesota hasn't voted Republican for president since 1972, but it isn't overwhelmingly Democratic: Trump lost it by only about four points in 2024, and Republicans remain competitive in the legislature and several congressional districts. The national environment and the candidates' profiles have nonetheless made Flanagan the clear favorite.

It's also historic: Flanagan would be the first Native American woman elected to the U.S. Senate.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peggy Flanagan | DFL | Lieutenant governor since 2019; former state representative; member of the White Earth Nation of Ojibwe; progressive associated with labor, education, child welfare, reproductive rights and economic equity |
| Michele Tafoya | Republican | Former NFL sideline reporter and four-time Emmy winner turned conservative commentator; won the August primary after bypassing the state GOP convention endorsement; runs on accountability, public-program fraud, fiscal restraint, public safety and affordability |
| Rebecca Whiting | Libertarian | Army veteran and small-business owner |
| Marisa Simonetti | Independent | Real estate professional |

Republican Alycia Gruenhagen is running as a write-in candidate to Tafoya's right.

## The Democratic primary

Flanagan beat Rep. Angie Craig about 59% to 40% in August despite Craig's fundraising and reputation as a proven suburban winner. The primary was a proxy for a broader Democratic debate: Craig the centrist, electorally pragmatic House member who had won tough races in MN-2; Flanagan the progressive with Bernie Sanders's and Elizabeth Warren's endorsements. Immigration was a major factor. Craig's support for tougher immigration legislation became damaging during the federal enforcement surge in Minnesota, while Flanagan was more forceful in opposing the administration. DFL primary voters chose an assertive progressive over the perceived safest nominee.

## Minnesota's geography and durability

| Region | Importance |
| --- | --- |
| Minneapolis / Hennepin County | Major DFL vote reservoir; strongly progressive |
| Saint Paul / Ramsey County | Heavily Democratic, diverse urban communities |
| Suburban Hennepin, Dakota, Washington | Crucial persuadable voters, increasingly Democratic |
| Rochester / Olmsted County | Educated, health-care-centered electorate trending Democratic |
| Duluth / St. Louis County | Historically labor-Democratic, though the Iron Range has shifted right |
| St. Cloud / Central Minnesota | Competitive to Republican, culturally conservative |
| Western and southwestern Minnesota | Increasingly strongly Republican |
| Northern rural and Iron Range | Historically DFL, with substantial Republican realignment |

Flanagan needs the large Hennepin and Ramsey margins plus strong suburban support; Tafoya needs big Greater Minnesota margins and real suburban inroads, hard in an unfavorable national climate.

Minnesota has voted Democratic in every presidential election since 1976, the longest such streak of any state, but that overstates its partisan security:

| Election | Democratic margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2016 president | D+1.5 |
| 2020 president | D+7.1 |
| 2024 president | D+4.2 |
| 2020 U.S. Senate | D+5.2 |

A strong enough Republican could plausibly win in good conditions. Republicans' bigger problem is history: they haven't won any statewide race in Minnesota since Tim Pawlenty's reelection as governor in 2006.

## The campaign's defining issues

- **Fraud and the Walz administration.** Arguably Tafoya's best issue. Minnesota has had several high-profile fraud scandals in public programs, including the Feeding Our Future case and investigations of Medicaid-funded services, and Tafoya says Flanagan, as lieutenant governor, shares responsibility for weak oversight. Flanagan acknowledges the state should have acted faster and backs stronger oversight and enforcement, but rejects the idea that these failures justify electing a Republican senator. It gives Tafoya a locally resonant argument less dependent on Trump's national popularity.
- **Immigration enforcement.** The administration's crackdown in Minnesota, including a controversial federal enforcement surge earlier in 2026, is central. Flanagan says the administration overstepped and endangered immigrant communities, and supports dismantling ICE and replacing its enforcement structure. Tafoya favors stronger enforcement but has criticized aspects of the administration's tactics in Minnesota. The dispute also covers federal authority, civil liberties and how Minnesota communities are treated.
- **Tafoya's attempt at moderation.** Her broadcasting career gives her name recognition, and she presents as an independent-minded conservative rather than a conventional MAGA candidate, with relatively moderate positions on abortion and some gun-policy questions and skepticism about parts of the Iran war. Trump endorsed her in October, a tension: she needs Republican enthusiasm without alienating anti-Trump suburban voters.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A defensive seat, not a top Republican target.** Holding it preserves Democrats' Senate position and would mark a historic breakthrough for Indigenous representation, and show a progressive can win statewide in a competitive Upper Midwest state.
- **A Tafoya win would be big for Republicans:** it would end a 20-year statewide losing streak, flip a Senate seat and show they can regain suburban Midwest ground.
- **Compare with the top of the ticket.** Sen. Amy Klobuchar is running for governor against Republican Lisa Demuth, and her popularity is a benchmark. If Flanagan runs well behind her, the Senate race is being shaped by candidate-specific weaknesses, not just the partisan environment.
- **The House race on the same ballot:** [MN-1](../../house/mn-01/).

## What to watch

Polls close at 8 p.m. Central, 9 p.m. Eastern. Watch Hennepin and Ramsey for Democratic turnout and margins, the suburban counties for Tafoya's crossover appeal, and the Iron Range and rural southern Minnesota for Republican strength. How Flanagan, Klobuchar and Attorney General Keith Ellison each run will also show Minnesota's coalitions.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
