Maine is one of the most consequential Senate contests of 2026. Five-term Republican Sen. Susan Collins has spent decades cultivating a reputation for moderation and independence, while Democrats argue that even moderate Republicans have enabled the Trump administration's agenda. Unlike Kansas or Iowa, where Democrats must overcome a big Republican presidential edge, Maine voted for Kamala Harris in 2024, yet Collins has repeatedly won over voters who otherwise back Democrats. The question is whether her personal brand can survive another nationalized election.

The race changed in the summer: Graham Platner, an oyster farmer and military veteran running an economically populist campaign, won the June Democratic primary but withdrew in July following controversies around his campaign. Former Maine Senate president Troy Jackson replaced him on the ballot.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Troy Jackson | Democrat | Challenger; former logger and labor advocate from Allagash in northern Maine; former president of the Maine Senate; stresses workers' rights, health care, affordability and curbing corporate influence |
| Susan Collins | Republican | Incumbent since 1997 seeking a sixth term; chair of Senate Appropriations; known for institutionalism, constituent services and occasional breaks with leadership |

Jackson shares Platner's working-class northern Maine identity but brings a long legislative record, union ties and familiarity with state institutions. His late start was still a real organizational handicap: about 100 days to run a general-election campaign against one of the Senate's most experienced incumbents.

## Why Maine is distinctive

Maine leans Democratic for president but has a strong independent streak. It elected independent governors Angus King and James Longley, and King is now an independent senator who caucuses with Democrats. Collins belongs to a New England moderate-Republican tradition that has mostly disappeared elsewhere. She first won in 1996 and has survived several Democratic waves, notably in 2020, when she beat Sara Gideon by about nine points while Joe Biden carried Maine by about nine, an 18-point split rare in this polarized era.

Jackson's logger-and-labor background could help with rural voters national Democrats struggle to reach. It isn't just progressive Portland against conservative rural Maine; he's trying to build a coalition across that divide.

## Collins's dilemma

Collins has positioned herself as an institutionalist and independent voice. She voted to convict Trump in his second impeachment trial after January 6, 2021, and has periodically opposed his policies and nominees. Democrats counter that her decisive votes on taxes, judges and Republican leadership helped advance his agenda.

The most durable controversy is her 2018 vote to confirm Brett Kavanaugh. She said he respected precedent, but the Court overturned Roe v. Wade in 2022, which for abortion-rights supporters badly damaged her credibility as a moderate. Collins says her seniority, especially as Appropriations chair, lets her deliver federal investment to Maine, an argument that has long worked with voters who might otherwise prefer Democrats.

## Jackson's strengths and weaknesses

Jackson argues Collins's moderation isn't enough when federal spending, health care, reproductive rights and the administration are at stake. His labor and legislative record give him credibility on working-class economics, and he stresses housing, health costs and workers' rights. Collins criticizes his temperament and confrontational style, and he had to build a statewide campaign quickly. The nomination switch cuts both ways: it removed a candidate burdened by serious controversies, but disrupted organization and let Collins portray Democrats as divided.

## Ranked-choice voting

Maine uses ranked-choice voting for federal elections. If nobody tops 50% of first choices, last-place candidates are eliminated and their ballots transfer by next preference. Qualified write-ins are on record, but Collins and Jackson dominate. If the first-choice count is close, tabulation could delay the result. That differs from Georgia, where missing a majority triggers a separate runoff.

## Why it matters nationally

- **The most obvious Democratic pickup.** It's the only Republican-held Senate seat up in a state Harris carried, so flipping it is more straightforward than winning Kansas, Iowa or Alaska. Collins's record shows presidential partisanship alone isn't a good predictor.
- **Close to essential.** Losing Maine would force Democrats to find another, harder Republican seat for the four-seat net gain they need.
- **A symbol.** A Collins defeat would mark the near-extinction of moderate New England Republicanism in the Senate.
- **The House race on the same ballot:** [ME-2](../../house/me-02/).

## What to watch

Maine's polls close at 8 p.m. Eastern. Watch Jackson's margins around Portland, whether he improves Democratic performance in working-class northern communities, and how much crossover support Collins keeps among Democrats and independents; her history of outrunning Republican presidential candidates makes those voters especially important.

The governor's race, between Democrat Hannah Pingree, Republican Bobby Charles and independent Rick Bennett, is a useful comparison. If Jackson badly trails Pingree, Collins's personal appeal may again decide it.
