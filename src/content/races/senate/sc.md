South Carolina has become one of the more interesting Senate races of 2026. What began as a routine reelection campaign for Republican Sen. Lindsey Graham changed with his death on July 11, after which Gov. Henry McMaster appointed his sister, Darline Graham, to the seat. The contest now pits the appointed incumbent against Democratic physician Annie Andrews in a state Donald Trump carried by about 18 points in 2024.

Polling shows a nearly even race. Republicans remain favored because of the state's conservative electorate, but a Democratic win is no longer inconceivable.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Darline Graham | Republican | Appointed incumbent; little previous electoral or legislative experience; won a contentious runoff over Rep. Ralph Norman; endorsed by Trump; campaigns on conservative economic, immigration and national-security policy |
| Annie Andrews | Democrat | Pediatrician and children's-health advocate; 2022 congressional candidate against Nancy Mace; campaigns on health care, affordability, reproductive rights, gun-violence prevention and opposition to the Washington establishment |

Also on the ballot are Libertarian Kasie Whitener and Constitution Party candidate Mark Hackett. Catherine Fleming Bruce is running as a Democratic write-in.

## The Republican succession

Lindsey Graham, a central figure in Republican foreign and national-security policy, had served in the Senate since 2003. McMaster's appointment of his younger sister was first seen as a placeholder, but she surprised observers by seeking a full term. Trump endorsed her immediately, and she beat Rep. Ralph Norman in the August 25 Republican runoff, 52.6% to 47.4%.

The result showed two vulnerabilities: nearly half of Republican primary voters preferred someone else, and her candidacy raised questions about dynastic succession and whether family ties were standing in for political experience. In a debate she struggled with a question about Taiwan and the South China Sea and acknowledged national security wasn't her area of expertise, a conspicuous weakness for the successor to Lindsey Graham.

Andrews needs heavy Black turnout, strength among younger and suburban voters and crossover from white independents. Graham's path is conventional: hold Republican margins in the Upstate, Horry County and the fast-growing conservative suburbs.

## The historical challenge

South Carolina hasn't elected a Democratic senator since Fritz Hollings's last win in 1998, nor a Democratic governor since Jim Hodges that same year. Lindsey Graham won reelection in 2020 by about 10 points despite enormous Democratic spending for Jaime Harrison, a warning against assuming close polling means a Democratic win.

## The issues

- **Affordability** is Andrews's strongest argument: household costs, health care and the claim that Washington politicians are too tied to wealthy interests, with her pediatric background connecting to children's health and family finances.
- **Trump** cuts both ways for Graham. His endorsement won her the nomination, but some Norman supporters resent it, and independents dissatisfied with the administration may balk at his preferred candidate.
- **Foreign policy and Iran.** Lindsey Graham was an outspoken advocate of military action against Iran. Darline Graham has supported the administration but hasn't established independent national-security credentials.
- **AI and economic development** came up in the candidates' debate, touching on technology investment, jobs and the effects of federal policy.

## The governor's race

South Carolina also elects a governor, with Republican Attorney General Alan Wilson facing Democratic state Rep. Jermaine Johnson. An October Siena survey had Wilson ahead 51–44 while the Senate race was tied 48–48. That seven-point gap suggests Graham's problems are partly candidate-specific, not a wholesale Democratic realignment. If Wilson wins comfortably while Andrews wins or comes close, the contrast will underline how unusual the Senate race is.

## Why it matters nationally

- **A major surprise if Andrews wins,** and a blow to Republican efforts to keep the Senate.
- **A first either way.** The winner will be the first woman elected to the U.S. Senate from South Carolina, and Andrews would be the state's first Democratic senator elected in 28 years.
- **A narrow Graham win would still say something:** the Republican coalition stays resilient even with an inexperienced nominee in a good Democratic year.
- **The House race on the same ballot:** [SC-1](../../house/sc-01/), the open Lowcountry seat.

## What to watch

South Carolina polls close at 7 p.m. Eastern. Watch Democratic turnout in Richland, Orangeburg and other heavily Black counties, Andrews's margins around Charleston, and whether Graham underperforms Republican gubernatorial nominee Wilson in the Upstate. Minor-party candidates could matter if the leaders finish within a point or two. South Carolina elects senators by plurality, so no majority or runoff is required in the general election.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
