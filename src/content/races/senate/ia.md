Iowa is one of the more surprising Senate battlegrounds of 2026. Republican Sen. Joni Ernst's retirement opened the seat for Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson and Democratic state Rep. Josh Turek, a Paralympic gold medalist. What began as a fairly safe Republican seat is now essentially tied, even though Trump carried Iowa by about 13 points in 2024.

The Democratic resurgence is happening in a state that has moved sharply right over a decade. Economic dissatisfaction, agricultural tariffs and opposition to the Iran war have made the environment unusually favorable to Democrats.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Josh Turek | Democrat | Two-term state representative from Council Bluffs; former professional wheelchair basketball player with two Paralympic golds; campaigns on affordability, health care, agriculture and working-class economics |
| Ashley Hinson | Republican | Three-term congresswoman from northeastern Iowa; former TV news anchor and state legislator; strong Trump ally stressing conservative economics, border security, agriculture and constituent service |

## How Iowa got here

Iowa is almost the inverse of Georgia: once a competitive Midwestern state, now increasingly Republican.

| Presidential election | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 2008 | D+9.5 |
| 2012 | D+5.8 |
| 2016 | R+9.4 |
| 2020 | R+8.2 |
| 2024 | R+13.2 |

Obama carried Iowa twice. Since then Republicans have taken nearly every statewide office, both Senate seats and all four House seats. With both Ernst and Gov. Kim Reynolds retiring, neither of Iowa's top two statewide races has an incumbent, for the first time since 1968.

## Farms, tariffs and the Iran war

Iowa's farm economy is unusually exposed to trade and energy costs. Farmers face expensive diesel, fertilizer and equipment, while tariffs and retaliatory restrictions have disrupted export markets, and the Iran war has pushed energy prices up further. That lets Turek run against the administration's economic policies without leaning on national cultural messaging. He is trying to rebuild the rural, working-class coalition associated with former Sen. Tom Harkin, not just mobilize college-educated suburbanites.

## Turek

Born with spina bifida, Turek became an internationally successful wheelchair basketball player and won Paralympic gold medals in 2016 and 2020. He then won a Republican-leaning Iowa House district. His campaign calls its philosophy "prairie populism": agricultural cooperatives, labor organizing, economic fairness and skepticism of concentrated corporate power.

He beat fellow Democrat Zach Wahls in the June primary, about 63% to 37%. That mattered because Turek stresses his ability to win Republicans, while Wahls was more tied to Iowa's liberal activists. Barack Obama has endorsed him, and Harkin has campaigned with him.

## Hinson

Hinson is a strong candidate. Years anchoring the news in Cedar Rapids gave her high name recognition in eastern Iowa, she has won competitive House races, and she's shown she can appeal past the most conservative voters. She has adapted to a hard national environment by stressing bipartisanship and occasionally distancing herself from Trump on trade and energy. At their October debate she cited legislation she'd worked on with Democrats and said she could cooperate with Democratic Senate leadership, while Turek tried to tie her to Trump's unpopular economic policies. Both are trying to rise above their parties' national reputations.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Possibly indispensable.** Democrats need a net gain of four seats, and Iowa would take one off the Republican side of the ledger.
- **The rural Midwest question.** Can Democrats rebuild a competitive coalition there, or are Trump-era Republican gains entrenched? A Turek win in a state that voted well to the right of the nation in 2024 would suggest an economic-populist message can reach alienated voters; a Hinson win in a bad Republican year would show the realignment's durability.
- **The House races on the same ballot:** [IA-1](../../house/ia-01/), [IA-2](../../house/ia-02/) and [IA-3](../../house/ia-03/).

## What to watch

Iowa polls close at 8 p.m. Central, 9 p.m. Eastern.

- **Polk County (Des Moines):** Turek needs strong turnout and large margins.
- **Linn and Johnson (Cedar Rapids, Iowa City):** crucial, though Hinson's eastern Iowa roots help her.
- **Scott, Black Hawk and Dubuque:** historically Democratic and labor areas where Republicans have made inroads.
- **Pottawattamie (Council Bluffs):** Turek's home turf and a test of his cross-party appeal.
- **Rural Iowa:** Hinson will carry most of it, but the size of her margins could decide the race.

The governor's race, Democrat Rob Sand against Republican Zach Lahn, is a useful comparison. Sand has generally polled better than Turek, which suggests some Iowa voters will back Democrats statewide yet hesitate over changing the Senate's balance.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
