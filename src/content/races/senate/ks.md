Kansas is one of the more surprising Senate battlegrounds of 2026. Republican Sen. Roger Marshall, seeking a second term, faces Democratic pastor Adam Hamilton, a prominent United Methodist leader whose broad appeal has made the race more competitive than Kansas's Republican presidential record suggests.

Kansas is still fundamentally Republican: Trump won it by roughly 16 points in 2024. But it has a meaningful moderate Republican electorate, a Democratic governor, and a history of rejecting ideologically hard-edged conservatives. The central question is whether Hamilton can rebuild the coalition that twice elected Gov. Laura Kelly, with a favorable national environment helping.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adam Hamilton | Democrat | Founding pastor of Church of the Resurrection in suburban Kansas City, one of the country's largest United Methodist congregations; religious moderate known for bridge-building and a pragmatic style |
| Roger Marshall | Republican | Incumbent since 2021; obstetrician-gynecologist and former congressman; strong Trump ally stressing agriculture, border security, conservative social policy and deregulation |
| David Graham | Libertarian | On the ballot; polling around 3–4%, possibly more than the gap between the leaders |

## Why Hamilton is unusual

Hamilton founded Church of the Resurrection in Leawood in 1990 and built it into a major institution of mainline Protestantism, stressing theological moderation, reconciliation and engagement across divides. That helps him in four ways:

- He's hard to caricature as a secular, coastal progressive and can talk about religion, morality and family in a vocabulary conservative Kansans know.
- A large congregation gives him a network of relationships, organizational experience and recognition.
- He can appeal to moderate Republicans who dislike Trump-style politics without identifying with the national Democratic Party.
- His religious identity contrasts with Marshall's career, which is more tied to partisan conservatism.

His pitch isn't mainly to flip evangelical conservatives, but to win a small, decisive share of moderate Republicans, independents and disengaged voters. A late-October Emerson survey had independents backing him 59% to 25%.

## Kansas is not uniformly red

No Democrat has won a U.S. Senate race in Kansas since 1932, a 94-year drought. But Laura Kelly won the governorship in 2018 and again in 2022, beating Derek Schmidt by about two points, and Kansas voters rejected a 2022 constitutional amendment that would have let the legislature restrict abortion. The moderate Republican tradition includes former governors Bill Graves and Mike Hayden, both of whom have endorsed Hamilton. A substantial constituency favors center-right pragmatism and resists the party's more ideological wing.

## Issues and controversies

- **Medical debt.** Investigative reporting found that Marshall's former medical practice sued more than 700 patients over unpaid bills, with 81 arrests associated with the debt proceedings. Marshall defends the need to collect payments and says he treated patients regardless of ability to pay. It's a costly story in an affordability election.
- **Residency.** Hamilton and Gov. Kelly criticize Marshall for keeping a home in Florida as evidence he's disconnected from Kansas. Marshall disputes the characterization.
- **The environment.** Trump's tariffs, the Iran war, farm costs and general dissatisfaction with Washington have hurt Republican incumbents, and Hamilton stresses the effect on Kansas farmers and families.
- **A campaign attack on Hamilton.** Marshall's campaign has raised how Hamilton handled allegations of abuse by a church volunteer at a youth camp in 2005. Hamilton says the initial investigation was inconclusive, that police were informed in 2006, and that he should have contacted authorities sooner. These are campaign allegations, not established findings.

## Why it matters nationally

- **Historic if it flips.** A Hamilton win would be roughly a 19-point swing from Trump's 2024 margin and would end nearly a century of Republican Senate wins in Kansas.
- **One of several seats Democrats need.** It could offset a loss elsewhere or be the deciding seat in a narrow majority.
- **A strategy model.** Hamilton is a culturally moderate, religiously fluent candidate running on economic populism and cross-party cooperation, not national partisan identity.

## What to watch

Kansas polls close at 7 p.m. local time, 8 p.m. Eastern, with some western counties on Mountain Time an hour later. Early votes should appear in many counties' first reports.

Watch Johnson County first, where Hamilton likely needs a big win. Sedgwick County is another bellwether, especially whether Wichita beats Democrats' recent statewide performance. Marshall's margins in rural central and western Kansas will show whether suburban gains are enough. Graham's share is the wildcard: at 3–4% it may exceed the gap between the leaders, and those votes can't be assigned automatically to either party.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
