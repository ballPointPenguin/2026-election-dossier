Kansas is one of the most competitive governor's races of 2026 and one of the few serious Republican pickup chances in a difficult national environment for the GOP. Term-limited Democratic Gov. Laura Kelly is leaving after eight years, and the race pits Republican state Senate President Ty Masterson against Democratic state Sen. Cindy Holscher.

Masterson is the slight favorite, but the race is effectively tied in recent polling. The question is whether Kansans will keep electing moderate Democratic governors while overwhelmingly favoring Republicans for president: Donald Trump carried Kansas by about 16 points in 2024, yet Kelly won the governorship in 2018 and 2022.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ty Masterson | Republican | State Senate president and Trump-endorsed; longtime conservative legislator from the Wichita-area suburbs (Andover), central to the Republican legislative agenda under Kelly; stresses tax cuts, economic growth, school choice, abortion restrictions and conservative social policy; running mate state Sen. | Cindy Holscher | Democrat | State senator representing part of suburban Johnson County, the competitive Kansas City metro; stresses public education, Medicaid expansion, reproductive rights, health-care affordability and the bipartisan governing approach associated with Kelly; running mate former state Rep. KC Ohaebosim |

Masterson's greatest asset is Kansas's underlying Republican electorate; his main vulnerability is his link to the legislature's more confrontational conservative agenda. Holscher's challenge is keeping Kelly's moderate Republican and independent supporters while generating strong Democratic turnout. Both nominees came out of contested August primaries: Holscher took 48.8% in a three-way Democratic primary and Masterson 43.2% in a crowded seven-candidate Republican field.

## Why Kansas elects Democratic governors

Kansas has a long history of ticket splitting. It hasn't voted for a Democratic presidential candidate since Lyndon Johnson in 1964, yet it has repeatedly elected Democratic governors, including John Carlin, Joan Finney, Kathleen Sebelius and Laura Kelly. The state has a pragmatic, relatively moderate Republican tradition, especially around Kansas City and among suburban professionals, educators and business leaders, and the contrast between that tradition and an increasingly conservative Republican legislature creates an opening for Democrats.

One pattern cuts against Holscher, though: Kansas frequently alternates parties in the governor's office, and in recent decades Democratic governors have often been followed by Republicans.

## The Brownback legacy

The backdrop is former Republican Gov. Sam Brownback, who beginning in 2012 enacted sweeping income-tax cuts meant to spur growth. The state instead saw revenue shortfalls, budget trouble and fights over school funding, and in 2017 a bipartisan legislative coalition overrode Brownback's veto to reverse much of the program. Holscher was part of that coalition. She now highlights Masterson's continued defense of Brownback-era tax policy, casting herself as a guard against renewed fiscal instability, while Masterson argues lower taxes and limited government will make Kansas more competitive. Brownback has endorsed Masterson, which gives Holscher a convenient contrast.

## The issues

| Issue | Holscher (D) | Masterson (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes | Property-tax relief and fiscal stability | Further tax cuts and conservative fiscal policy |
| Medicaid | Supports expansion | Opposes conventional expansion |
| Public schools | More funding, including special education | School choice and spending restraint |
| Abortion | Defends reproductive rights | Supports additional restrictions |
| Cannabis | Supports legalization | Opposes recreational legalization |
| Political approach | Bipartisanship and continuity with Kelly | Conservative legislative agenda |

Abortion is especially interesting. In 2022 Kansas voters rejected a constitutional amendment that would have removed explicit protection for abortion rights, about 59% to 41%, showing a big gap between the statewide electorate and the conservative legislature. Holscher hopes to benefit; Masterson must win voters who side with Republicans on taxes and the economy but not on abortion.

## Where the election will be decided

- **Johnson County (Kansas City suburbs).** The key swing region, with a growing, educated suburban electorate that has become increasingly Democratic. Holscher represents part of it and needs a large margin. At an Election Eve rally in Johnson County, Masterson himself said he expected to lose it but hoped to keep the margin manageable, which sums up the Republican strategy.
- **Wichita and Sedgwick County.** Traditionally Republican with meaningful Democratic constituencies; Masterson's nearby Andover roots could help.
- **Lawrence, Topeka and university towns.** Douglas County is a Democratic stronghold, and Topeka and Manhattan offer turnout chances among students and public-sector workers.
- **Rural Kansas.** Overwhelmingly Republican: Holscher must limit losses among moderate rural voters, while Masterson needs strong turnout and big margins.

## The Hamilton Senate race

Kansas is also holding an unusually competitive [Senate race](../../senate/ks/) between Republican Roger Marshall and Democrat Adam Hamilton. The paste's Emerson poll had Hamilton ahead 49–45 while Masterson led Holscher 50–46 in the same survey, so Kansas could elect a Democratic senator while replacing its Democratic governor with a Republican. Hamilton and Holscher appeal to somewhat different constituencies: Hamilton, a prominent Methodist pastor, has drawn attention for his religious identity and moderate image, while Holscher runs more explicitly as a continuation of Kelly's administration. Their results will show whether the Democratic opening is about individual candidates, dissatisfaction with Trump or a broader realignment.

## Why it matters

- **A Democratic defense in a deeply Republican state.** Unlike Iowa, where Democrat Rob Sand is favored to take a Republican-held governorship, Kansas tests whether Kelly's coalition was transferable. A Holscher win would suggest a durable preference for divided government and pragmatic Democratic governors; a Masterson win would suggest Kelly's two wins depended on her personal appeal and her particular opponents.
- **Divided or unified government.** Kansas Republicans are expected to keep the legislature, so a Masterson win would create unified Republican state government, potentially enabling big changes to tax, education and social policy, while a Holscher win would preserve divided government and the Democratic veto.

## Possible results

- **Masterson by 4–7 points:** Republicans recapture the governorship, broadly consistent with the state's partisan lean.
- **Masterson narrowly:** Kansas stays unusually competitive for Democrats in statewide executive races.
- **Holscher wins:** Kelly's coalition survives beyond Kelly despite a strongly Republican electorate.

## What to watch

Kansas polls close at 7 p.m. local time, 8 p.m. Eastern, with the state split across Central and Mountain time. Watch Johnson County's margin, Sedgwick County and the gap between the governor's and Senate results.
