Arizona is one of the most consequential governor's races of the 2026 midterms. Democratic Gov. Katie Hobbs is seeking a second term against Republican Rep. Andy Biggs, a staunch Trump ally and former chair of the House Freedom Caucus. Hobbs enters Election Day a substantial favorite, with a polling lead of roughly six to nine points, remarkable in a state Donald Trump won by about 5.5 points in 2024.

The race is also a contest over Arizona's trajectory: its increasingly competitive suburbs, immigration and border policy, and the administration of the 2028 presidential election.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Katie Hobbs | Democrat | Incumbent governor; former secretary of state; elected in 2022 by a narrow margin over Kari Lake; pragmatic Democrat stressing education, reproductive rights, water security and economic development; frequent vetoes of the Republican legislature's bills; running mate John Giles, former Republican mayor of Mesa |
| Andy Biggs | Republican | U.S. representative and former state senate president; prominent hard-right figure tied to Trump, congressional obstruction of federal spending, stringent immigration enforcement and opposition to abortion rights; won the primary decisively over more establishment rivals; running mate state Sen. Sine Kerr |

Two minor-party tickets are also running, Risa Lombardo and Lisa Castillo for the Green Party and Teri Ann Hourihan and Angela Harrolle Mohan for No Labels, neither with substantial polling support.

## Why Arizona is a battleground

Arizona has realigned over the past decade from a reliably Republican state of Barry Goldwater and John McCain to a highly competitive one:

| Election | Democratic | Republican | Winner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| President 2020 | 49.4% | 49.1% | Biden |
| Governor 2022 | 50.3% | 49.6% | Hobbs |
| Senate 2024 | 50.0% | 47.7% | Gallego |
| President 2024 | 46.7% | 52.2% | Trump |

Trump's 2024 win alongside Ruben Gallego's Senate win shows a large ticket-splitting electorate. Three dynamics matter most.

**1. Maricopa County decides almost everything.** Maricopa, with Phoenix, Mesa, Scottsdale, Chandler, Gilbert and much of the metro area, cast about 61% of Arizona's 2024 presidential vote, and every statewide winner since 2012 has carried it. The Phoenix suburbs' transformation is central to Democratic competitiveness: college-educated suburbanites, independents and some traditionally Republican voters have grown more willing to back moderate Democrats, especially against candidates tied to Trump's movement.

**2. Hobbs courts moderate Republicans.** Picking former Mesa Mayor John Giles as her running mate is arguably her campaign's most symbolic decision. Giles was a Republican mayor of one of Arizona's biggest cities and publicly supported Kamala Harris in 2024, and he represents the old Arizona Republican establishment alienated by Trump-era politics. Hobbs is trying to assemble Democrats, independents and anti-MAGA Republicans. Biggs, by contrast, is an uncompromising conservative who won about 74% in the Republican primary, an ideological profile that may be a handicap statewide.

**3. Latino voters and immigration.** A September Equis Research survey had Hobbs leading Biggs by about 30 points among Latino likely voters, with some 2024 Trump voters crossing over. That's notable because Trump's 2024 win showed Democrats can't assume Latino support. Immigration enforcement, border security, affordability and health care can produce very different coalitions depending on candidates and climate.

## Where they differ

| Issue | Hobbs (D) | Biggs (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immigration | Border security alongside a less confrontational approach to immigration | Aggressive enforcement and alignment with Trump's immigration policies |
| Abortion | Supports reproductive autonomy and Arizona's abortion-rights protections | Strongly anti-abortion; previously sponsored federal restrictions |
| Taxes and economy | Targeted affordability measures and economic development | Broad tax cuts, smaller government |
| Education | Public-school funding and accountability | School choice and education savings accounts |
| Water | Groundwater management and long-term water security | Agricultural interests, property rights and water infrastructure |

## Two consequential details

- **Arizona is electing its first lieutenant governor.** Voters created the office in 2022, and 2026 is the first election with joint governor tickets. Previously the secretary of state was first in the line of succession, so the executive branch's structure changes.
- **The legislature is competitive.** Republicans hold narrow majorities in both chambers, and Democrats hope to win legislative control for the first time since the 1960s. A Hobbs win with legislative gains could substantially change the state's policy direction.

The 2028 election also looms: Arizona was a focal point of disputes over the 2020 result, and the governor's role in election administration and certification gives this race significance beyond ordinary state policy.

## Why it matters

- **Hobbs wins like her polls** would be a substantial Democratic improvement over 2024 and another sign that Arizona stays competitive even when its presidential vote leans Republican.
- **A test of the anti-MAGA suburban coalition:** whether it extends beyond individual candidates to reshape the state's institutions.
- **House races on the same ballot:** [AZ-1](../../house/az-01/), [AZ-2](../../house/az-02/) and [AZ-6](../../house/az-06/).

## What to watch

Arizona polls close at 7 p.m. local time, 9 p.m. Eastern, and early results should begin arriving around 8 p.m. local. Early ballots may favor Hobbs strongly while Election Day votes favor Biggs, and late-arriving ballots dropped off on Election Day take extra time to process, so a narrow result could take days.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
