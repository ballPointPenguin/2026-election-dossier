Georgia has one of the closest governor's races in the country, with Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms and Republican Rick Jackson essentially tied heading into Election Day. Republican Gov. Brian Kemp is term-limited after eight years, which gives Democrats a real chance at a governorship they haven't held since 2003.

It's a revealing test of Georgia's realignment. Donald Trump carried the state by 2.2 points in 2024, but Democrats have shown they can win statewide through high turnout in metro Atlanta, growing suburban support and strong Black participation. Unlike some other competitive governor's races, Georgia has no obvious frontrunner: a Democratic victory is entirely plausible, and so is another narrow Republican win.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keisha Lance Bottoms | Democrat | Atlanta mayor 2018–2022 through the pandemic, policing protests and public-safety controversies; former Biden administration adviser; stresses Medicaid expansion, a higher minimum wage, public education, affordability and a change of direction after two decades of Republican governors; would be Georgia's first woman governor and, per the source, the first Black woman elected governor in American history |
| Rick Jackson | Republican | Billionaire founder of Jackson Healthcare, a major healthcare staffing company based in Georgia; political newcomer and business-minded outsider who stresses his childhood in foster care, economic opportunity, public safety, tax cuts and government efficiency; proposes phasing out the state income tax and a private-insurance alternative to conventional Medicaid expansion; has put more than $200 million of his own money into the campaign |

Bottoms won the Democratic primary outright in May. Jackson beat Lt. Gov. Burt Jones in a June Republican runoff after a primary dominated by Jackson's huge spending.

## Geography

Georgia is a state where demographic change has made statewide races competitive without yet overturning Republican control of state government.

- **Metro Atlanta.** Fulton and DeKalb are Democratic strongholds, and Cobb and Gwinnett have realigned. Bottoms needs enormous urban-core turnout and strong margins in the diversifying suburbs.
- **Secondary cities.** Savannah, Augusta, Macon, Columbus and Athens contain Democratic constituencies surrounded by heavily Republican areas, and their turnout and margins could be decisive in a close race.
- **Rural Georgia and the exurbs.** Jackson needs commanding Republican margins across North Georgia, the outer Atlanta suburbs and much of rural territory. Black-majority counties in southwestern Georgia are important Democratic exceptions.

The transformation comes from metro Atlanta's growth, rising racial and ethnic diversity and the realignment of college-educated suburban voters. The countervailing force is that Republicans have stayed remarkably competitive with moderate and suburban voters in state elections, especially under Kemp, who won reelection in 2022 by roughly 7.5 points while Democrat Raphael Warnock won another Senate term. Candidate quality and political identity matter a lot here.

## Where they differ

| Issue | Bottoms (D) | Jackson (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State income tax | Targeted reductions, including relief for teachers | Halve the income tax in his first term, then phase it out |
| Health care | Full Medicaid expansion | Private-insurance-oriented expansion with work requirements |
| Abortion | Expand reproductive rights | Supports Georgia's six-week restriction |
| Housing | Affordable-housing grants and loans, limits on abusive landlords | Property-tax restraint and economic growth |
| Education | More public-school funding and educator pay | Efficiency, parental choice and teacher pay |
| Data centers | Pause or restrict new development | Require local community approval |

Jackson's tax plan is especially consequential: the income tax supplies roughly half of Georgia's state budget, so big cuts would require matching spending cuts, replacement revenue or unusually strong growth. Data centers are another distinctive issue, since Georgia has become a hyperscale hub and electricity costs, water use, land and local control are salient.

## The Trump–Kemp alliance

Trump and Kemp were bitterly divided after 2020, when Kemp refused to overturn Georgia's presidential result, yet both now back Jackson, despite having originally supported his primary opponent, Burt Jones. Jackson presents himself as both a Trump ally and Kemp's business-oriented successor.

Bottoms is benefiting from the national Democratic apparatus: Barack Obama and Kamala Harris have campaigned for her, and Sen. Jon Ossoff's well-funded reelection effort gives Democrats organizational advantages across the ballot.

## The Ossoff factor

The [Senate race](../../senate/ga/) is also highly competitive, with Democrat Jon Ossoff facing Republican Rep. Mike Collins. Recent polling has Ossoff up about seven points, much better than Bottoms's near-tie with Jackson. That suggests some Georgians will back Ossoff for Senate and Jackson for governor, so the question for Bottoms is whether the turnout machinery built around Ossoff can deliver enough extra Democratic votes to beat Jackson's edge among ticket-splitters.

## The runoff rule

Georgia requires statewide winners to get more than 50%. If neither major candidate does, the top two go to a December 1 runoff. The Libertarian Party lost statewide ballot access, so Chase Oliver isn't on the governor's ballot, but write-ins and other circumstances could still make the majority threshold relevant.

## Why it matters

- **If Bottoms wins:** Democrats end a 24-year Republican hold on the governorship, Georgia elects its first woman governor, and Sun Belt Democrats show they can contest executive power, not just Senate seats.
- **If Jackson wins narrowly:** Georgia remains a battleground where Republicans keep a modest edge in state elections even in an unfavorable national environment.
- **If a runoff is needed:** Georgia stays in the national spotlight through December, with turnout operations and the Senate race shaping the outcome.
- **2028:** Georgia will likely be a pivotal presidential battleground, and the governor has roles in election administration, appointments and certification, though the secretary of state remains the principal election administrator.

## What to watch

Georgia polls close at 7 p.m. Eastern. Early-vote results may initially favor Democrats, with Election Day votes potentially moving toward Republicans. Watch metro Atlanta turnout, the secondary cities, the margin between Bottoms and Ossoff, and whether anyone crosses 50%.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
