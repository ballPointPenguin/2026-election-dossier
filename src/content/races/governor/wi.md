Wisconsin is one of the most consequential governor's battlegrounds of 2026, though the race has developed a clearer Democratic edge than many expected. With two-term Democratic Gov. Tony Evers retiring, Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley faces Republican U.S. Rep. Tom Tiffany. Crowley enters Election Day ahead by roughly four to six points in polling averages, and the final Marquette University Law School poll has him up 51% to 44% among likely voters.

That's notable because Wisconsin was the closest state in the 2024 presidential election, which Donald Trump carried by about 0.9 points. A Crowley win would keep the governorship Democratic, potentially make him Wisconsin's first Black governor, and put him in charge of a major presidential battleground in 2028.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| David Crowley | Democrat | Milwaukee County executive since 2020, 40; former state assemblyman; stresses fiscal management, public services, housing, health care and regional economic development; campaign focuses on affordability, public-school funding, child care and health-care access; narrowly won a crowded primary over progressive state Rep. Francesca Hong by roughly 4,000 votes, with Evers's endorsement; running mate former state Treasurer Sarah Godlewski |
| Tom Tiffany | Republican | U.S. representative for the heavily Republican 7th District in northern Wisconsin since 2020, 68; former state senator who served in both legislative chambers; strong Trump ally who advocates tax cuts, a property-tax freeze, deregulation and more conservative approaches to public safety and education; attacks Crowley's Milwaukee County record and tries to make the race a referendum on Democratic governance, especially crime, taxes and data centers; running mate David Varnam |

The ballot has these two major-party tickets plus registered write-in candidates.

## The Democratic primary

Crowley's nomination was far from inevitable. Wisconsin Democrats started with a large field including former Lt. Gov. Mandela Barnes, Lt. Gov. Sara Rodriguez and progressive legislators, and Crowley withdrew briefly before returning with Evers's encouragement. In August he narrowly beat Francesca Hong, a democratic socialist from Madison, by less than half a percentage point. The result exposed a real ideological split between a pragmatic, establishment-aligned candidate and a more explicitly progressive one, and his ability to consolidate that coalition while attracting independents has been a central general-election development.

## Wisconsin's history: the ultimate battleground

Wisconsin is synonymous with razor-thin elections: five of its last seven presidential elections were decided by under a point, and both of Evers's wins were fairly narrow. The Milwaukee and Madison metros provide big Democratic margins while rural Wisconsin is strongly Republican, so results often hinge on turnout, suburban voting and small shifts. A five-point Democratic governor's win would be relatively large by recent Wisconsin standards.

## The Scott Walker legacy

Recent Wisconsin politics is inseparable from former Republican Gov. Scott Walker, who after winning in 2010 enacted Act 10, sharply restricting collective bargaining for most public employees. The protests, recall election and polarization that followed made Wisconsin a national laboratory for conservative state governance. Evers beat Walker in 2018 but spent much of his tenure facing Republican legislative majorities. 2026 could be a turning point in that long fight over the state's institutions.

## The central issues

| Issue | Crowley (D) | Tiffany (R) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes | Targeted affordability measures and investment in services | Income-tax cuts and a property-tax freeze |
| Health care | Expand Medicaid access and a BadgerCare public option | Market-oriented reforms and spending restraint |
| Child care | Cap eligible families' child-care costs at 7% of income | Stresses tax relief and economic growth |
| Education | Increase public-school funding | School accountability and conservative education priorities |
| Public-sector unions | Supports overturning Act 10 | Voted for Act 10 as a legislator and still defends it |
| Abortion | Supports constitutional protection of abortion rights | Opposes abortion rights |
| Data centers | Supports a construction moratorium | Criticizes subsidies and local impacts but opposes a general moratorium |

**The $3 billion surplus.** Wisconsin enters the election with a state budget surplus of roughly $3 billion. Tiffany wants much of it returned through lower income and property taxes; Crowley wants it used for public services, health-care access and lower costs for working families. Unlike states facing shortfalls, Wisconsin has room to choose, though a surplus isn't recurring revenue, so permanent tax cuts or spending commitments would have to be tested against future projections.

**Act 10.** Crowley supports reversing the collective-bargaining limits; Tiffany voted for Act 10 and defends it. The issue matters for teachers, public employees, organized labor and local budgets, and symbolizes the contest between Wisconsin's progressive labor tradition and the conservative movement that rose under Walker.

**Data centers.** As in Texas, Oregon, Nevada and elsewhere, data-center development is a flashpoint. Tiffany portrays Crowley as too supportive of large AI data-center projects, citing his earlier enthusiasm for making Wisconsin a national AI-infrastructure hub, and Crowley endorsed a construction moratorium in October. Tiffany has criticized the costs and consequences but hasn't backed a comparable statewide moratorium. A September Marquette poll found 74% of respondents said the costs of large data centers outweigh the benefits and only 25% said benefits outweigh costs, a cross-partisan consensus on scrutiny of electricity use, water, subsidies and infrastructure costs.

## The geography of victory

- **Milwaukee County.** Crowley's home base and a major Democratic vote reservoir; strong turnout among Black, younger and urban voters is essential. His county-executive experience helps, though Tiffany attacks his record on crime and county services.
- **Madison and Dane County.** Wisconsin's strongest large Democratic county, with an exceptionally active progressive electorate: Crowley needs huge margins and enthusiasm from Hong's former primary supporters.
- **The Milwaukee suburbs.** The traditionally Republican WOW counties (Waukesha, Ozaukee and Washington) remain crucial: Democrats have made gains among college-educated suburban voters, but Republicans still count on big margins.
- **Northern and western Wisconsin.** Tiffany's congressional base is the heavily Republican north, while the smaller industrial cities, rural counties and Mississippi River communities of the west have shifted toward Republicans but sometimes back Democrats statewide.

## Why Tiffany is struggling

His most obvious liability is his close tie to Trump: he supported efforts to challenge certification of Biden's 2020 victory, including voting against Electoral College votes from Arizona and Pennsylvania, and Crowley makes it a central theme. Trump's approval in Wisconsin was just 37% among registered voters in the final Marquette survey. Crowley also leads among independents, 56% to 26% in the final Marquette poll, a 30-point edge that may be the most encouraging Democratic statistic. Tiffany has substantial financial backing, has benefited from several wealthy donors, has significantly outspent Crowley, and keeps the loyalty of most Republican voters.

## The legislature: a possible Democratic trifecta

Democrats are trying to win both legislative chambers, made more plausible by the more competitive legislative maps adopted in 2024; Republican-friendly maps had let the GOP keep commanding majorities even in very close statewide elections. If Crowley wins and Democrats take both chambers, Wisconsin would have unified Democratic government for the first time since 2010, opening the door to major legislation on labor rights, Medicaid expansion, school funding, reproductive rights and election administration. A trifecta isn't guaranteed: Republicans keep advantages in a number of districts, and the Senate's staggered elections complicate a quick flip. Even without one, Crowley would preserve a Democratic veto against a Republican legislature.

## Why it matters

- **A 2028 battleground.** The governor has substantial authority over agencies, appointments and election-related legislation, though election administration is shared among the Wisconsin Elections Commission and local officials. A Crowley win preserves a Democratic check on Republican proposals about voting procedures; a Tiffany win would give Republicans the governorship for the first time since 2019 and could shift the institutional balance before the presidential election. The simultaneous attorney general race between Democrat Josh Kaul and Republican Eric Toney matters too, given the office's role in election-related litigation.
- **A larger shift.** If Democrats also make big legislative gains, 2026 could mark a decisive change in Wisconsin's political balance after more than fifteen years of conflict over redistricting, labor rights and divided government.
- **House races on the same ballot:** [WI-1](../../house/wi-01/) and [WI-3](../../house/wi-03/). There is no Wisconsin Senate race this year.

Other recent polls also favor Crowley: a late-October Wick poll (Republican-sponsored) had him up about three points and a November 1 Research Co. poll showed a lead of five to six points. FLIPR also lists a YouGov poll (Sep. 22–Oct. 9, 1,715 registered voters) at Crowley 46, Tiffany 42. Recent polling averages put his lead at roughly 4.5 to 5.5 points depending on methodology. What matters is that Crowley has led consistently, not on one favorable survey.

## Possible results

- **Crowley by 5–8 points:** a strong Democratic performance, well above Wisconsin's typical statewide margins, suggesting a pronounced anti-Republican midterm environment.
- **Crowley narrowly:** Democrats defend the governorship, but Wisconsin stays structurally competitive.
- **Tiffany wins:** a notable Republican upset against the polling consensus, showing the conservative coalition's resilience despite Trump's unpopularity.

## What to watch

Wisconsin's polls close at 8 p.m. Central, 9 p.m. Eastern. The state had recorded more than 693,000 early and absentee votes as of the Friday before Election Day, ahead of the same point in 2022, though that alone doesn't show which party benefits. Watch Milwaukee and Dane counties, the WOW suburbs and the margin among independents.

Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
