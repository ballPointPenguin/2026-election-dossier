Iowa is shaping up as one of the most surprising Democratic pickup chances of the 2026 midterms. Democratic State Auditor Rob Sand faces Republican farmer and political newcomer Zach Lahn for the governorship Republican Kim Reynolds is leaving. Sand enters Election Day up roughly seven points, though Iowa voted for Donald Trump by more than 13 in 2024, and a margin anything like that would be a remarkable swing in a state Democrats have struggled to contest for a decade.

It's also interesting because both candidates run as reform-minded outsiders to the state's political establishment, from very different ideological directions.

| Candidate | Party | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rob Sand | Democrat | State auditor, former prosecutor, 44; Iowa's only Democratic statewide elected official, first elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022; identity built on accountability, anticorruption, fiscal oversight and independence from party establishments; from Decorah in northeastern Iowa and stresses his rural roots; moderate who aims to appeal to independents and some Republicans; running mate Dave Muhlbauer, a farmer and former county supervisor |
| Zach Lahn | Republican | Farmer from Belle Plaine, 40, and conservative activist; grassroots conservatism, agricultural populism, skepticism of large corporations and the Make America Healthy Again movement; backs substantial tax cuts, tougher scrutiny of agricultural chemicals and measures against corporate concentration in agriculture; running mate state Rep. Derek Wulf |

Lahn's nomination was a major upset. Rep. Randy Feenstra, the presumed frontrunner, had Trump's endorsement shortly before the primary, but Lahn won with grassroots conservative support. Trump then endorsed Lahn and campaigned for him in October.

## Iowa's transformation

Iowa was once a quintessential Midwestern swing state, politically closer to Wisconsin or even Minnesota than to today's solidly Republican Iowa. Barack Obama won it twice, by nearly ten points in 2008, and Republicans have won it decisively in three straight presidential elections since 2016. Several factors explain the shift:

- A relatively large rural and small-town electorate has moved sharply Republican.
- A comparatively modest metropolitan population gives Democrats fewer big-city strongholds than in Wisconsin or Minnesota.
- White working-class voters, once central to the Democratic coalition, have moved substantially toward Republicans.
- Republican control of state government has become more entrenched, with large legislative majorities and a growing registration advantage.

Iowa nonetheless has a tradition of moderate, independent-minded Democrats, including former Govs. Tom Vilsack and Chet Culver and former Sen. Tom Harkin. Sand's campaign is trying to revive it.

## Where Sand must win

- **Des Moines and its suburbs.** Polk County is Iowa's largest Democratic vote reservoir. Sand needs strong turnout in Des Moines and improved performance among independents in the growing suburbs.
- **Iowa City and Ames.** University communities in Johnson and Story counties are reliable Democratic constituencies, where big margins and strong youth turnout matter.
- **Northeastern Iowa and small towns.** Perhaps the best test of his appeal: his Decorah roots and rural identity may let him outperform national Democrats in communities that have moved decisively Republican.

The central question is whether Sand can rebuild enough of Iowa's old Democratic coalition without reversing its whole Republican realignment.

## Cancer and water quality

One of the campaign's most distinctive issues is Iowa's unusually high cancer incidence. Agricultural runoff, nitrates in drinking water, pesticide exposure, radon and other hazards have become political concerns. Lahn has made cancer prevention and clean water central, with support from the Make America Healthy Again movement, stressing reduced chemical exposures, better agricultural practices and challenging corporate influence. Sand also backs conservation and water quality but puts more weight on public-health infrastructure, preventive screening, Medicaid access and established environmental interventions.

There is real scientific concern about some agricultural exposures and nitrate contamination, but Iowa's elevated cancer rate can't be pinned on one established cause: age structure, screening, smoking, alcohol, radon and other factors complicate the picture.

## What a Sand victory would mean

Republicans hold the governorship and both legislative chambers and are likely to keep their legislative majorities, so a Sand win would mean divided government, not a Democratic trifecta. He could veto, make executive appointments, influence agency policy and negotiate budgets, but big statutory changes would need Republican cooperation. He would also end 16 years of Republican governors and be Iowa's first Democratic governor since Chet Culver left in 2011. Nationally he could become a model for Democrats competing in Republican-leaning rural states: culturally moderate, fiscally attentive, locally rooted and skeptical of partisan orthodoxy.

## The Senate benchmark

Iowa also has a competitive open [Senate race](../../senate/ia/) between Democrat Josh Turek and Republican Ashley Hinson. Marist's October likely-voter poll had the governor's race at Sand 52%, Lahn 45%, and the Senate race at Turek 48%, Hinson 45%. Sand's stronger showing suggests his appeal goes beyond a generic Democratic advantage. If Sand wins while Hinson wins the Senate race, it would be a striking case of candidate-specific ticket splitting; if both Democrats win, it suggests a much broader resurgence. The House races on the same ballot are [IA-1](../../house/ia-01/), [IA-2](../../house/ia-02/) and [IA-3](../../house/ia-03/).

## Possible results

- **Sand by 5–8 points:** a remarkable Democratic overperformance in a Trump +13 state, validating his crossover strategy.
- **Sand narrowly:** Iowa remains strongly Republican for president but keeps a real constituency for moderate statewide Democrats.
- **Lahn wins:** Iowa's conservative realignment is strong enough to overcome Sand's polling edge.

Sand is the clear favorite on the polling, but Iowa's recent Republican record makes a Democratic win notable, not inevitable. It's one of the strongest Democratic governor pickup chances of 2026, alongside Georgia and Alaska, though the mechanism differs: Sand isn't just riding a national wave but has a distinctive brand that attracts voters who've left the party.

## What to watch

Iowa's polls close at 8 p.m. Central, 9 p.m. Eastern. Watch Polk, Johnson and Story counties for Democratic margins, northeastern Iowa for crossover strength, and the gap between Sand and Turek.
